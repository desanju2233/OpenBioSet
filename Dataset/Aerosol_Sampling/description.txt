Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Digital Microfluidic Lab-on-a-Chip Integrated Aerosol Particle Sample Collection, Pre-Concentration, and Colorimetric Analysis (Quasi-Real-Time Cascade-Impactor-Coupled Sampling)

REAGENTS USED:
  - Aerosol Impaction Surface (collection surface of the electrowetting chip onto which airborne particles are impacted directly, representing deposited aerosol constituents such as sulfate, nitrate, and ammonium)
  - Ultra-Pure Solvent (micro-droplet digitally directed across the impaction surface to wash and dissolve aerosol constituents)
  - Colorimetric Reagent (reagent that reacts with the extracted aerosol constituents to produce colored complexes for LED/photodiode absorbance detection)

STEPS:
1. Particle collection: Impact airborne aerosol particles directly onto the exposed collection surface of the electrowetting chip during a brief collection phase, with the air stream uninterrupted.
2. Extraction/washing: Interrupt the air stream, dispense a micro-droplet of ultra-pure solvent, and digitally scan/actuate it across the impaction surface (approx. 1 min sampling time) to dissolve and pre-concentrate the aerosol constituents into the droplet.
3. Reagent merge and mix: Dispense a colorimetric reagent droplet, merge it with the extraction droplet by applying voltages to the appropriate electrodes, and mix by shuttling the merged droplet across electrodes for approximately 5 seconds at an actuation voltage of about 50 V to form a colored reaction product.
4. Detection: Measure the absorbance of the colored complex at the target wavelength at room temperature using the on-chip LED (light emitting diode)/photodiode setup.
5. Disposal and cycle repeat: Dispose of the analyzed droplet; repeat the collection-extraction-mix-detect cycle so that sampling can continue in quasi-real time.

INTERPRETATION: Successful integrated sample collection and analysis is confirmed by achieving a time resolution of 1 minute per measurement (roughly two orders of magnitude faster than conventional manual cascade impactor methods), with the very small extraction-droplet volume producing a relatively concentrated solution that allows detection limits in air on the order of tens of ng/m3 (attogram-level analyte mass), supporting quasi real-time, fully automated on-chip quantification of major inorganic aerosol constituents (sulfate, nitrate, ammonium) as well as future extension to airborne bioagent detection.
