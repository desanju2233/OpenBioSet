Source link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9013781/
Paper DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgac021

Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: bCARMEN (Bacterial Combinatorial Arrayed Reactions for Multiplexed Evaluation of Nucleic acids) - Droplet Microarray CRISPR-Cas13 Detection of Bacterial Species and Antibiotic Resistance Genes

REAGENTS USED:
  - Extracted Bacterial gDNA or Synthetic Target (input nucleic acid, diluted to ~10^3 genome equivalents/copies per uL)
  - PCR Master Mix (Q5 Hot Start polymerase-based, for one-pot multiplexed preamplification)
  - Primer Pool (pooled species-specific or gene-specific primer pairs, total final concentration 3.5 uM)
  - Cas13a Protein (purified Leptotrichia wadei Cas13a, 45 nM final)
  - crRNA Guide (in-vitro transcribed, species- or resistance-gene-specific, 22.5 nM final)
  - Quenched Fluorescent RNA Reporter (RNase Alert v2, 500 nM final)
  - Detection/Nuclease Assay Buffer (40 mM Tris-HCl, 60 mM NaCl, pH 7.3, with 1 mM each ATP/GTP/UTP/CTP, murine RNase inhibitor, and T7 polymerase mix)
  - Fluorescent Barcode Dyes (distinct dye sets for sample droplets and detection-set droplets, 2 uM final)

STEPS:
1. Sample processing and amplification: Perform one-pot, multiplexed PCR preamplification of the target locus (e.g., the topA gene for species identification, or resistance-gene panels) using Q5 Hot Start polymerase with the pooled primer set; run 30 cycles with a 65C annealing temperature.
2. Detection set assembly: Combine purified Cas13a protein, a species/gene-specific crRNA guide, the quenched fluorescent RNA reporter, and nuclease assay buffer (with NTPs, RNase inhibitor, and T7 polymerase mix) to form each detection set.
3. Barcoding: Dilute the amplified target sample and add a distinct fluorescent dye barcode; similarly add a distinct fluorescent dye barcode to each detection set, so that droplet identity (which target or which crRNA guide) can be recovered from color after imaging.
4. Emulsification and pooling: Emulsify each barcoded sample and detection mix into nanoliter droplets; pool equal volumes of all sample droplets and all detection droplets together.
5. Microarray loading: Load the pooled droplet mixture onto a DropArray microfluidic chip containing over 150,000 microwells (each sized to hold two droplets), allowing stochastic pairwise loading so that every target-guide combination is represented across the array.
6. Droplet merging and incubation: Merge the paired droplets in each microwell (e.g., via corona-treater-induced coalescence) to initiate the Cas13 collateral-cleavage detection reaction, then incubate the sealed chip at 37C (imaged at 0, 1, and 3 hour time points).
7. Imaging and readout: Image the microarray using fluorescence microscopy (or, in the droplet-free CARMEN v2 variant, a conventional cell-phone camera under UV illumination) to determine which target-guide well pairs show a positive collateral-cleavage fluorescence signal.
8. Data analysis: Map the premerge barcode identity of each well to its postmerge fluorescence signal to generate a target-by-guide heatmap, calling a well positive when signal exceeds 6 standard deviations above background.

INTERPRETATION: Successful bCARMEN detection is confirmed when the correct target-guide crRNA pair produces a fluorescence signal greater than 6 SD above background with minimal cross-reactivity to non-target species/genes; the paper reports 51/52 (98%) species-specific crRNAs meeting this threshold across a 52-species bacterial panel and 100% (27/27) accuracy for a 14-resistance-gene panel tested on clinical isolates, with a droplet-free CARMEN v2 variant enabling cell-phone-camera readout within approximately 3 hours.
