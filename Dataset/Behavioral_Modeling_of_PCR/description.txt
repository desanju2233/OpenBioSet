Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Droplet-Based PCR Thermal Cycling on a Microelectrofluidic System (MEFS)

REAGENTS USED:
  - DNA Sample (target genomic DNA solution containing the region to be amplified)
  - PCR Reagent (primer pair, all four deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs), and a heat-stable Taq DNA polymerase)

STEPS:
1. Dispense a DNA sample droplet and a PCR reagent droplet (primers, dNTPs, Taq polymerase).
2. Merge the sample and reagent droplets to form the PCR master-mix droplet.
3. Thermocycle the droplet through the three-step PCR thermal profile, repeated for multiple amplification cycles:
   a. Strand separation: heat to 95C for 45 seconds to separate the parent DNA duplex strands.
   b. Hybridization of primers: cool to 54C for 30 seconds to allow each primer to anneal to its complementary strand.
   c. DNA synthesis: heat to 72C for 90 seconds, the optimal temperature for Taq DNA polymerase, to extend and synthesize new DNA strands from each primer.
4. After thermal cycling completes, detect the fluorescence signal from the amplified DNA droplet.
5. Discard the analyzed droplet.

INTERPRETATION: The paper models this three-step PCR thermal-cycle chemistry (strand separation, primer hybridization, DNA synthesis) using SystemC for both a reconfigurable continuous-flow PCR system and a droplet-based PCR system built on electrowetting actuation. Both implementations use the same underlying PCR chemistry and thermal profile; the droplet-based system is shown via simulation to achieve higher system throughput (10.48 hrs vs. 19.31 hrs to process 100 DNA solutions), better system-correction capability (61.5% correction of unqualified samples vs. 0% for continuous-flow), and lower design/integration complexity (several mm^2 of flow-control area vs. ~700 mm^2 for the mechanical continuous-flow system), demonstrating the droplet-based approach as the superior implementation for this PCR chemistry.
