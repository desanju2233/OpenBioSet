Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Rapid Mixing Protocol on Digital Microfluidics via Reciprocating Droplet Motions - methodological protocol for high-efficiency droplet mixing using contact charge electrophoresis (CCEP) to overcome diffusion limitations during automated chemical reactions.

REAGENTS/MATERIALS USED:
  - Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) Aqueous Solution (0.4 wt%, 0.2 +/- 0.05 uL droplets, density 1.01 g/cm^3)
  - Phenolphthalein (PHP) Indicator Solution (ethanol:distilled water:PHP = 79:20:1 wt%, 0.2 +/- 0.05 uL droplets, density 0.78 g/cm^3; colorless, turns pink on reaction with base)
  - Silicone Oil (KF-96L, dynamic viscosity 28.6 mPa*s, density 0.95 g/cm^3), used as the dielectric suspending medium filling the chamber
  - Blue Dye Droplet and Yellow Dye Droplet (alternative colorant pair used to visually confirm mixing, merging to green when fully mixed)

STEPS:
1. Platform setup: Assemble a 3D digital microfluidic (DMF) platform with two parallel pin-header-socket electrode modules (2 mm pitch), a lower electrode module mounted in a U-shaped 3D-printed chamber with transparent acrylic walls, and an upper electrode module mounted on a translation stage (vertical spacing adjustable 2-4 mm). Fill the chamber with silicone oil as the dielectric suspending medium.
2. Horizontal droplet charging and transport: Dispense a droplet of NaOH solution so it contacts a ground electrode (becoming negatively charged) and a droplet of PHP indicator solution so it contacts a positively activated electrode (becoming positively charged); sequentially actuate neighboring electrodes to transport both droplets horizontally toward each other.
3. Electrocoalescence (horizontal case): When the oppositely charged NaOH and PHP droplets meet, they merge electrically via electrostatic interaction into a single droplet.
4. Horizontal reciprocating mixing: Actuate the merged droplet to shake horizontally back and forth between the positive and ground electrodes (CCEP actuation at 200 V, 300 V, or 400 V, 2 mm electrode pitch) until the PHP colorless-to-pink color change indicates mixing is complete, typically within about one minute (as fast as ~2 s to reach a 0.7-0.8 mixing index).
5. Horizontal/vertical droplet charging and transport (3D case): Dispense NaOH and PHP droplets so both contact positive electrodes (both positively charged); sequentially actuate neighboring electrodes to transport the droplets horizontally toward each other, then apply a ground signal to the upper electrode to transport them vertically.
6. Electrocoalescence (vertical case): As the droplets reciprocate vertically between upper and lower electrodes, they acquire opposite charges (due to differing reciprocation speeds from their density difference) and merge electrically when they touch.
7. Vertical reciprocating mixing: Actuate the merged droplet to shake vertically between the lower and upper electrodes (CCEP actuation at 400 V, electrode distance 2, 3, or 4 mm) until the PHP color change confirms complete mixing; the fastest full mixing (~2.4 s, mixing index 0.95) is achieved at 4 mm electrode distance and 400 V.
8. Mixing-index quantification: Record droplet motion/mixing at 120 fps (digital camera with macro lens) and internal flow patterns at 250-1000 fps (high-speed camera); crop and convert images to grayscale, identify the region of interest by edge detection, correct pixel intensity (grayscale minus green channel), and compute the standard deviation of corrected intensity per frame to calculate the temporal mixing index M(%) = (1 - (sigma - sigma_final)/sigma_final) x 100.
9. Repeat steps 2-4 (horizontal) or 5-7 (vertical) with a blue-dye/yellow-dye droplet pair in place of NaOH/PHP to visually confirm mixing (blue + yellow merging to green) and validate the CCEP-driven reciprocating-motion mixing procedure for general reagent pairs.
