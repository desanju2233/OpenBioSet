Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Portable Droplet Microfluidic Device for Cortisol Measurement Using a Competitive Heterogeneous (Magnetic Bead) ELISA

REAGENTS USED:
  - Cortisol Sample (free bioactive cortisol in biological fluid, e.g. saliva/blood, aspirated via cannula, 350 nL total sample draw per measurement cycle)
  - Anti-cortisol Antibody-Coated Magnetic Beads (Tosylactivated Dynabeads MyOne coated with anti-cortisol-3-BSA antibody, suspended 50:50 in glycerol:PBS to reduce sedimentation)
  - Cortisol-HRP Conjugate (horseradish-peroxidase-labelled cortisol competitor, held at constant concentration during sample analysis)
  - PBS Wash Buffer (two washing droplets per droplet train, used to remove unbound analyte from the magnetic bead pellet)
  - TMB Substrate (colorimetric indicator droplet; catalysed by HRP from its native diamine form to a blue-green oxidised product, with absorbance inversely proportional to cortisol concentration)

STEPS:
1. Droplet train generation: A 12-line peristaltic micropump co-flows the sample, antibody-coated magnetic bead suspension, and cortisol-HRP conjugate, segmenting them by oil at a T-junction into a single ~1060 nL "sample droplet" as the first droplet of a four-droplet train.
2. Two PBS washing droplets (~550 nL each) and one TMB indicator droplet (~550 nL) are generated by the pump immediately after the sample droplet, completing the four-droplet train.
3. Competitive binding incubation: The droplet train flows through PTFE tubing at room temperature for 8 minutes, allowing sample cortisol and cortisol-HRP to competitively bind the antibody-coated magnetic beads.
4. Magnetic bead capture: As the sample droplet reaches the automated magnetic trap, electromagnetic tweezers (activated via an inlet light-gate) capture and pellet the magnetic beads, extracting them from the sample droplet.
5. Washing: The captured bead pellet is held at the focus of the magnetic field while the two following PBS wash droplets pass through the trap, removing unbound cortisol and cortisol-HRP.
6. Re-dispersion and colour development: When the TMB indicator droplet reaches the trap (detected by an outlet light-gate), the beads are released and re-dispersed into it; HRP on the beads catalyses the TMB colour change over a further 2 minutes of reaction time.
7. Detection: The indicator droplet passes through an inline spectrophotometer (456 nm LED / photodetector flow cell), and the absorbance (inversely proportional to sample cortisol concentration) is measured and logged against a pre-determined calibration curve.
8. Disposal: All droplets and carrier oil flow to a waste sachet after detection.
9. Repeat cycle: The pump autonomously repeats the whole four-droplet train generation, incubation, washing, colour development and detection cycle approximately every 10 seconds, enabling continuous/high-frequency cortisol monitoring; each calibration concentration was measured over 10 repeat droplet trains.

INTERPRETATION: Successful cortisol quantification is confirmed by a calibration curve of (log) absorbance vs. cortisol concentration across the analytical range 3.175-100 ng mL-1, with absorbance inversely proportional to cortisol concentration as expected for a competitive assay, a sample-to-signal time of about 10 minutes, and relative standard deviation (<1%) substantially lower than the equivalent 96-well bulk plate assay, supporting use of the device as a point-of-care platform for continuous or high-frequency biomarker monitoring.
