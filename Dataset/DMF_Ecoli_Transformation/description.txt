Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Digital Microfluidic (DMF) Chemically Competent E. coli Transformation, Heat-Shock, Outgrowth, and Fluorescent Reporter Expression

REAGENTS USED:
  - Chemically Competent E. coli Cells (DH5alpha, JBEI-4930, prepared by CaCl2 method, ~1x10^10 cells/mL)
  - CaCl2 Buffer (0.1 M CaCl2 in water, first resuspension/wash solution)
  - CaCl2/Glycerol Buffer (0.1 M CaCl2 with 15% glycerol, final competent-cell storage buffer)
  - Plasmid DNA (fluorescent reporter plasmid, e.g., pBAD-mTagBFP2, pRSET-EmGFP, pDsRed_T3-S4T, or kanamycin-resistant pBbB2k-GFP)
  - LB Broth (Luria-Bertani outgrowth/recovery medium)
  - Kanamycin Selection Agar (LB agar with kanamycin, for selection of transformants carrying the reporter plasmid)

STEPS:
1. Cell culture: grow an E. coli colony in LB broth at 37 C, 200 rpm, until early log phase (OD600 = 0.3).
2. Chill the cell suspension on ice for 15 min, then pellet by centrifugation.
3. First CaCl2 exchange: replace the medium with 0.1 M CaCl2 and resuspend the pellet; incubate on ice for 30 min.
4. Pellet the suspension again by centrifugation and exchange the supernatant for 0.1 M CaCl2 containing 15% glycerol to yield chemically competent cells at a final concentration of ~1x10^10 cells/mL; aliquot and store at -80 C until use.
5. On the digital microfluidic device, combine an aliquot of competent cells with a droplet of plasmid DNA (fluorescent reporter plasmid) and pre-incubate on ice.
6. Apply a heat-shock pulse to the merged droplet on-chip to drive plasmid uptake.
7. Add LB broth to the heat-shocked droplet and incubate at 37 C for outgrowth/recovery.
8. Dispense the recovered culture onto kanamycin selection agar and incubate at 37 C to allow colony growth and reporter protein expression.
9. Detection: assess successful transformation and expression by fluorescence readout of the expressed reporter protein (e.g., GFP, BFP, RFP, or mCherry) from surviving colonies.

INTERPRETATION: Successful on-chip transformation is confirmed by kanamycin-resistant colony growth combined with detectable fluorescence from the expressed reporter protein, demonstrating that the digital microfluidic platform can automate the full chemically-competent-cell transformation, culture, and expression workflow traditionally performed by hand on the benchtop.
