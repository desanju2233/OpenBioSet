Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Droplet Microfluidic DNA Bisulfite Conversion (BSC) Module Using Magnetic Bead-Based Methylation on Beads (MOB) Chemistry

REAGENTS USED:
  - Extracted Genomic DNA (gDNA) sample (2 uL per reaction)
  - Ammonium Bisulfite / Zymo Lightning Conversion Reagent (13 uL, sulfonation and hydrolytic deamination of unmethylated cytosine)
  - Silica-Coated Superparamagnetic Beads (SSBs, Promega Magnesil KF MD, 5 uL, solid-phase DNA capture and transport medium)
  - M-Binding Buffer (60 uL, adsorbs sulfonated DNA onto the silica bead surface)
  - Wash Ethanol (40 uL, removes residual bisulfite reagent)
  - M-Desulphonation Buffer (20 uL, sodium hydroxide solution that desulfonates uracil-sulfonate into stable uracil)
  - M-Wash Buffer (40 uL x2, removes residual sodium hydroxide)
  - Elution Buffer / Tris-EDTA Buffer (releases purified, bisulfite-converted DNA from the SSBs)

STEPS:
1. Mix 2 uL of extracted gDNA with 13 uL of Lightning Conversion (bisulfite) reagent and 5 uL of SSBs to form a droplet; load into the first reservoir chamber of the droplet chip.
2. Heat the droplet to 95 C for 8 min to denature the DNA sample into single-stranded DNA.
3. Lower the temperature to 54 C for 1 h to allow the bisulfite to complete deamination of unmethylated cytosine and sulfonation, yielding uracil-sulfonate.
4. Merge the droplet with a 60 uL droplet of M-binding buffer (transported across an open channel by the magnetically-actuated SSB cluster) and briefly incubate to allow the sulfonated DNA to adsorb onto the silica surface of the beads.
5. Transport the SSBs (separated from the reagent droplet across a narrow sieve structure by magnetic force) into a 40 uL ethanol wash droplet to eliminate any remaining bisulfite reagent.
6. Transport the SSBs into a 20 uL M-desulphonation buffer droplet; this alkali solution desulfonates the uracil-sulfonate into stable uracil bases.
7. Wash the SSBs twice, transporting them sequentially into two separate 40 uL M-wash buffer droplets to remove residual sodium hydroxide.
8. Transport the SSBs into the elution (Tris-EDTA) buffer droplet and reverse the DNA-binding process (removing the magnet to allow the beads to disperse and release DNA) to elute the purified, bisulfite-converted DNA.
9. Decant the SSBs into the previous well (magnetically separating the beads from the eluted-DNA droplet) and retrieve the final droplet containing purified, bisulfite-converted DNA from the chip for downstream analysis (e.g., MethyLight qPCR using methylation-specific primers and TaqMan probes).

INTERPRETATION: Successful on-chip bisulfite conversion is confirmed by comparable qPCR cycle threshold (CT) values between the BSC chip and the standard in-tube MOB process (28.34 vs. 28.45, SD 0.14 vs. 0.25) using conversion-specific beta-Actin primers, absence of amplification in the unconverted negative control, and a strong linear relationship (R^2 > 0.99) between input DNA quantity (1-100 ng) and normalized recovered DNA quantity for both methylated and unmethylated ADAMTS1-targeting primer sets, with low variability across replicate lanes and devices, demonstrating that the droplet-based chip matches benchtop bisulfite conversion performance while substantially reducing pipetting steps and processing time.
