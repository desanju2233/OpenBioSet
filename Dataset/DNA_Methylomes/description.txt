Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Droplet-Based Bisulfite Sequencing ("Drop-BS") for High-Throughput Single-Cell DNA Methylome Library Preparation

REAGENTS USED:
  - Single-Nucleus Suspension (input nuclei for co-encapsulation)
  - Lysis Buffer (contains micrococcal nuclease, MNase, for single-nucleus lysis and genomic DNA fragmentation)
  - Droplet-Generation Oil (fluorinated carrier oil for water-in-oil droplet encapsulation, ~35 um diameter droplets)
  - Barcode Beads (hydrogel beads bearing unique, UV-photocleavable DNA barcode oligos)
  - End-Repair/Ligation Enzyme Mix (prepares fragmented scDNA ends for barcode ligation)
  - Ligation Mix (ligates released barcode oligos onto scDNA fragments)
  - Proteinase K (breaks droplets and digests protein to recover barcoded DNA for pooling)
  - Bisulfite Conversion Reagent (sodium bisulfite salt solution, converts unmethylated cytosines to uracil in droplets, ~99% conversion rate)
  - Random Primer (adds a second priming site to bisulfite-converted single-stranded DNA)
  - Klenow Fragment Mix (extends from the random primer to synthesize the second strand)
  - PCR Master Mix (used for both pre-amplification and indexing PCR)
  - P5/P7 Amplification Primers (pre-amplification adapter-addition primers)
  - i5/i7 Indexing Primers (sample-indexing primers for the final sequencing library)
  - EvaGreen Dye (intercalating dye for real-time monitoring of indexing PCR amplification)

STEPS:
1. scDNA encapsulation and fragmentation: Co-encapsulate single nuclei with lysis buffer (containing MNase) in oil droplets on a droplet-generation microfluidic device (~35 um droplets, ~10% single-occupancy, <0.5% double-occupancy) to lyse nuclei and fragment genomic DNA; incubate through a cold, room-temperature, and elevated-temperature step to complete lysis/fragmentation.
2. scDNA and barcode bead droplet pairing and fusion: Combine barcode beads with an end-repair/ligation enzyme mix, then fuse these bead-containing droplets with the reinjected scDNA droplets on a droplet-fusion microfluidic device (electrocoalescence), and add ligation mix to the fused droplets.
3. scDNA ligation and barcoding: Expose the fused droplets to UV light to release the barcoded oligos from the photocleavable linker on the beads, then carry out an extended (overnight) ligation step to tag each single cell's fragmented DNA with a unique barcode, followed by a brief high-temperature step to inactivate the ligation enzyme.
4. Recovery and bisulfite conversion: Break the droplets and digest with Proteinase K to pool the barcoded DNA; denature the pooled DNA to single strands, then co-encapsulate with bisulfite conversion reagent in droplets on a bisulfite-conversion microfluidic device, and incubate (high-temperature denaturation pulse followed by a longer moderate-temperature hold) to convert unmethylated cytosines to uracil (~99% conversion rate, validated with unmethylated lambda DNA).
5. Library preparation: Randomly prime the bisulfite-converted single-stranded DNA to add a second priming site, extend with a Klenow fragment polymerase to synthesize the complementary strand, then perform a low-cycle pre-amplification PCR to add P5/P7 adapter sequences, followed by an indexing PCR (with i5/i7 indexing primers) monitored in real time via EvaGreen intercalating-dye fluorescence to generate the final Illumina-compatible, barcoded single-cell bisulfite sequencing library.

INTERPRETATION: Successful library generation is confirmed by real-time EvaGreen fluorescence amplification during the indexing PCR step and by downstream sequencing metrics (per-cell read depth, bisulfite conversion rate ~99%, and species-mixing purity in control experiments), supporting use of Drop-BS to process up to 10,000 single cells within approximately 2 days for cell-type-resolved DNA methylome profiling of mixed cell lines and brain tissue.
