Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Droplet-Actuator Bead-Based Chemiluminescent Immunoassay for Cardiac Marker Detection (cTnI / CK-MB / Myoglobin)

REAGENTS USED:
  - Cardiac Sample (biological sample, e.g. whole blood, serum, or plasma, containing a cardiac marker such as cardiac troponin I, CK-MB, or myoglobin)
  - Capture Beads (magnetically responsive beads coated with primary capture antibody)
  - Blocking Antibody (blocking protein/antibody reagent)
  - Reporter Antibody (secondary reporter antibody)
  - Wash Buffer (droplet-based merge-and-split dilution wash buffer, 0.05M TRIS, 0.1 M NaCl, 0.1 mg/mL BSA, 0.02% Tween20, pH 9.5)
  - Chemiluminescent Substrate (detection substrate, e.g. APS-5)

STEPS:
1. Dispense a cardiac sample droplet, a capture-bead droplet, and a blocking-antibody droplet.
2. Mix the capture beads with the blocking antibody to form reagent 1.
3. Combine the sample droplet with reagent 1 to form the assay droplet; incubate at room temperature (~23C) for 3 minutes to allow capture antibody-antigen binding.
4. Execute a droplet-based wash step: merge the assay droplet with wash buffer and discard the resulting supernatant, removing unbound sample material.
5. Dispense a reporter-antibody droplet and merge it with the assay droplet; incubate 2 minutes at room temperature to form the capture-antibody-antigen-reporter-antibody complex.
6. Execute a droplet-based wash protocol (11 repeated merge-and-split dilution washes with wash buffer) to deplete unbound reporter antibody and antigen from the bead droplet.
7. Combine the washed bead droplet with chemiluminescent substrate; incubate ~2 minutes at 25C.
8. Detect the chemiluminescent signal for 5 seconds, indicative of the quantity of the cardiac marker in the biological sample.
9. If the measured signal exceeds the top-end of the standard curve, execute an auto-dilution sequence (merge with a diluent droplet, re-detect) to bring the concentration back within the linear range of detection.
10. Discard the analyzed droplet.

INTERPRETATION: Successful cardiac marker quantification is confirmed by a calibration curve relating chemiluminescent response (counts/s) to marker concentration (ng/mL) for cTn-I, CK-MB, and myoglobin, with good linearity and recovery percentages clustered near 100% across the calibrator concentration range, and with total assay time (sample loading to detectable signal) of well under 5-10 minutes for multiplexed cardiac marker panels, supporting use of the platform for rapid triage of chest-pain patients and assessment of acute myocardial infarction (AMI) risk.
