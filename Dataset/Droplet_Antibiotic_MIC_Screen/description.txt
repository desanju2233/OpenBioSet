Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Droplet-Based Digital Antibiotic Susceptibility (Minimum Inhibitory Concentration, MIC) Screen for Detecting Single-Cell Clonal Heteroresistance in an Isogenic Bacterial Population

REAGENTS USED:
  - Bacteria (Escherichia coli DH5-alpha carrying a TEM-20 beta-lactamase plasmid and a constitutively expressed YFP reporter plasmid, diluted to an encapsulation rate of ~0.1-0.3 bacteria per droplet)
  - Growth Medium (LB Lennox medium, co-encapsulated with the bacteria and antibiotic in each droplet)
  - Cefotaxime (beta-lactam antibiotic sodium salt; a separate droplet library is prepared for each concentration in a two-fold dilution series)

STEPS:
1. Droplet encapsulation: Using a flow-focusing microfluidic chip, an aqueous phase of bacteria, LB growth medium, and cefotaxime (at a fixed concentration for a given library) is encapsulated into monodisperse ~2 nL water-in-oil droplets stabilized by surfactant-stabilized fluorocarbon oil, forming a separate droplet library for each antibiotic concentration.
2. Dilution to single-cell occupancy: The starting bacterial suspension (~1x10^5 CFU/mL) is diluted so the average encapsulation rate (lambda) is ~0.1-0.3 bacteria per droplet, ensuring the vast majority of non-empty droplets contain only a single cell (~1,500 non-empty droplets screened per antibiotic concentration).
3. Off-chip incubation: Droplets are collected into a test tube and incubated off-chip overnight at 37 C, during which encapsulated bacteria proliferate and synthesize YFP unless growth is inhibited by the antibiotic.
4. Fluorescence detection: After incubation, each droplet is reintroduced to a microfluidic reading chip and its fluorescence is measured by confocal microscopy (488 nm excitation, 525/50 nm emission for YFP).
5. Digital growth classification: Each droplet's signal is compared against a fixed relative-fluorescence threshold (500) to classify it as growth-positive (1, strong fluorescence from a proliferated colony) or growth-negative (0, background fluorescence only, no proliferation).
6. Fraction/viability calculation: The fraction of positive droplets f+(c) is computed for each antibiotic concentration c and normalized by the positive fraction in the absence of antibiotic (f+(0)) to obtain single-cell viability F(c) = f+(c)/f+(0).
7. Repeat across concentration series: Steps 1-6 are repeated for a dilution series of cefotaxime concentrations (e.g., 16 two-fold dilutions in the virtual-array format, ranging from sub-inhibitory to fully inhibitory levels), each screened as an independent droplet library/measurement.
8. Disposal: After fluorescence readout, each analyzed droplet is discarded.

INTERPRETATION: The assay is digital and binary at the single-droplet level (grow/no-grow). Plotting viability F(c) against cefotaxime concentration and fitting with a Gompertz function yields the population MIC (concentration at 50% viability); the negative derivative of this fit gives the probability distribution p(MIC) of individual single-cell MICs, revealing clonal heteroresistance when the lowest concentration giving 100% inhibition is many-fold higher than the highest non-inhibitory concentration (heteroresistance was confirmed here, >8-fold). The same digital framework, combined with a colour-coded 'virtual array' of pooled dilutions, also allows precise measurement of the inoculum effect (MIC rising with bacterial density) and the amount of antibiotic per bacterium (MIA = MIC/N_CFU+), which remained stable (~0.5 ug/mL/CFU) across densities, supporting the method as a robust, high-throughput platform for characterizing heteroresistance and inoculum-density-resistant susceptibility testing.
