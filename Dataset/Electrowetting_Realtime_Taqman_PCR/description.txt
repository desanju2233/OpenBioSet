Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Electrowetting-Based Digital Microfluidics Coupled to Real-Time (TaqMan) PCR for Human SNP Allelic Discrimination

REAGENTS USED:
  - Allele 1 Control Template (human genomic DNA control containing allele 1 template only, targeted by the VIC-labeled probe)
  - Allele 2 Control Template (human genomic DNA control containing allele 2 template only, targeted by the FAM-labeled probe)
  - Both-Alleles Control Template (heterozygous control containing both allele 1 and allele 2 templates)
  - No-Template Control (no DNA)
  - TaqMan Allelic Discrimination Master Mix (commercially available TaqMan allelic discrimination kit reagent, ABI PN:4312559, for the C440T mutation of the CYC2C9*2 gene; contains differentially labeled FAM/VIC fluorescent probes, primers, dNTPs, and Taq polymerase; ~300 nL droplets)

STEPS:
1. For each of the four allelic controls (allele 1 only, allele 2 only, both alleles, no template), dispense a ~300 nL droplet of the control template onto the electrowetting chip.
2. Dispense a matching ~300 nL droplet of TaqMan master mix reagent for each control.
3. Actuate and merge each control droplet with its corresponding master mix droplet on the electrowetting chip to form four complete PCR reaction droplets.
4. Transport each droplet back and forth several hundred times under electrowetting control to verify standard droplet manipulations (transport, mixing, splitting, dispensing) are unaffected by the PCR reagents, then position the droplets at opposite ends of the electrode array.
5. Remove the electrowetting control voltage and thermocycle the entire chip inside the custom-built hot-air thermocycler mounted on the fluorescence microscope.
6. After each thermal cycle, read out FAM and VIC fluorescence intensity from each droplet in real time to build per-cycle amplification time-courses.
7. Discard each droplet after the thermocycling/detection run is complete.

INTERPRETATION: Successful allelic discrimination is confirmed by real-time fluorescence amplification curves showing successful DNA amplification within the droplets, and by end-point FAM/VIC intensity plots that cleanly separate droplets bearing allele 1 only, allele 2 only, both alleles, and no template, demonstrating that the addition of PCR reagents has little effect on electrowetting droplet manipulation, that the electrowetting chip environment does not inhibit PCR, and that the platform can discriminate a human SNP differing by only one base pair.
