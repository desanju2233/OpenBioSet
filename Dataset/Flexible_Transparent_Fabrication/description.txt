Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Flexible & Transparent Digital Microfluidics Fabrication Protocol (Droplet Manipulation and Actuation Characterization on an ITO/PET/PE EWOD Device)

REAGENTS USED:
  - DI Water (droplet manipulation demo and contact-angle/leakage/driving-voltage characterization, 8-10 uL droplets)
  - 0.1 M Sodium Chloride Solution (contact-angle characterization, 10 uL droplets)
  - 1 M Sodium Chloride Solution (contact-angle characterization, 10 uL droplets)

STEPS:
1. Droplet Manipulation Demo: A DI water droplet is dispensed onto the transparent ITO/PET electrode array (2 mm x 2 mm electrodes, 0.5 mm spacing) and actuated by electrowetting to move straight and turn across the array.
2. Droplet Merging Demo: A second DI water droplet is dispensed and actuated to merge with the first droplet on the same electrode array.
3. Contact Angle Characterization: DI water, 0.1 M NaCl, and 1 M NaCl droplets (10 uL each) are individually dispensed and actuated under increasing AC voltage (1 kHz); the contact angle of each droplet is measured, decreasing nearly linearly with voltage before reaching saturation.
4. Leakage Current Characterization: A droplet is dispensed and held between two electrodes on the Rain-X-treated PE film and PET substrate; the AC voltage supply is swept from 250 Vrms to 450 Vrms in 25 V increments, with the leakage current density measured five times at each voltage step.
5. Minimum Driving Voltage Characterization: DI water droplets of 8, 10, and 12 uL are each dispensed and actuated with electrode on/off switching times of 1, 2, 3, and 4 s, and the minimum voltage required for reliable droplet movement is measured at each combination.

INTERPRETATION: Successful device performance is confirmed by reliable droplet dispensing, translocation, and merging on the thin (less than 200 um), flexible, and optically transparent ITO/PET/PE device; a water contact angle increase from 78.3 to 103.2 degrees after Rain-X treatment; leakage current densities that remain low (tens to ~190 nA/mm2) across the 250-450 Vrms operating range, confirming the PE film is an acceptable thin dielectric; contact angles for DI water and NaCl solutions that decrease with increasing voltage toward saturation; and minimum driving voltages that decrease with slower electrode on/off switching and increase with droplet volume, together validating the device as a low-cost, transparent alternative to conventional glass/silicon EWOD chips for colorimetric and optical droplet-based assays.
