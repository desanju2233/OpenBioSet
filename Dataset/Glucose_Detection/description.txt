Paper doi: 10.1016/j.aca.2003.12.030
Demonstration video: No demonstration video located

Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Colorimetric enzyme test strip reaction for measuring glucose concentration in a fluid sample, run as a series of small droplet reactions on a lab-on-a-chip device.

REAGENTS USED:
  - glucose sample: the fluid being tested; five separate portions are used, one per trial, so a small calibration-style series of readings can be built up
  - enzyme reagent: a ready-mixed reagent containing glucose oxidase and peroxidase along with a color-forming dye; it reacts with glucose to produce a colored product, with more color developing as more glucose is present
  - distilled water: used in a side reaction alongside the reagent, prepared and set aside for each trial but not measured

STEPS:
1. Place a small portion of the glucose sample on the chip.
2. Add a small portion of the enzyme reagent next to it.
3. Bring the two together and mix them for about 10 seconds so the reaction can begin.
4. Hold the combined droplet in place and measure the color/light signal it gives off for 30 seconds. This reading reflects how much glucose was in the sample.
5. Separately, combine a portion of distilled water with a portion of the enzyme reagent, mix for 30 seconds, and discard it.
6. Repeat steps 1-5 four more times, for a total of five trials. In each trial the amount of glucose sample stays the same, but the amount of enzyme reagent added is changed (first a small amount, then twice as much, then twice as much again, then twice as much again, before returning to the original small amount for the fifth trial).
7. After all five trials are complete, discard the reacted droplets from each trial.

INTERPRETATION: Each of the five trials produces a signal reading whose strength corresponds to how much glucose was present in that sample. Comparing the five readings against each other (or against known glucose standards run the same way) lets a technician build a calibration curve and estimate the glucose concentration of an unknown sample. A stronger signal generally means more glucose reacted with the reagent.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
A NOTE ON HOW CLOSELY THIS MATCHES THE CITED SOURCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The cited paper (Srinivasan, Pamula & Fair, 2004) describes an electrowetting droplet-chip glucose assay using a colorimetric enzyme-kinetic method (Trinder's reaction): a glucose oxidase/peroxidase/dye reagent droplet is merged with a sample droplet, mixed for about 15 seconds, and the color signal is read continuously for at least 30 seconds. That final step - a mix, then a 30-second optical read whose strength scales with glucose concentration - matches this .bs file's five detect steps exactly on timing (30s) and closely on mixing time (10s in the code vs. 15s in the paper).

Two things differ and are worth being upfront about:

1. In the real protocol, the five (or more) calibration points are made by varying the GLUCOSE CONCENTRATION of the sample while keeping the reagent-to-sample ratio fixed. In this .bs file, the glucose amount is held constant across all five trials and it is instead the REAGENT amount that is varied (10, 20, 40, 80, then back to 10 units). This is a different way of generating a multi-point series, and it does not exactly reproduce the paper's dilution method, though the end goal (multiple readings to compare against each other) is the same. This is a benchmark-authoring choice, not something we changed.

2. The paper explicitly states that this kinetic method "does not require a blank reading." This .bs file nonetheless prepares a water+reagent droplet in every trial and discards it without ever reading it. It does not affect the final glucose readings (it is disposed of and never feeds into any detect step), but it is an extra, scientifically unnecessary step relative to the cited source. We are documenting it rather than silently removing it, since it is part of the original benchmark code.

No structural/dependency bugs were found in this file - each of the five glucose+reagent reaction droplets is read via detect before being discarded, so every reported reading is genuinely derived from its corresponding glucose sample. The .bs file also declares a "Sample" material that is never dispensed or used anywhere in the code; this appears to be a leftover/unused declaration from the original benchmark authoring and has no effect on execution.

Overall: the reaction chemistry, the fixed 30-second read, and the multi-trial calibration-style structure all point to this being an adaptation of the Srinivasan et al. 2004 droplet glucose assay for the BioScript benchmark suite, with the two documented differences above (what is varied per trial, and the extra unread blank step).
