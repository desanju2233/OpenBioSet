Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Cell-Free Glycoprotein Biosynthesis Protocol on Digital Microfluidic Chips (Flow-Based Microfluidic System for Cell-Free Glycoprotein Biosynthesis)

REAGENTS USED:
  - Plasmid DNA Stream (pJL1-sfGFP-DQNAT-6xHis acceptor protein plasmid, energy mix, salts, and metabolites)
  - S30 Cell-Free Extract Stream (E. coli BL21 Star (DE3) S30 crude extract plus T7 RNA polymerase)
  - CjLLOs Reagent (C. jejuni lipid-linked oligosaccharides bearing the GalNAc5(Glc)Bac heptasaccharide, glycan donor for the surface-tethered CjPglB oligosaccharyltransferase)
  - Wash Buffer (PBS with 10 mM imidazole, for IMAC resin washing)
  - Elution Buffer (PBS with 300 mM imidazole, for IMAC product recovery)

STEPS:
1. Protein Synthesis Module: A plasmid/energy/salts/metabolites stream and an S30 extract/T7 RNA polymerase stream are combined at the inlet of a serpentine microfluidic chip and mixed by diffusion as they travel through the channel; each chip provides a 30 min reaction residence time, and multiple chips are linked in series (up to four chips for a 2 h total residence time) to continuously synthesize sfGFP-DQNAT-6xHis.
2. Protein Glycosylation Module: The synthesized protein stream is continuously flowed over CjPglB oligosaccharyltransferase that is site-specifically biotinylated and tethered to the channel surface (via silane-PEG5000-biotin/NeutrAvidin/biotin chemistry), together with CjLLO glycan donor; the device is held at 30 C and the reaction proceeds across a series of chips at 30 min residence time per chip, achieving up to 100% glycosylation efficiency within 2 h.
3. Protein Purification Module (Loading/Flowthrough): The glycosylated protein stream is passed through a channel packed with Ni2+-charged affinity resin held in place by an array of posts at the outlet; hexahistidine-tagged glycoprotein binds the resin while unbound material exits as the flowthrough fraction.
4. Protein Purification Module (Wash): The inlet stream is switched to wash buffer (PBS + 10 mM imidazole) at 2 uL/min to remove non-specifically bound proteins and cellular debris from the resin bed; the wash fraction is collected.
5. Protein Purification Module (Elution): The inlet stream is switched to elution buffer (PBS + 300 mM imidazole) at 2 uL/min to recover the purified hexahistidine-tagged glycoprotein product as the elution fraction; the resin-bound flowthrough droplet is then discarded.

INTERPRETATION: Successful glycoprotein biosynthesis is confirmed by significantly increased sfGFP titers under continuous flow relative to batch/off-chip CFPS (Figure 2A), immunoblot detection of the monoglycosylated (g1) product with anti-His and hR6 (anti-heptasaccharide) antibodies confirming efficient on-chip glycosylation comparable to off-chip untethered reactions (Figure 3C), and recovery of purified glycoprotein product at 78 +/- 10% purity with 45 +/- 14% capture efficiency via the on-chip IMAC module (Figure 4), together demonstrating a first-in-kind modular glycosylation-on-a-chip platform for continuous, decentralized glycoprotein biomanufacturing.
