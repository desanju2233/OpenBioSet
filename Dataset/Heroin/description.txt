Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Direct Competitive Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) for Opiate (Heroin) Detection

REAGENTS USED:
  - Sample (Urine)
  - Immobilized Capture Antigen (Heroin enzyme)
  - Drug-Enzyme Conjugate (Heroin conjugate)
  - Wash Buffer
  - TMB Substrate (K-Blue substrate)
  - Stop Solution

STEPS:
1. Primary Incubation: Add 10 units of the urine sample and mix with the immobilized heroin enzyme. Add 100 units of the heroin drug-enzyme conjugate and mix for 60 seconds. Incubate the mixture at 23°C (room temperature) for 45 minutes to allow free heroin/opiates in the sample to compete with the conjugate for antibody binding sites. Discard the supernatant droplet.
2. Wash Cycle: Wash the reaction zone three times to remove any unbound sample and unbound conjugate. For each wash cycle, add 300 units of wash buffer, mix with the retained enzyme for 45 seconds, and discard the wash supernatant.
3. Substrate Development: Add 100 units of K-Blue substrate to the retained enzyme. Mix and incubate at 23°C for 30 minutes to allow the colorimetric reaction to develop.
4. Stop Reaction and Detection: Add 100 units of stop solution and mix for 60 seconds to halt the enzymatic reaction. Measure the resulting optical signal (absorbance) of the final droplet. Discard the final reaction mixture.

INTERPRETATION: The final signal intensity (absorbance) is inversely proportional to the concentration of heroin/opiates in the sample. A lower signal indicates a higher presence of heroin, while a higher signal indicates little to no heroin (as more conjugate successfully bound to the antibodies).

A NOTE ON HOW CLOSELY THIS MATCHES THE CITED SOURCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The authored BioScript code closely matches the Neogen Corporation commercial Opiate Group Forensic ELISA kit protocol. The standard microplate-based competitive ELISA for opiates (including heroin) has been adapted for a Digital Microfluidic Biochip (DMFB). Volumetric mismatches and optical detection errors (measuring fluorescence instead of absorbance) present in earlier iterations were corrected. The standard room temperature incubations are explicitly set to 23°C, the wash cycles reflect the discrete 3-cycle loop with 300 µL volumes, and the continuous multi-well reading has been successfully translated into a singular droplet absorbance detection step.
