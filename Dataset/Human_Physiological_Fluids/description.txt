Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Digital Microfluidic (Electrowetting) Colorimetric Enzyme-Kinetic Glucose Assay on Human Physiological Fluids (Serum, Plasma, Urine, Saliva)

REAGENTS USED:
  - Calibrant (100 mg dL-1 glucose standard solution, 1.5 uL droplets)
  - Glucose Reagent (6 U mL-1 glucose oxidase, 6 U mL-1 peroxidase, 6 mM 4-aminoantipyrine, 6 mM TOPS, in 0.1 M phosphate buffered saline pH 7.0, 1.5 uL droplets)
  - Plasma Sample (1.5 uL droplets)
  - Serum Sample (1.5 uL droplets)
  - Saliva Sample (spiked with glucose, 1.5 uL droplets)
  - Urine Sample (spiked with glucose, 1.5 uL droplets; subject to uric acid interference with the Trinder reaction)

STEPS:
1. Calibration run: Dispense a 1.5 uL calibrant droplet (100 mg/dL glucose standard) and a 1.5 uL glucose reagent droplet onto adjacent electrodes (electrode pitch 1.5 mm, spacer height 500 um).
2. Merge and mix the calibrant/reagent droplet by shuttling across multiple electrodes for 15 s.
3. Measure absorbance continuously for at least 30 s using the LED-photodiode setup (545 nm green LED, TSL257 photodiode) and compute RateCALIB from the slope of the absorbance-vs-time curve.
4. Plasma assay: Dispense a 1.5 uL plasma droplet and a 1.5 uL glucose reagent droplet, merge/mix for 15 s, and measure absorbance for at least 30 s to obtain RatePLASMA.
5. Serum assay: Repeat the same dispense/merge-mix/absorbance-read cycle using serum in place of plasma to obtain RateSERUM.
6. Saliva assay: Repeat the same cycle using (glucose-spiked) saliva to obtain RateSALIVA.
7. Urine assay: Repeat the same cycle using (glucose-spiked) urine to obtain RateURINE.
8. Concentration calculation: For each sample, compute CSAMPLE = (RateSAMPLE / RateCALIB) x 100 mg dL-1.
9. Reference comparison: Compare each on-chip concentration against a reference value obtained on a bench-top spectrophotometer (Gensys 20) using a conventional 100-fold dilution; note that the urine result shows a significant deviation from the reference value, attributed to interference by uric acid reacting with hydrogen peroxide in the Trinder reaction (interference that the 100-fold dilution in the reference method suppresses but the digital-microfluidic chip's 2-fold dilution does not).

INTERPRETATION: Successful glucose quantification is confirmed by close agreement between the on-chip and reference concentrations for plasma, serum, and saliva (total error 3, 8, and 3 mg dL-1 respectively), demonstrating basic feasibility of colorimetric enzyme-kinetic bioassays on real physiological samples using an electrowetting-based digital microfluidic lab-on-a-chip; the larger urine discrepancy (16 mg dL-1) highlights a matrix-specific interference (uric acid) that must be addressed (e.g., via additional sample dilution/wash) for that fluid type.
