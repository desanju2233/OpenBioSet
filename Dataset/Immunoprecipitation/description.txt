Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Digital Microfluidic Immunoprecipitation (DMF-IP) for Targeted Protein Purification with Mass Spectrometry Readout

REAGENTS USED:
  - Magnetic Particles (superparamagnetic particles coated with an antibody against the target protein)
  - Sample (complex biological matrix, e.g., serum, containing the target analyte)
  - Wash Buffer (Tris-HCl, pH 7.6)
  - Elution Buffer (100 mM citric acid)

STEPS:
1. Particle Loading: Dispense a droplet of antibody-functionalized magnetic particles onto the device.
2. Capture: Dispense a droplet of sample and merge it with the immobilized particles. Actively resuspend and mix for 10 minutes to allow the target analyte to bind the antibody-coated particles.
3. Wash Cycle: Repeat twice - dispense a droplet of Tris-HCl wash buffer, merge with the particle-bound complex, and mix for approximately 3 minutes to remove unbound sample matrix.
4. Elution: Dispense a droplet of 100 mM citric acid elution buffer and merge with the washed particles. Mix for 5 minutes to release the purified target from the particles.
5. Detection: Recover the eluted droplet and analyze the purified target by direct-infusion tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) for 1 minute. Discard the final reaction mixture.

INTERPRETATION: Successful immunoprecipitation is confirmed by a strong mass spectrometry signal for the target protein's diagnostic tryptic peptide transition in the eluted droplet, in contrast to the low signal observed for the same transition in unprocessed sample matrix, demonstrating effective removal of background matrix and preconcentration of the target analyte.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A NOTE ON HOW CLOSELY THIS MATCHES THE CITED SOURCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The authored BioScript code is a direct translation of the core, small-volume (3-5 uL) eight-step DMF-IP procedure described by Seale et al. (2016): particle loading, sample capture, a two-cycle wash, elution, and MS detection. Wash and elution mixing durations (10 min capture, ~3 min per wash, 5 min elution) and the elution buffer identity (100 mM citric acid) are taken directly from the paper's DMF-IP General Procedure, and the 5-minute elution time matches the value the authors settled on after their own optimization experiments (Figure 2), rather than an arbitrarily chosen number. Because BioScript's instruction set exposes only dispense, mix, heat, repeat, detect, and dispose, the paper's explicit magnet-engage/particle-isolation steps (steps 2, 4, and 8, in which a magnet immobilizes particles so the supernatant can be exchanged) have no dedicated primitive and are folded implicitly into the surrounding dispense/mix pairs - the same abstraction already used for wash cycles in the ELISA-style scripts in this test suite. The paper's second technique, P-CLIP (a macro-to-microscale paper-wicking preconcentration interface for samples >=100 uL), is a physically distinct workflow involving passive lateral-flow pumping and sequential electrode deactivation with no analog in this instruction set, and is intentionally not modeled; only the core small-volume DMF-IP protocol shown in Figure 1 is captured here.
