PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Digital Microfluidics for Automated Proteomic Processing
Source Link  : https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3157851/pdf/jove-33-1603.pdf
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.3791/1603
Journal      : Journal of Visualized Experiments (JoVE), 2009, Vol. 33
Platform     : Digital Microfluidics (DMF) -- two-plate EWOD configuration


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a digital microfluidics (DMF) based method for fully automated,
integrated proteomic sample processing on a single miniaturised device. The workflow covers
all major biochemical processing steps required in clinical proteomics prior to mass
spectrometric analysis: protein extraction by precipitation, resolubilization, reduction,
alkylation, and enzymatic (tryptic) digestion.

DMF is a microscale fluid-handling technique in which nanoliter-to-microliter droplets are
manipulated on an open surface coated with a dielectric layer and electrodes. Applying an
electrical potential to the droplet causes charges to accumulate on either side of the
dielectric, creating electrostatic handles that allow droplets to be dispensed, moved, merged,
mixed, and split. This makes DMF a natural fit for rapid, sequential, multistep, miniaturised
automated biochemical assays.

The paper demonstrates the method using bovine serum albumin (BSA) as a model protein,
achieving identification of 25 distinct peptides (99.9% confidence interval) with 44% sequence
coverage by nano-HPLC-MS/MS after on-chip processing. The approach eliminates the human error
inherent in conventional macroscale pipetting-based or robot-based proteomic sample handling
and enables precise duplication of conditions across laboratories.


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Configuration : Two-plate DMF device
Bottom plate  : Patterned chromium (Cr) electrodes (250 nm) on glass substrate.
                Dielectric: Parylene-C (2-5 um, chemical vapor deposition).
                Hydrophobic layer: Teflon-AF (50 nm, 1% wt/wt in Fluorinert FC-40,
                spin-coated at 2000 rpm for 60 s, post-baked at 160 C for 10 min).
Top plate     : Unpatterned indium tin oxide (ITO) glass (transparent contiguous electrode).
                Hydrophobic layer: Teflon-AF (50 nm, same conditions as bottom plate).
Gap           : 140 um total (set by two pieces of double-sided tape on bottom plate edges).
Actuation     : 100 V_rms / 18 kHz AC signals applied via 40-pin connectors.
Control       : LabView (National Instruments, Austin, TX) with in-house calibration program.
                Home-built control box: high-voltage relay array (NI DaqPad), function
                generator, and amplifier. Feedback control calibrated at startup.
Droplet vol.  : ~600 nL per dispensed droplet (defined by 1 mm square electrode dimensions,
                50 um gaps, 2.5 mm square reservoirs).

Fabrication (Part 1):
  1. Clean glass in Piranha solution (3:1 H2SO4:H2O2, 30%) for 10 min with agitation.
  2. Rinse in DI water, dry with N2, deposit 250 nm Cr by electron beam evaporation.
  3. Dehydrate Cr-coated substrate: rinse in isopropanol, bake 5 min at 115 C.
  4. Spin-coat HMDS (30 s, 3000 rpm) then Shipley S1811 photoresist (same parameters).
  5. Pre-bake (100 C, 2 min); expose to UV for 5 s through photomask.
  6. Develop in Shipley MF 321 developer (3 min); wash in DI water; post-bake 100 C, 1 min.
  7. Etch exposed Cr in Cr etchant (30 s); strip photoresist in AZ300T (10 min); rinse DI/N2.
  8. Deposit Parylene-C (2-5 um, CVD); spin-coat Teflon-AF 50 nm; post-bake 160 C, 10 min.
  9. Coat ITO glass top plate with Teflon-AF 50 nm; post-bake 160 C, 10 min.


RESERVOIR MAP (Figure 1b)
--------------------------
R-1  : Protein sample (serum or purified protein dissolved in working buffer WB)
R-2  : Precipitant -- 20% Trichloroacetic acid (TCA) in DI water
R-3  : Waste reservoir (receives supernatant after precipitation and wash eluates)
R-4  : Wash buffer -- 70/30 Chloroform/Acetonitrile (ACN)
R-5  : Resolubilizing buffer -- 100 mM Ammonium Bicarbonate pH 8.0, 0.08% Pluronic F127 w/v
R-6  : Reductant -- 10 mM TCEP (tris(2-carboxyethyl)phosphine) in WB
R-7  : Alkylating agent -- 12 mM Iodoacetamide (IAM) in WB
R-8  : Trypsin -- dissolved in WB at 1/5 of total protein sample concentration


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Device Set-up (Part 2)
  - Remove polymer coatings from bottom substrate contact pads by scraping with scalpel.
  - Couple exposed pads to 40-pin connectors.
  - Power on LabView computer, control box, function generator, and amplifier.
  - Assemble device: position two pieces of double-sided tape (140 um total) on bottom
    substrate edges; complete with unpatterned ITO top plate (Teflon side facing down).
  - Attach ground connector to top substrate.
  - Run LabView calibration program to initialise feedback control.
  - Load sample and reagents into reservoirs R-1 through R-8.
  - Load and execute LabView actuation code to initiate droplet actuation.

Step 2 | Sample and Reagent Preparation (Part 3)
  Working buffer  : 100 mM TrisHCl pH 7.8, 0.08% Pluronic F127 w/v.
  R-1 (sample)    : Dissolve protein(s) in WB; pipette into R-1.
  R-2 (TCA)       : 20% TCA in DI water; pipette into R-2.
  R-4 (wash)      : 70/30 Chloroform/ACN; pipette into R-4.
  R-5 (resolub.)  : 100 mM AmBic pH 8.0, 0.08% Pluronic F127; pipette into R-5.
  R-6 (TCEP)      : 10 mM TCEP in WB; pipette into R-6.
  R-7 (IAM)       : 12 mM Iodoacetamide in WB; pipette into R-7.
  R-8 (trypsin)   : Trypsin in WB at 1/5 total protein concentration; pipette into R-8.

Step 3 | Protein Extraction by Precipitation (Part 4, Steps 2)
  Inputs     : ProteinSample (R-1), TrichloroaceticAcid (R-2)
  Operation  : Dispense ~600 nL protein droplet from R-1 and ~600 nL TCA droplet from R-2.
               Merge droplets on device surface (Figure 2, Frame 1).
               Incubate 5 min at room temperature; proteins precipitate onto surface
               (Figure 2, Frames 2-3).
               Actuate supernatant away to waste reservoir R-3 (Figure 2, Frame 4).
  Duration   : 5 min (300 s)
  Temperature: Room temperature (~25 C)

Step 4 | Precipitate Washing (Part 4, Step 3)
  Inputs     : WashBuffer (R-4) -- 70/30 Chloroform/ACN
  Operation  : Dispense 3 sequential ~600 nL wash buffer droplets from R-4.
               Drive each droplet across the precipitated protein to waste reservoir R-3
               (Figure 2, Frame 5).
  Wash cycles: 3 (unrolled as 3 independent dispense/drain operations in BioScript)
  Purpose    : Removes residual TCA, soluble contaminants, and lipids from precipitate.

Step 5 | Protein Resolubilization (Part 4, Step 4)
  Input      : ResolubilizingBuffer (R-5)
  Operation  : Allow precipitate to dry on device surface.
               Dispense ~600 nL resolubilizing buffer droplet from R-5 onto precipitate.
               Incubate 20 min until precipitate fully dissolves (Figure 2, Frame 6).
  Duration   : 20 min (1200 s)
  Temperature: Room temperature (~25 C)
  Notes      : AmBic buffer (pH 8.0) is trypsin-compatible. Pluronic F127 prevents biofouling.

Step 6 | Reduction (Part 4, Step 5)
  Inputs     : TCEP_Reductant (R-6), resolubilizedSample
  Operation  : Dispense ~600 nL TCEP droplet from R-6 and merge with sample droplet.
               Mix combined droplet by circular actuation across 6 electrodes.
               Incubate 1 h at room temperature in a humidified chamber (Figure 3, Frames 1-2).
  Duration   : 60 min (3600 s)
  Temperature: 25 C (room temperature, humidified chamber)
  Purpose    : TCEP reduces disulfide bonds between cysteine residues.

Step 7 | Alkylation (Part 4, Step 6)
  Inputs     : Iodoacetamide (R-7), reducedSample
  Operation  : Dispense ~600 nL IAM droplet from R-7 and merge with sample droplet.
               Mix combined droplet by circular actuation across 6 electrodes (same as Step 6).
               Incubate 15 min at room temperature in a humidified chamber, protected from
               light (Figure 3, Frames 2-3).
  Duration   : 15 min (900 s)
  Temperature: 25 C (room temperature, humidified chamber, light-protected)
  Purpose    : IAM alkylates free thiols on cysteines to prevent re-oxidation of disulfides.

Step 8 | Tryptic Digestion (Part 4, Step 7)
  Inputs     : Trypsin (R-8), alkylatedSample
  Operation  : Dispense ~600 nL trypsin droplet from R-8 and merge with sample droplet.
               Mix combined droplet by circular actuation across 6 electrodes (same as Step 6).
               Incubate 3 h at 37 C on a hot plate in a humidified chamber
               (Figure 3, Frames 3-4).
  Duration   : 180 min (10800 s)
  Temperature: 37 C (hot plate, humidified chamber)
  Purpose    : Trypsin cleaves at Lys-X and Arg-X peptide bonds.

Step 9 | Post-Processing Sample Preparation (Part 5, off-chip)
  1. Remove top substrate; quench digestion by adding 0.6 mL of 2.5% TFA in water.
  2. Purify quenched product using C18 ZipTips (Millipore, Billerica, MA) per manufacturer
     instructions.
  3. Dilute purified sample in DI water to a final volume of 100 mL.
  Notes      : TFA quenches trypsin enzymatic activity. C18 ZipTip SPE desalts and
               concentrates peptides for MS injection.

Step 10 | Mass Spectrometry Analysis (Part 6)
  HPLC system : Eksigent Technologies nano-HPLC
  Trap column : 3 mm dia. C18 beads, 150 um ID x 2.5 cm
  Sep. column : 3 um dia. C18 beads, 75 um ID x 15 cm (reversed-phase)
  MS          : LTQ (Thermo-Fisher Scientific)
  Emitter     : Nanoelectrospray emitter, 20 um ID tapered to 10 um ID (NewObjective)
  LC method   :
    Loading    -- 2 uL sample at 5 uL/min in 5% acetonitrile/water onto trap column
    Separation -- 0.5 uL/min; linear gradient 20% to 80% ACN over 25 min
    Hold       -- 80% ACN for additional 10 min
  DB search   : SEQUEST (Bioworks v3.01, Thermo-Fisher Scientific)
                Probability threshold: < 1.0E-03 (99.9% confidence interval)
                Sequence coverage threshold: >= 30%
  Results     : BSA model protein; 25 distinct peptides identified; 44% sequence coverage
                (Figure 4)


BIOSCRIPT COMPILER NOTES
-------------------------
BioScript file : jebrail_dmf_proteomic_processing.bs
Compiler       : lilott8/BioScript (ANTLR4-based)
IR output      : 4 basic blocks (BB1-BB4), 1 if/else gate, 4 CFG edges
DAG file       : jebrail_dmf_proteomic_processing_main_dag.dot
Duration units : All durations converted to minutes (m) or seconds (s);
                 BioScript supports s and m only (no h token).
                 1h  (reduction)  -> 60m  (IR: 3600.0 SECOND)
                 3h  (digestion)  -> 180m (IR: 10800.0 SECOND)
Modules        : proteinPrecipitationDetection, digestCompletionDetection
Manifests      : ProteinSample, TrichloroaceticAcid, WashBuffer, ResolubilizingBuffer,
                 TCEP_Reductant, Iodoacetamide, Trypsin

CFG structure  :
  BB1  main         -- entry: precipitation, wash, resolubilization, reduction,
                        alkylation, digestion, QC detect
  BB2  bsbbif_2_t   -- digestSignal > 0: digest complete; dispose(digestedSample)
  BB3  bsbbif_3_f   -- digestSignal <= 0: digest incomplete; drain(digestedSample)
  BB4  bsbbif_4_j   -- terminal: dispose(digestSignal)

  CFG edges: BB1->BB2, BB1->BB3, BB2->BB4, BB3->BB4 (diamond shape)

Abstraction notes:
  Precipitation  : mix + drain abstracts TCA-driven protein precipitation and
                   supernatant removal to R-3. No native precipitate primitive in BioScript.
  Wash unrolling : 3 wash steps unrolled as 3 explicit dispense/drain pairs.
                   No loop construct supported in lilott8/BioScript compiler.
  Resolubilization: Second ProteinSample dispense abstracts the on-surface dried
                   precipitate being re-contacted with the buffer droplet (compiler
                   requires a live variable to mix into).
  Circular mixing: BioScript mix abstracts both the circular 6-electrode electrode
                   actuation and the subsequent timed incubation in a single statement.
  Temperature    : heat at 25c models room-temperature incubation steps;
                   heat at 37c models hot plate used for trypsin digestion.
  drain vs dispose: drain used for waste removal and failed QC paths;
                   dispose used for successfully processed sample at QC pass branch.
