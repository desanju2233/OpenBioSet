Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Digital Microfluidic Platform for Multiplexed Fluorometric Enzyme Assays (Acid alpha-Glucosidase/GAA for Pompe Disease and Acid alpha-Galactosidase/GLA for Fabry Disease) in Dried Blood Spots for Newborn Lysosomal Storage Disease Screening

REAGENTS USED:
  - DBS Extract (single 3-mm dried-blood-spot punch extracted in 100 uL of 0.1% w/v Tween20 extraction buffer, 30 min at room temperature; one extract shared for both GAA and GLA assays)
  - GAA Reagent (2.5 mmol/L 4-methylumbelliferyl-alpha-D-glucoside substrate in sodium acetate, pH 3.8, with 6 umol/L acarbose inhibitor)
  - GLA Reagent (5 mmol/L 4-methylumbelliferyl-alpha-D-galactoside substrate in 40 mmol/L sodium acetate, pH 4.5, with 75 umol/L N-acetyl-D-galactosamine inhibitor)
  - Extraction Buffer (EXT: 0.1% w/v Tween20 in water)
  - Stop Buffer (STB: 0.2 mol/L NaHCO3, pH 10.0, 0.01% w/v Tween20)
  - Calibrant (known concentration of 4-methylumbelliferone, 4-MU, used for 2-point fluorescence calibration)

STEPS:
1. Two-point fluorescence calibration: dispense a calibrant droplet and read fluorescence (high calibrant value); dispense a stop-buffer-only droplet and read fluorescence (low calibrant/background value).
2. Substrate background (surrogate t=0): merge GAA reagent with extraction buffer (no DBS extract) plus stop buffer and read fluorescence as the GAA background; repeat the same merge/read for GLA reagent as the GLA background.
3. For each DBS extract loaded onto the cartridge (up to 11 samples per run): dispense one extract droplet and split it into two parallel reactions by merging separately with GAA reagent and GLA reagent.
4. t=0 measurement: immediately merge a droplet from each reaction with stop buffer and read fluorescence (GAA t=0 and GLA t=0), then discard these aliquots.
5. Incubate the remaining GAA and GLA reaction droplets at 37 C (1 h for the reduced-incubation protocol; 6 h in the original bench-comparison protocol).
6. Endpoint measurement: merge each incubated reaction droplet with stop buffer and read endpoint fluorescence (GAA endpoint and GLA endpoint), converting readings to enzyme activity via the calibration curve.
7. Screening interpretation: compare GAA and GLA endpoint activity against the normal/affected separation boundary established from control and disease samples; flag and discard reaction droplets whose activity falls at or below the cutoff as presumptively affected (Pompe or Fabry), otherwise report as normal.
