Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Mobile Microdroplet Megascale Detector (µMD) Platform for Rapid, Sub-Picogram-per-Milliliter, Multiplexed Digital Droplet ELISA (dELISA) of Proteins

REAGENTS USED:
  - Serum Sample (human serum or FBS spiked with target proteins, input to the microbead processor)
  - GM-CSF Antibody-Coated Beads (color-coded microbeads, d = 5.4 um, functionalized with anti-GM-CSF capture antibody)
  - IL-6 Antibody-Coated Beads (color-coded microbeads, d = 4.5 um, functionalized with anti-IL-6 capture antibody)
  - Wash Buffer (T20 buffer, used between each labelling step to remove unbound reagent from the immobilized beads)
  - Detection Antibody (biotinylated/enzyme-linked detection antibody solution, 0.7 nM in T20 buffer, forms the sandwich immunocomplex)
  - ELISA Substrate (QuantaRed Enhanced Chemifluorescent HRP Substrate, mixed with released beads before droplet encapsulation)

STEPS:
1. Bead capture incubation: Color-coded GM-CSF and IL-6 antibody-coated beads are mixed with the serum sample and incubated together for 60 minutes so their target proteins are captured from the sample.
2. Bead immobilization and first wash: The beads are immobilized on a semipermeable membrane in the microbead processor and washed with T20 buffer to remove unbound serum components.
3. Detection antibody labelling: 0.1 mL of 0.7 nM detection antibody in T20 buffer is delivered to the immobilized beads and incubated for 30 minutes, forming the sandwich immunocomplex on beads that captured a target protein.
4. Second wash and release: The beads are washed again with T20 buffer to remove unbound detection antibody, then released from the membrane by reversing the flow.
5. Droplet encapsulation: The released beads are mixed with the ELISA (HRP) substrate in a herringbone mixing channel and encapsulated, one bead per droplet on average, into water-in-oil droplets using a parallelized (100-generator) Millipede-geometry droplet generator.
6. Enzymatic amplification (delay line): The droplets pass through an on-chip spiral delay-line channel for approximately 3.2 minutes, allowing the HRP enzyme on positive beads to amplify the fluorescence signal to a detectable level.
7. Fluorescence detection: Each droplet is optically interrogated using time domain-encoded, mobile-phone-camera-based fluorescence detection to determine (i) whether it contains a bead and its color code (GM-CSF or IL-6 target), and (ii) whether it fluoresces red, indicating capture of a target protein molecule.
8. Digital classification and disposal: Each droplet is scored as positive or negative for its target based on the red fluorescence signal relative to a detection threshold, tabulated as active-enzymes-per-bead counts for each target, and then discarded.

INTERPRETATION: The assay is digital: each droplet reports a binary positive/negative signal for its bead-associated target, and the fraction of red-fluorescing beads (active enzymes per bead, AEB) is converted to protein concentration via a calibration curve. The platform achieved limits of detection of 0.0045 pg/mL (320 aM) for GM-CSF and 0.0070 pg/mL (350 aM) for IL-6 in PBS, matched the sensitivity of the laboratory gold-standard Simoa digital ELISA system (R^2 = 0.95 in spiked FBS), showed negligible cross-talk between the GM-CSF and IL-6 channels in duplex measurements (R^2 > 0.99 for both targets against spiked concentrations), and measured endogenous GM-CSF and IL-6 in human serum from 14 subjects with excellent agreement to Simoa (R^2 = 0.96), supporting its use as a low-cost, mobile, high-throughput point-of-care platform for ultrasensitive multiplexed protein biomarker detection.
