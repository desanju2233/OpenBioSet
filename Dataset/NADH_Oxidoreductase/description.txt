SOURCE LINK: https://bio-protocol.org/en/bpdetail?id=5722
PAPER DOI: https://doi.org/10.21769/BioProtoc.5722

ASSAY TYPE:
Enzymatic activity assay of 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase (NADH-Dependent Oxidoreductase Activity).

REAGENTS USED:
- Reaction Buffer (contains acetoacetyl-CoA substrate, 30 uM final)
- NADH Solution (10 mM)
- Purified OsAIM1 Enzyme (0.5 ug)
- Purified GST Protein (0.5 ug, negative control)

STEPS:
1. Primary Preparation (Sample): Dispense 100 units of the reaction buffer containing the acetoacetyl-CoA substrate at 25 degrees C. Dispense 1 unit of the 10 mM NADH solution to obtain a final NADH concentration of 0.1 mM. Mix thoroughly to ensure homogenization.
2. Enzymatic Reaction (Sample): Initiate the reaction by dispensing 1 unit of the purified OsAIM1 protein. Mix immediately to ensure uniform distribution of the enzyme in the reaction solution.
3. Detection (Sample): Measure the absorbance at 340 nm, corresponding to the characteristic absorption peak of NADH. Record absorbance values every 15 seconds in a 4-cycle loop (60 seconds) to monitor continuous oxidation. Discard the droplet.
4. Control Branch: Dispense 100 units of reaction buffer and 1 unit of NADH solution, then mix. Dispense 1 unit of purified GST protein as a negative control to account for non-enzymatic NADH oxidation. Mix and measure absorbance every 15 seconds for 4 cycles, then discard.

INTERPRETATION:
The enzymatic reaction rate is determined from the linear decrease in absorbance at 340 nm over time, reflecting NADH oxidation during the reaction. This accompanies the reversible NADH-dependent reduction of acetoacetyl-CoA to 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA.

The authored BioScript code closely matches the cited Hu et al. (2026) protocol. The continuous microplate spectrophotometric reading has been adapted to discrete droplet absorbance detection using a timed multi-read loop. Standard reaction volumes (100 uL buffer, 1 uL NADH) map cleanly to discrete volume units, and the parallel negative control branch utilizing GST protein ensures high-fidelity translation to the digital microfluidic biochip (DMFB) architecture.
