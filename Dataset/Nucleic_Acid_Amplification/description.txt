Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Hybrid Digital Microfluidics-Droplet Microfluidics (DMF-DrMF) Platform for On-Demand Nano-Liter Droplet Generation from LAMP Reaction Buffer

REAGENTS USED:
  - Silicone Oil (5 cSt, Sigma-Aldrich) - fills the DMF section cavity between top and bottom plates
  - Mineral Oil (light oil for molecular biology) with 0.5% wt/wt Span80 surfactant - oil phase circulating in the DrMF section
  - LAMP Buffer (1x ThermoPol reaction buffer, New England Biolabs) - aqueous phase used as a representative nucleic acid amplification test liquid

STEPS:
1. Slowly introduce silicone oil into the DMF section to fill the cavity between the top and bottom plates without producing air gaps.
2. Insert mineral oil into the DrMF section at a low flow rate (500 nL/min) to slowly expel air from the device without forming air bubbles; confirm no air bubbles remain in either section before proceeding.
3. Insert LAMP buffer into the DMF section by activating the larger reservoir electrode and pipetting the buffer through the hole drilled in the top plate.
4. Move the LAMP buffer toward the DMF-DrMF interfacing electrode by sequentially activating and deactivating neighboring DMF electrodes.
5. Activate the DrMF syringe pumps: apply positive (pushing) pressure to the oil phase and negative (pulling) pressure to the aqueous phase (LAMP buffer) at the outlet, using an AC actuation signal of 40 VRMS at 5 kHz maintained at the interfacial electrode to ensure a constant buffer supply.
6. Generate nano-liter droplets at the flow-focusing junction between the DMF and DrMF sections; test oil (pushing) flows from 500 to 980 nL/min against a constant LAMP buffer (pulling) flow of 1000 nL/min, always progressing from smaller to larger flows to avoid excessive pressure buildup.
7. Record 20-25 second videos of droplet flow through the DrMF microfluidic channel using a monochrome camera with magnification lens and LED-based illumination.
8. Process the videos (cropping, grayscale conversion, thresholding) to isolate droplets from the background and determine droplet volume, circulation speed, and production frequency; export results to a .csv file.

INTERPRETATION: Successful hybrid DMF-DrMF operation is confirmed by stable, narrowly-distributed nano-liter droplet production (0.5-2 nL droplet volumes, 2-4 droplets/second production frequency, and circulation speeds up to ~1540 um/s) across the tested oil flow range, matching the throughput of standalone DrMF devices while providing more controlled (lower-variance) droplet volume and speed progression. This proof-of-concept establishes the feasibility of using the DMF section to mix and supply reagents on-demand to the DrMF section for future droplet digital nucleic acid amplification applications (e.g., droplet digital LAMP or PCR), pending the addition of a heating element and a fluorescence detection module for amplification monitoring.
