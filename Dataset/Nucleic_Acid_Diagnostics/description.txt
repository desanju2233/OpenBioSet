Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Digital Microfluidics with Distance-Based Detection (DMF-DBD) for RT-LAMP-Based Nucleic Acid Diagnostics — Detection of SARS-CoV-2 RNA in Saliva

REAGENTS USED:
  - Extracted SARS-CoV-2 RNA (viral RNA extracted from saliva; blank, low viral load ~10 PFU/mL, and high viral load ~4.6x10^4 PFU/mL samples)
  - RT-LAMP Master Mix (WarmStart colorimetric/fluorescent RT-LAMP reagents with SARS-CoV-2-specific primers, guanidine hydrochloride, and nuclease-free water)
  - Capto(TM) Adhere Agarose Bead Suspension (solid-phase clean-up beads that selectively bind low molecular-weight LAMP by-products, e.g. primer-dimers, leaving high-molecular-weight LAMP amplicons in solution)
  - SYBR(R) Safe Intercalating Dye (100x, fluorescent reporter used for distance-based detection readout)

STEPS:
1. Amplification: Load an aliquot of extracted RNA and RT-LAMP master mix onto the DMF array, merge and mix the two droplets, then heat the merged droplet at 65 C for 17 minutes (reduced-duration amplification, selected to preserve discrimination between high and low viral loads).
2. Bead Clean-Up: Load a droplet of washed Capto(TM) adhere bead suspension, merge it with the amplified LAMP product, and actively mix (rotate in a circular pattern) for approximately 1 minute to deplete low molecular-weight background species.
3. Dye Addition: Load a droplet of 100x SYBR(R) Safe solution, merge it with the bead-cleaned amplicon droplet, and mix for approximately 2 minutes to intercalate the dye into the high-molecular-weight LAMP amplicons.
4. Detection: Drive the resultant droplet to the on-chip distance-based detection (DBD) paper substrate, where it touches the loading zone and wicks by capillary action through the flowing zone; the fluorescent signal travel distance is imaged/read (by eye or camera) over several minutes and correlates with the amount of amplified target present, then the analyzed droplet is discarded.

Steps 1-4 are repeated independently for each of the three sample conditions (blank/no virus, low viral load, and high viral load saliva) to generate the semi-quantitative comparison reported in the paper.

INTERPRETATION: Successful detection is confirmed when the normalized DBD travel distance (and corresponding fluorescent signal) increases monotonically with viral load, allowing statistically significant discrimination between blank, low (~10 PFU/mL, ~4.9x10^4 copies/mL), and high (~4.6x10^4 PFU/mL, ~2.2x10^8 copies/mL) SARS-CoV-2-spiked saliva samples (two-sided t-test, p_blank-low = 0.02863, p_low-high = 0.00077) using a 17-minute reduced amplification time, all performed in a single automated DMF cartridge without any dedicated optical detection apparatus.
