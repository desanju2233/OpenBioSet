Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Picoliter-Scale Electrowetting Digital Microfluidic Pyrosequencing (Bead-Bound DNA Extension Coupled to ATP-Sulfurylase/Luciferase Chemiluminescent Detection)

REAGENTS USED:
  - DNA-Bound Paramagnetic Beads (target DNA template bound to streptavidin-functionalized paramagnetic beads, magnetically held at the reservoir exit)
  - Polymerase (DNA polymerase used to catalyze template-directed nucleotide incorporation)
  - dNTP (single deoxynucleotide triphosphate, e.g. dATP, cyclically dispensed one base at a time during sequencing-by-synthesis)
  - APS (adenosine 5'-phosphosulfate, substrate for ATP sulfurylase)
  - ATP Sulfurylase (enzyme converting released pyrophosphate + APS into ATP)
  - Luciferin (D-luciferin substrate for firefly luciferase, 400 ug/mL working concentration)
  - Luciferase (QuantiLum recombinant firefly luciferase, 1.0-2.0 ug/uL working concentration for optimal picoliter-scale signal)
  - Wash Buffer (50 mM TRIS pH 7.6, 5.0 mM magnesium acetate, 0.5 mM EDTA, 1.0 mM DTT, 0.01-0.02% wt Tween 20, used to rinse beads between cycles and prevent cross-contamination)

STEPS:
1. Bead loading: Dispense the DNA-bound paramagnetic bead droplet and a polymerase droplet, magnetically hold the beads at the reservoir exit, and mix to form the primed template droplet.
2-5. Nucleotide addition (repeated once per base in the sequencing cycle): Dispense a single dNTP droplet and mix it with the bead-bound template so that, per Equation 1 (DNA + dNTP --Polymerase--> DNA-dNP + PPi), a correct incorporation releases pyrophosphate (PPi) into the droplet.
6. ATP generation: Dispense APS and ATP sulfurylase droplets and sequentially mix them with the extension droplet so that, per Equation 2 (PPi + APS --ATP Sulfurylase--> ATP + SO4^2-), the released PPi is converted to ATP.
7. Chemiluminescent detection: Dispense luciferin and luciferase droplets and sequentially mix them with the ATP-containing droplet so that, per Equation 3 (ATP + Luciferin + O2 --Luciferase--> AMP + PPi + Oxyluciferin + CO2 + Light), light is produced in proportion to the amount of ATP (and therefore the number of bases incorporated); observe/quantify the resulting chemiluminescence intensity with a cooled CCD camera over a fixed exposure window.
8. Signal evaluation and bead rinse: If the detected intensity falls below the empirically determined limit-of-detection threshold (~7 nM ATP equivalent, S/N = 5), dispose of the reacted droplet and re-form the bead-bound template by mixing the magnetically-held beads with a fresh wash-buffer droplet before the next cycle, to eliminate dilution/cross-contamination effects; otherwise carry the reacted droplet forward as the template for the next nucleotide-addition cycle.
9. Cycle repeat: Repeat steps 2-8 for each subsequent base in the sequencing run.
10. Final rinse and disposal: After the sequencing cycles are complete, rinse the bead-bound template with successive wash-buffer droplets to remove residual reagent, then discard.

INTERPRETATION: Successful pyrosequencing chemistry on the picoliter-scale device is confirmed by a linear chemiluminescence response to ATP concentration (limit of detection ~7 nM ATP, corresponding to single-nucleotide incorporation on ~2 amol of DNA in a 300 pL droplet) and by luciferase-concentration optimization (1.0-2.0 ug/uL) yielding sufficient initial peak intensity for reliable detection despite the inherently lower light output at picoliter droplet volumes, supporting the feasibility of miniaturized digital-microfluidic pyrosequencing for low-reagent-volume, field-portable DNA sequencing.
