Source link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3613134/pdf/nihms394155.pdf
Paper DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2012.07.022

Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Picoliter-Scale Electrowetting-on-Dielectric (EWD) Droplet Dispensing, Splitting, and Bead/Chemiluminescent Reaction Manipulation

REAGENTS USED:
  - DI Water (deionized water, test droplet fluid)
  - Paramagnetic Bead Solution (Invitrogen Dynabeads M-270 2.8 um streptavidin-functionalized paramagnetic beads)
  - Luciferase Buffer (1 ug/ul luciferase reagent solution)
  - Luciferin/ATP Substrate (400 ug/ml luciferin with 0.1 uM ATP)

STEPS:
1. Dispense an 18 pl DI water droplet from the reservoir on the 33 um electrode device (5 um gasket) at 17.1 Vrms.
2. Split the dispensed droplet into two daughter droplets by simultaneously activating two adjacent electrodes, then discard both resulting droplets after confirming successful splitting.
3. Dispense a 12 pl paramagnetic bead droplet from the reservoir on the 33 um electrode device with a 3 um gasket.
4. Split the bead droplet into two daughter droplets using the reservoir-electrode/third-channel-electrode splitting protocol; discard one daughter droplet.
5. Dispense a luciferase buffer droplet and a luciferin/ATP substrate droplet.
6. Merge the remaining bead droplet with the luciferase buffer droplet, then merge the resulting droplet with the luciferin/ATP substrate droplet to form the reaction droplet.
7. Incubate the merged reaction droplet at 25C for 1 minute.
8. Detect the chemiluminescent signal produced by the ATP-luciferin reaction using a cooled CCD/EMCCD camera sensor.
9. Discard the reaction droplet.

INTERPRETATION: Successful dispensing and splitting of picoliter-scale droplets (18 pl and 12 pl on 33 um electrodes; 5 pl on 21 um electrodes) at low actuation voltages (17.1-22 Vrms dispensing, 18.7 Vrms splitting) confirms the physical scaling and low-voltage operation of the multi-layer-insulator EWD platform. Successful dispensing of the 12 pl paramagnetic bead droplet at the same voltages used for DI water confirms that bead-containing droplets can be manipulated without additional actuation penalty. The paper notes that the resulting chemiluminescent signal from the ATP-luciferin reaction at this droplet scale was too low to be detected even with a cooled EMCCD camera, so the detection step models the intended assay workflow rather than a successful quantitative measurement obtained in this study.
