Paper DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-27470-1

Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Droplet Digital Polymerase Chain Reaction (ddPCR) Using a Home-Made Pipette Droplet Microfluidics Kit

REAGENTS USED:
  - Luna Probe One-Step RT-qPCR 4X Mix
  - Human GAPDH Primers and Probes (FAM-labeled)
  - 10 w/v% F127 Aqueous Solution (co-surfactant to prevent droplet coalescence during thermal cycling)
  - Diluted Human Control RNA (target)
  - Nuclease-Free Water

STEPS:
1. Prepare a 20 uL reaction mix: 5 uL Luna Probe One-Step RT-qPCR 4X Mix, 1 uL human GAPDH primers and probes, 2 uL 10 w/v% F127 aqueous solution, 0-10 uL diluted human control RNA as target, and nuclease-free water to volume.
2. Generate uniform nanoliter droplets of the reaction mix by pipetting through a torque-deformed elliptical pipette tip into fluorinated oil (HFE 7500 with surfactant) pre-loaded in a PCR tube.
3. Place the PCR tube with droplets into a portable thermal cycler and run: 10 min reverse transcription at 52 C, 2 min initial denaturation at 95 C.
4. Perform 40 thermal cycles of 10 s at 95 C (denaturation) and 30 s at 60 C (annealing/extension).
5. Image the droplets for green (FAM) fluorescence using either a smartphone camera with a blue-light transilluminator and amber filter, or a handheld fluorescence mini microscope.
6. Count the number of positive (fluorescent) droplets to quantify the target nucleic acid, without needing the total droplet count or Poisson statistics, since at low target concentrations positive droplet count approximates target copy number.

INTERPRETATION: A clear, bright green fluorescence signal in a droplet indicates successful amplification of the GAPDH target within that droplet (a "positive" droplet); the total count of positive droplets scales linearly with the nominal target amount over the tested range (0-1 picogram per 20 uL reaction), giving a calculated limit of detection of ~3.92 copies per reaction, comparable to commercial ddPCR systems, despite counting-based quantification carrying ~20-30% coefficient of variation at low target concentrations.
