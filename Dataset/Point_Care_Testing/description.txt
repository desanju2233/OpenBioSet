Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Heterogeneous Magnetic-Bead Sandwich Immunoassay on a Digital Microfluidic (DMF) Platform, applied to Human Insulin and Interleukin-6 (IL-6) Quantification

REAGENTS USED:
  - Sample Droplet (analyte solution containing human insulin or IL-6 antigen)
  - Capture-Antibody-Conjugated Magnetic Bead Droplet (magnetic beads coated with antigen-specific capture antibody)
  - HRP-Labeled Reporter Antibody Droplet (secondary/reporter antibody labeled with horseradish peroxidase, forms the sandwich complex)
  - Wash Buffer (used for serial-dilution-based washing of the magnetic beads)
  - Chemiluminescent Substrate (for the HRP enzyme-substrate reaction used for detection)

STEPS:
1. Dispense the sample droplet, the capture-antibody magnetic bead droplet, and the HRP-labeled reporter antibody droplet from their on-chip reservoirs and merge them together at the reaction site.
2. Incubate the merged droplet, transporting it back and forth across the electrode array to resuspend and mix the magnetic beads and promote formation of the capture-antibody/antigen/reporter-antibody sandwich complex.
3. Immobilize the magnetic beads at the reaction site using a permanent magnet positioned beneath the chip.
4. With the beads held in place by the magnet, split off and remove the excess supernatant (unbound sample and unbound reporter antibody) to a waste reservoir.
5. Dispense a fresh wash-buffer droplet and merge it with the immobilized bead droplet, then split off and remove the diluted supernatant again; repeat this dilution-based wash cycle (~1:6 dilution per cycle) for 5 cycles to achieve a cumulative ~7776-fold dilution of unbound material and near-complete bead retention.
6. Release the washed magnetic-bead droplet from the magnet and transport it across five electrodes to resuspend the beads via the internal recirculating flow generated by droplet motion.
7. Dispense a chemiluminescent (HRP) substrate droplet and merge it with the resuspended, washed bead droplet to initiate the enzyme-substrate chemiluminescent reaction.
8. Transport the chemiluminescent product droplet to the detection site and record the endpoint chemiluminescence signal using a photon-counting detector; use the signal to determine antigen (insulin or IL-6) concentration from a standard curve, with a total time to result of approximately 7 minutes per assay.
