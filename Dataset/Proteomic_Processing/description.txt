```text
Source link: http://microfluidics.utoronto.ca/papers/DMF_processing.pdf
Paper DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/ac900522a

Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Digital Microfluidic (DMF) Multistep Proteomic Sample Processing (Reduction, Alkylation, Tryptic Digestion) with MALDI-MS Readout

REAGENTS USED:
  - Protein Sample (e.g., insulin, lysozyme, or BSA in working buffer)
  - TCEP (Tris(2-carboxyethyl)phosphine reductant, 10 mM in working buffer)
  - Iodoacetamide (alkylating agent, 12 mM in working buffer)
  - Trypsin (protease, in working buffer)

STEPS:
1. Reduction: Dispense the protein sample droplet and a droplet of TCEP reductant onto the array. Merge and actively mix for 60 seconds, then incubate at room temperature (23°C) for 30 minutes to reduce disulfide bonds to free thiols.
2. Alkylation: Dispense a droplet of iodoacetamide and merge with the reduced sample droplet. Mix for 60 seconds, then incubate at room temperature (23°C) for 10 minutes to covalently cap the free thiols and prevent disulfide reformation.
3. Digestion: Dispense a droplet of trypsin and merge with the alkylated sample droplet. Mix for 60 seconds, then incubate at room temperature (23°C) for 60 minutes to enzymatically digest the protein into constituent peptides.
4. Detection: Analyze the final peptide digest by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization mass spectrometry (MALDI-MS) for 1 minute to confirm reduction, alkylation, and digestion, and to identify peptides. Discard the final reaction mixture.

INTERPRETATION: Successful processing is confirmed by mass shifts in the MALDI-MS spectrum: loss of the intact disulfide-linked mass after reduction, a +57 Da shift per thiol after alkylation, and the appearance of characteristic tryptic peptide masses (searchable against a protein database, e.g., via Mascot) after digestion.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A NOTE ON HOW CLOSELY THIS MATCHES THE CITED SOURCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The authored BioScript code is a direct translation of the linear, non-branching portion of the Luk & Wheeler (2009) DMF workflow: sequential on-chip reduction, alkylation, and tryptic digestion, read out by MALDI-MS. Reagent identities (TCEP, iodoacetamide, trypsin), incubation temperature (room temperature / 23°C), and hold times (30 min reduction, 60 min digestion) are taken directly from the paper's Qualitative Analysis section describing stages ii-iv of the workup. The 10-minute alkylation hold reflects the paper's reported time-to-completion for full alkylation, rather than the shorter 2-minute intermediate timepoint the paper uses only to visualize partially-alkylated species in Figure 3c. Dispense volumes are abstracted to a generic 100-unit droplet, since BioScript's volumetric unit model does not natively express the paper's ~100 nL droplets at analyte-specific molar concentrations (e.g., 40 µM insulin, 10 mM TCEP, 12 mM iodoacetamide). Unlike the ELISA-style scripts in this test suite, this protocol has no repeat/wash cycles, which is intentional and matches the paper's central claim: DMF droplets act as isolated microreactors, so no intermediate wash or purification step is needed between reduction, alkylation, and digestion.
```
