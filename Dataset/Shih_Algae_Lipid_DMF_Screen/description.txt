PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : A Droplet-Based Screen for Wavelength-Dependent Lipid Production in Algae
Source Link  : https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Steve-Shih/publication/262874413_A_droplet-based_screen_for_wavelength-dependent_lipid_production_in_algae/links/00b7d53909cf950ec1000000/A-droplet-based-screen-for-wavelength-dependent-lipid-production-in-algae.pdf?__cf_chl_f_tk=N2GSHFBauUsIm5B8xwZxkkXn_LuBAACs9pC5GteCtAg-1782901072-1.0.1.1-e0TB_oLJRbhpUUI8lmO01R0_1wpK5AF10F3ILeZ6Fv4
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1039/c4ee01123f
Journal      : Energy & Environmental Science, 2014, DOI: 10.1039/c4ee01123f
Platform     : Digital microfluidics (DMF) on open electrode array with custom LED illumination


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes the first microfluidic method applied to discovery of algae culture
conditions favourable for generating high-energy lipids. The platform uses digital microfluidics
(DMF) — where droplets are manipulated on an open electrode array by application of electric
fields — to perform automated, multiplexed long-term culture and analysis of Cyclotella cryptica
(CCMP 332), a diatom widely used as a model organism for biofuel lipid research.

The protocol is divided into three functional phases:
  1. Multicolour illumination screen: 7 LED wavelengths + dark control at two intensities,
     screening neutral lipid production and biomass accumulation simultaneously over 24 h.
  2. Blue/yellow cycling screen: 8 timed combinations of blue (~450 nm) and yellow (~580 nm)
     illumination at high intensity, identifying the optimal condition for biofuel production.
  3. ROS mechanistic assay: DCFDA fluorescence quantification of reactive oxygen species under
     yellow, blue, and ascorbate-treated conditions to elucidate the photooxidative stress
     mechanism underlying wavelength-dependent lipid accumulation.

The method reduces manual intervention from >600 pipette steps (conventional multiwell screen)
to fewer than 20, while generating equivalent or greater data density. Two pipette steps are
sufficient to run a single device for 24 h, producing 48 measurements (24 absorbance + 24
fluorescence) across 8 culture conditions at 3 time points.


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Open-array DMF device; no pumps, valves, or mechanical mixers required.

Electrode layout (per device):
  - 2 four-electrode active reservoirs (algae suspension + LipidTOX dye)
  - 8 algae culture zone electrodes
  - 8 bussed dye dispensing electrodes
  - 8 absorbance detection electrodes (L-shaped, for daughter droplet routing)
  - 8 fluorescence detection electrodes
  - 8 waste reservoir electrodes with 1 mm-diameter through-holes

Dispensing: All dispenser electrode modules feature narrow central electrodes
  (>= 0.5:1 ratio relative to destination electrode) to facilitate necking during
  droplet formation; critical for reliable parallel dispensing on bussed electrodes.

Detection zones: 9 mm pitch; compatible with standard 384-well multiwell plate reader.
  Transparent windows for transmission (absorbance) and opaque-bottom wells for fluorescence.

Waste management: Spent reagents driven to waste electrodes; fluid wicks through 1 mm
  through-holes onto absorbent tissue below device after each measurement cycle.

Droplet volumes: 150 uL to 2 uL (wide range controlled by varying electrode sizes/shapes
  and bussed groupings).

Automation: Impedance sensing with feedback control over droplet position; programmed
  actuation sequences for culture, dispensing, detection, and waste routing.

Illumination: Custom LED arrays positioned below device; multicolour array (7 peak
  wavelengths: red ~625 nm, orange ~590 nm, yellow ~580 nm, green ~525 nm, blue ~450 nm,
  purple ~400 nm, white broadband) and blue/yellow dual-LED arrays for cycling experiments.
  Intensities: 50 and 250 umol/m2/s (low and high).


ORGANISM AND REAGENTS
---------------------
Organism    : Cyclotella cryptica (CCMP 332); purchased from National Center for Marine
              Algae and Microbiota (NCMA, East Boothbay, ME).
Culture     : f/2 medium (NCMA, Maine, NE) supplemented with biotin and cyanocobalamin
              (2 nM each, NCMA) and 0.02% w/v Pluronic F68; Pyrex media bottles (max 200 mL);
              20 C; 12 h white light / 12 h dark alternating cycle; weekly subculture 1:10.
              Inoculation density ~9.0e5 cells/mL (exponential phase).
On-chip     : 1.5e6 cells/mL in complete media; 150 uL loaded into algae active reservoir.
              LipidTOX working solution: 200x dilution in complete media; 120 uL loaded.

LipidTOX    : HCS LipidTOX Green neutral lipid stain (Life Technologies, Burlington, ON,
              Canada); fluorogenic reporter fluorescing in presence of neutral lipids (TAG);
              ex 488 nm / em 520 nm.

DCFDA       : DCFDA cellular reactive oxygen species detection assay kit (ab113851, Abcam,
              Cambridge, MA); reporter fluorescing in presence of ROS; ex 490 nm / em 526 nm.

Ascorbic    : 50 ug/mL ascorbic acid in complete media; antioxidant for ROS rescue arm.

PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Device Sterilisation and Algae Preparation (off-chip)
  Sterilise   : DMF devices incubated in 70% ethanol for 10 min prior to use.
  Algae prep  : Maintained by weekly subculture at 1:10 ratio; density confirmed by
                absorbance at 660 nm in 200 uL aliquots in clear-bottom 96-well plates
                using a plate reader. Exponential growth phase confirmed before loading.
  Absorbance  : 1 mL media aliquot (no cells) as reference; 1 mL culture aliquot measured
  spectrum    : by UV/Vis spectrophotometer (Eppendorf, Westbury, NY). C. cryptica
                absorbance maxima at blue (strong) and red (weak) wavelengths, consistent
                with primary photosynthetic pigment chlorophyll c.

Step 2 | Device Loading — Active Reservoir Initialisation
  Inputs      : Algae suspension (150 uL, 1.5e6 cells/mL) and LipidTOX solution (120 uL,
                200x stock dilution in complete media) pipetted into their respective
                active reservoirs.
  Pipette steps: 2 (only manual steps required for entire 24 h run).
  Circulation : Each active reservoir continuously circulates on its four electrodes in a
                circular mixing pattern to prevent cell settling and maintain homogeneous
                density before each dispense step. In BioScript, abstracted as a mix loop
                on the reservoir droplets prior to dispensing.

Step 3 | Culture Droplet Dispensing (Screening Step 2)
  Operation   : 8 culture droplets (12 uL each) dispensed from algae active reservoir to
                8 algae culture zone electrodes in series via bussed electrode actuation.
  Notes       : Representative single droplet lineage encoded in BioScript; hardware
                bussing handles 8 simultaneous culture droplets per device.

Step 4 | Label Droplet Dispensing (Screening Step 3)
  Operation   : ~15 uL source droplet dispensed from LipidTOX active reservoir to dye
                dispensing bus; 8 label droplets (2 uL each) formed simultaneously.
                If source volume insufficient, second source droplet dispensed from reservoir.

Step 5 | Absorbance Measurement — Cell Density (Screening Step 4)
  Operation   : 8 daughter culture droplets (2 uL each) dispensed from culture zones and
                actuated to L-shaped absorbance detection electrodes.
  Detection   : Device placed on transparent 384-well plate; PHERAstar plate reader
                (BMG Labtech, Durham, NC); 660 nm; scan matrix 15x15; scan width 3 mm;
                focal height 8.7 mm. Records cell density at each time point.
  Time points : t = 0 h (baseline), t = 6 h, t = 24 h.

Step 6 | Fluorescence Measurement — Neutral Lipid (Screening Step 5)
  Operation   : 8 daughter culture droplets merged 1:1 with 8 label droplets; incubated
                5 min for LipidTOX staining; mixed droplets driven to fluorescence
                detection electrodes.
  Detection   : Device placed on black 384-well plate; PHERAstar plate reader; 488 nm
                excitation / 520 nm emission; scan matrix 20x20; scan width 3 mm;
                focal height 8.7 mm; gain 550. Records neutral lipid content per droplet.
  Waste       : Mixed droplets driven to waste reservoirs after measurement; fluid wicks
                through 1 mm through-holes onto tissue below device.
  Time points : t = 0 h, t = 6 h, t = 24 h.
  Notes       : Screening Steps 3-5 (Steps 4-6 here) repeated after each illumination
                incubation phase (after 6 h and after 18 h additional incubation).

Step 7 | Multicolour Illumination Screen (Protocol 1)
  Conditions  : 7 LED wavelengths (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, white) +
                dark control; low (50 umol/m2/s) and high (250 umol/m2/s) intensity.
                All LEDs on continuously for 24 h; n=3 devices per condition.
  Schedule    : Load device (Step 2) → dispense and measure t=0 (Steps 3-6) →
                6 h illumination → repeat Steps 3-6 (t=6h) →
                18 h illumination → repeat Steps 3-6 (t=24h).
  Key finding : Yellow LEDs (~580 nm) produce the highest neutral lipid accumulation
                per cell (>2x vs. dark control) at high intensity (Fig. 3d/f).
                Blue LEDs (~450 nm) produce the highest biomass growth rate (Fig. 3b).
                First report of culture wavelength dependence of algal neutral lipid
                production; mechanism correlates with oxidative stress (see Step 9).
  QC gate     : absorbSignal1 > 0 → fluorescence measurement (BB2/pass);
                absorbSignal1 <= 0 → drain culture droplet (BB3/fail).

Step 8 | Blue/Yellow Cycling Screen (Protocol 2)
  Conditions  : 8 timed combinations of blue (~450 nm) and yellow (~580 nm) LEDs at
                high intensity (250 umol/m2/s), 24 h total; n=3 devices per condition.
                Combinations (h blue / h yellow): 24/0, 0/24, 1/23, 3/21, 6/18,
                12/12, 15/9, 20/4.
  Schedule    : Blue phase (900m = 15h) → measure t=15h → yellow phase (540m = 9h)
                → measure t=24h. Baseline t=0h measured before illumination.
  Optimal     : 15h blue / 9h yellow → ~4-fold lipid increase vs. dark control;
  condition   : ~2-fold vs. yellow or blue alone (Fig. 4b). Growth rates remain high
                (above any single colour other than blue) due to blue phase proliferation.
  Macroscale  : Best condition (15h blue / 9h yellow), 24h yellow, and 24h blue validated
  validation  : in 200 mL Pyrex flasks (n=5). Trends identical to DMF microscale (Fig. 4c/d).
                Confirms microfluidic screen hits translate to conventional-scale cultures.
  QC gate     : absorbSignal2 > 0 → fluorescence measurement (BB5/pass);
                absorbSignal2 <= 0 → drain culture droplet (BB6/fail).

Step 9 | ROS Mechanistic Assay (Protocol 3)
  Purpose     : Determine whether wavelength-dependent lipid accumulation correlates with
                reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, consistent with a photooxidative
                stress mechanism (analogous to nutrient-deprivation stress).
  Conditions  :
    Arm A     : Yellow illumination 24h — expected high ROS (positive arm).
    Arm B     : Blue illumination 24h — expected low ROS (negative control).
    Arm C     : Yellow illumination 24h + 50 ug/mL ascorbic acid — antioxidant rescue;
                expected ROS reversal to near-blue levels.
    Arm D     : Silicon deprivation (off-chip, macroscale positive control; known ROS
                inducer in C. cryptica via silicon-deficiency stress).
  DCFDA       : Algae grown at 5.0e6 cells/mL in 200 mL Pyrex flasks for >=1.5 weeks
  protocol    : under standard conditions (12h light/dark). Centrifuged 3000 rpm, 5 min;
                resuspended in 50 mL appropriate media variant. Illuminated 24h under
                assigned condition. Cells evaluated with DCFDA kit per manufacturer
                instructions: 5 mL aliquots centrifuged, washed 1x in buffer, resuspended
                in 500 uL DCFDA solution or complete media (control); incubated 37 C 30 min
                dark; centrifuged, washed, resuspended in 1x buffer. 150 uL aliquots placed
                in black-bottom 96-well plates; read at ex 490 nm / em 526 nm; n=5 per arm.
  Result      : Yellow light and silicon deprivation produce significantly higher ROS than
                blue light or normal conditions; ascorbate reverses the yellow-light ROS
                elevation (Fig. 5c). Supports hypothesis that yellow light causes
                photooxidative stress — when photosynthesis cannot keep pace with photon
                absorption, excited chlorophyll states (1Chl* and 3Chl*) form singlet-state
                oxygen (1O2*) and other ROS. Blue-light-specific antioxidant synthesis
                (xanthophyll cycle genes ZEP, VDE) may explain the wavelength specificity.
  QC gate     : rosSignalYellow > 0 → dispose all ROS arm droplets (BB8/pass);
                rosSignalYellow <= 0 → drain all ROS arm droplets (BB9/fail).
