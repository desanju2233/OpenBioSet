Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Digital Microfluidic Colorimetric Detection of Nitroaromatic Explosive Particles (TNT/DNT) via Reaction with KOH

REAGENTS USED:
  - TNT Sample (commercial grade 2,4,6-trinitrotoluene, or pure 2,4-dinitrotoluene, dispensed by pipette onto the electrowetting chip)
  - KOH Reagent (potassium hydroxide solution that reacts with nitroaromatic explosives to form colored complexes)

STEPS:
1. Dispensing: Manually pipette a droplet of TNT (or DNT) sample and a droplet of KOH reagent onto the electrowetting chip.
2. Merging: Merge the two droplets by applying voltages to the appropriate electrodes.
3. Mixing: Electrowetting-enabled mixing -- transport/shuttle the merged droplet across electrodes to enhance mixing flow, forming the colored reaction complex.
4. Detection: At the completion of mixing, measure the absorbance of the colored complex at room temperature using an on-chip LED (with characteristic absorbance peaks reported at 336 nm and 510 nm for TNT-KOH and 407 nm and 657 nm for DNT-KOH complexes).
5. Disposal: Discard the analyzed droplet after the absorbance reading is taken.

INTERPRETATION: Successful colorimetric explosive detection is confirmed by a linear calibration curve of absorbance versus TNT concentration (y = 0.0056x + 0.0145, R^2 = 0.9863) over the 4-20 ug/mL range, yielding a calculated detection limit of approximately 2.6 ug/mL -- competitive with antibody-based surface plasmon resonance competition assays (7.8 ug/mL). Because the TNT and DNT colored complexes produce mutually independent absorbance peaks, TNT can be quantitatively estimated even in the presence of DNT, a combination common in contaminated soil, supporting on-site, automated colorimetric detection of land-mine and munitions-related nitroaromatic contamination without requiring an experienced chemical analyst.
