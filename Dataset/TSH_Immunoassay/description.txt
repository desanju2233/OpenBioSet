Protocol details:

ASSAY TYPE: Automated Digital Microfluidic Magnetic-Particle-Based Chemiluminescent Immunoassay for Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH), DOE-Optimized Protocol

REAGENTS USED:
  - Antibody-Coated Paramagnetic Microparticles (Abbott Laboratories; ~3.0 x 10^8 particles/mL in DMF-compatible particle diluent, pH 10, 5 um diameter, magnetic susceptibility 1.05)
  - TSH Standards (0, 0.4, and 4 uIU/mL, supplemented with 0.05% v/v Pluronic L64)
  - DMF-Compatible Wash Buffer (custom-prepared, used for post-capture and post-conjugate particle washing)
  - HRP-Conjugated Anti-TSH Detection Antibody (mouse monoclonal, 2 ug/mL working solution in conjugate diluent)
  - Hydrogen Peroxide / Stable Peroxide Substrate Component (SuperSignal ELISA Femto, supplemented with 0.025% v/v Pluronic L64)
  - Luminol-Enhancer Solution (SuperSignal ELISA Femto chemiluminescent substrate, second component)

STEPS (Optimized Immunoassay protocol, as validated against the pilot/DOE-derived parameters):
1. Particle Capture: Dispense antibody-coated paramagnetic particles from a reservoir and separate from the particle diluent using the magnetic lens; deliver five pooled sample droplets (~4 uL) of TSH standard to the immobilized particles and mix for 3 min (the DOE-optimized incubation time).
2. First Wash: Wash the particles four times in wash buffer, separating particles from the supernatant after each wash using the magnetic lens.
3. Conjugate Binding: Dispense HRP-conjugated anti-TSH detection antibody, deliver to the immobilized particles, and mix for 2 min.
4. Second Wash: Wash the particles four times in wash buffer, separating from the supernatant; unlike the pilot protocol, the chemiluminescent reaction is initiated immediately after the final wash rather than queuing particles in wash buffer, to avoid signal loss from unequal wait times.
5. Chemiluminescent Reaction: Resuspend the washed particles in one droplet of H2O2 substrate component, merge with one droplet of luminol-enhancer solution, and mix for 4 min until the chemiluminescent signal saturates.
6. Detection: Using the integrated, motorized photomultiplier tube (PMT), collect the chemiluminescent signal ~10 s after the substrate mixing step, for each of the four replicate measurements per concentration.
7. Endpoint: Average the four replicate measurements at each TSH concentration and fit to a linear equation; the limit of detection (LOD) is defined as the concentration corresponding to the blank (0 uIU/mL) signal plus 3 standard deviations of the blank measurements. Dispose of the reacted droplet after measurement.

INTERPRETATION: A successful, correctly optimized assay is confirmed by a chemiluminescent signal that increases monotonically with TSH concentration (0, 0.4, 4 uIU/mL) with low inter-replicate variability (CV 6-10%), yielding a linear calibration curve with an LOD of ~0.15 uIU/mL -- sufficiently low to screen for hyperthyroidism (TSH < 0.3 uIU/mL) -- and reduced sample incubation time (3 min) relative to the non-optimized prototype protocol (6 min), demonstrating that the DOE-derived parameters (five sample droplets, 3 min capture incubation) improve both sensitivity and speed relative to previous non-automated work.

A NOTE ON HOW CLOSELY THIS MATCHES THE CITED SOURCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The authored BioScript code is a direct translation of the paper's "Optimized Immunoassays" procedure (Experimental Section, p. 9641), which itself modifies the seven-step pilot protocol described immediately prior in the same section. It captures the particle-capture incubation (3 min, five pooled droplets), the two four-cycle wash stages bracketing conjugate addition, the HRP-conjugate incubation (2 min), the H2O2/luminol-enhancer chemiluminescent reaction (4 min mix), and the ~10 s PMT signal-collection window, using the optimized (not pilot) timing values throughout. Because BioScript's grammar has no primitive for magnetic separation, particle pelleting/supernatant removal is folded implicitly into each wash-loop's dispense-mix-dispose cycle (mirroring the wash-loop pattern used in the ciprofloxacin reference script) rather than being modeled as a distinct operation. Because BioScript does not support explicit droplet-count or merged-volume parameters, the paper's "five pooled unit droplets (~4 uL)" sample delivery is represented as a single nominal "dispense 20 units of tsh_standard" rather than five discrete droplet-merge operations; the "20 units" figure is an arbitrary placeholder volume, not a value sourced from the paper. The two four-times wash loops are modeled with BioScript's repeat construct rather than as four explicit unrolled dispense/mix/dispose steps. A "heat f at 25c for 10s" instruction was added immediately before detection as a scripting convenience to explicitly stage the substrate at a nominal bench-top temperature prior to signal collection; this step is a modeling addition and is not described in the paper, which specifies only mixing followed directly by PMT measurement with no explicit thermal-control step at this point. The detect operation is declared using BioScript's only available detection primitive, "fluorescence," even though the physical readout in the paper is a chemiluminescent (non-excitation-based) signal, since the compiler/grammar exposes no distinct "chemiluminescence" detect module. The paper's 3^3 full-factorial DOE analysis (varying analyte concentration, incubation time, and sample volume across 108 discrete experiments), the coefficient/ANOVA modeling used to derive the optimized parameters, the magnetic-lens finite-element force modeling, the integrated-PMT-vs-well-plate-reader HRP calibration comparison, and the pilot (non-optimized) protocol's queuing artifact are physically distinct sub-analyses/validation experiments used to justify and derive the optimized protocol rather than part of the optimized assay execution itself, and are intentionally not modeled; only the final validated Optimized Immunoassay procedure (Table 1) is captured here.
