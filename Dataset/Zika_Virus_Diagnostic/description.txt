Protocol Details
ASSAY TYPE

Portable Sample Processing for Molecular Assays: Application to Zika Virus Diagnostics (Integrated Extraction, NASBA Amplification, and Cell-Free Toehold Switch Detection on Digital Microfluidics)

REAGENTS USED
Plasma Sample (human blood plasma containing Zika virus RNA)
Lysis Buffer
Proteinase K
Carrier RNA
Magnetic Beads (silica-coated paramagnetic particles for nucleic acid capture)
Wash Buffer
Ethanol Wash Buffer
Elution Water
NASBA Master Mix
NASBA Enzymes
Cell-Free (CF) Master Mix (cell-free protein expression system components)
Toehold Switch DNA (synthetic riboregulator circuit for Zika RNA detection)
STEPS
Lysis & Digestion: Mix the plasma sample with lysis buffer, heat at 95°C for 2 minutes, add Proteinase K and mix for 30 seconds, add carrier RNA and mix for 30 seconds, then incubate at room temperature for 15 minutes.
Nucleic Acid Capture: Dispense and mix magnetic beads with the lysed sample for 5 minutes to bind viral RNA.
Primary Washes: Perform 4 successive wash cycles using wash buffer, mixing for 1 minute per wash followed by supernatant disposal.
Secondary Washes: Perform 2 successive wash cycles using ethanol wash buffer, mixing for 1 minute per wash followed by disposal.
Elution: Dispense elution water and mix with the captured beads for 3 minutes to recover purified RNA.
NASBA Amplification: Mix the RNA eluate with NASBA master mix, heat at 65°C for 2 minutes, cool to 41°C for 10 minutes, add NASBA enzymes, and incubate at 41°C for 90 minutes.
Cell-Free Detection: Mix cell-free master mix with toehold switch DNA for 30 seconds, combine with the NASBA amplification product, incubate at 37°C for 175 minutes, and detect fluorescence/colorimetric signal for 5 minutes.
Endpoint: Evaluate the Zika detection result against a threshold (≤ 50) to selectively dispose of the reaction mixture.
INTERPRETATION

Successful Zika virus detection is confirmed by robust target amplification through NASBA, followed by cell-free toehold switch expression of a reporter protein, producing a measurable fluorescent or colorimetric signal that indicates the presence of viral RNA in the plasma sample.
