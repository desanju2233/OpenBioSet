PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Sensitive Detection and Quantification of SARS-CoV-2 in Saliva
Source Link  : https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-91835-7
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-91835-7
Journal      : Scientific Reports, 2021, Vol. 11, Article 12425
Platform     : Saliva-based nucleic acid testing pipeline; BSL3 TRIzol RNA extraction,
               BSL1 RT-PCR (CFX384, Bio-Rad) and ddPCR (QX200, Bio-Rad) analysis
Institution  : University of Chicago Medical Center (IRB 20-0520)


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a comprehensive saliva-based SARS-CoV-2 nucleic acid testing pipeline
developed and validated at the University of Chicago Medical Center. It provides a complete
alternative to nasal or nasopharyngeal swab testing using two parallel molecular readouts:
real-time reverse transcription PCR (RT-PCR) and droplet digital PCR (ddPCR), both targeting
the nucleocapsid N1 and N2 genes and the RnaseP internal control.

Specimens were prospectively collected from 137 curbside outpatients (92 symptomatic, 45
asymptomatic pre-operative) and 29 COVID-19 inpatients, with paired nasal swab (NS) results
from the CLIA-certified clinical microbiology laboratory (CML) using the FDA-EUA Cepheid
Xpert Xpress system as the comparator gold standard.

The protocol is divided into three sequential phases:
  1. TRIzol-based RNA extraction (TRE method) from saliva specimens in a BSL3 facility
  2. RT-PCR assay (relative quantification; positive calling Ct < 40 for N1 or N2)
  3. ddPCR assay (absolute quantification; empirical threshold from 45 pre-op negatives)

Key outcomes:
  - RT-PCR saliva: 100% concordance with CML-positive cases (curbside and inpatient cohorts)
  - ddPCR saliva: 93.75% concordance (curbside); potential for higher sensitivity at low
    viral loads vs nasal swabs and RT-PCR (15 additional positives vs CML NS assay)
  - Viral load range: 10^6-fold across patients; saliva shows more uniform distribution
    than nasal swabs (less operator variability in volume collection)


COHORTS
-------
Curbside outpatients (N = 137):
  - Symptomatic (COVID-19 testing): N = 92
  - Asymptomatic (pre-operative screening, used as ddPCR threshold derivation cohort): N = 45
  - All SARS-CoV-2 negative by CML nasal swab (pre-op cohort)

Inpatients (N = 29):
  - All confirmed SARS-CoV-2 positive on hospital admission by CML
  - NS and saliva collected several days after admission (post-diagnosis)
  - CML identified 14/29 NS samples as positive at research collection timepoint


SPECIMEN COLLECTION
-------------------
Saliva:
  - Self-collection by drool technique (spitting into sterile tube to the 2 mL line)
  - 2 mL Zymo DNA/RNA Shield (ZymoR1100-50) added immediately at point of collection
  - Transported to BSL3 facility for RNA extraction

Nasal Swab (NS):
  - Anterior nares nasal swab collected by research team into 1 mL Amies transport solution
    (Thermo Scientific)
  - One NS sent to CML for standard-of-care analysis using FDA-EUA Cepheid Xpert Xpress
    (targets: N2 and envelope E gene; positive if Ct < 45 for either)
  - Second NS sent to BSL3 for research RT-PCR and ddPCR analysis alongside saliva


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Saliva Specimen Receipt
  Input      : SalivaSpecimen (1 mL pre-stabilised aliquot)
  Operation  : dispense
  Notes      : 1 mL aliquot of Zymo-stabilised saliva enters the TRIzol extraction
               protocol. BioScript `dispense` abstracts receipt into the workflow.

Step 2 | TRIzol LS Addition
  Reagents   : TRIzol LS (Thermo Fisher Scientific, #10296010), 3 mL
  Operation  : mix (30 s)
  Volumes    : 1 mL sample + 3 mL TRIzol LS
  Notes      : Brief vortexing to homogenise. Guanidinium thiocyanate in TRIzol
               denatures proteins and protects RNA from RNases during extraction.

Step 3 | Chloroform Addition and Phase Emulsification
  Reagents   : Chloroform, 0.6 mL
  Operation  : mix (15 s)
  Notes      : 0.6 mL chloroform added to TRIzol lysate; brief vortexing creates
               water-in-organic emulsion. Separates RNA (aqueous upper phase) from
               proteins and DNA (organic phase and interphase).

Step 4 | Phase Separation Centrifugation
  Condition  : 12,000 x g, 4 C, 15 min
  Operation  : heat at 4c for 15m (BioScript abstraction for centrifugation)
  IR output  : heat(phaseMixture) @ 4.0 CELSIUS @ 900.0 SECOND
  Phases     : (1) Colourless upper aqueous phase (RNA)
               (2) White interphase (DNA/protein)
               (3) Red-pink organic phase (protein/lipid)
  Notes      : BioScript has no centrifugation primitive. `heat at 4c` models the
               cold centrifuge condition, consistent with the samlali and moazami
               reference .bs files' use of `heat` for non-heating physical operations.

Step 5 | Aqueous Phase Transfer and Precipitation Setup
  Reagents   : Isopropanol (1 mL), Glycogen (1 uL)
  Operation  : mix x2 (10 s each)
  Volumes    : 0.8 mL upper aqueous phase + 1 mL isopropanol + 1 uL glycogen
  Notes      : Aqueous phase carefully transferred to new tube (interphase avoided).
               Glycogen acts as co-precipitant to improve RNA pellet visibility and
               yield at low concentrations typical of saliva samples.

Step 6 | Overnight RNA Precipitation
  Condition  : -20 C overnight (minimum 12 h)
  Operation  : heat at 4c for 720m (BioScript abstraction; IR: 43200.0 SECOND)
  Notes      : Paper states "-20 C overnight or room temperature for 10 min."
               Cold overnight path encoded for maximum yield. Negative temperatures
               not tested in lilott8/BioScript compiler; 4 C used as functional
               cold abstraction. The compiler correctly emits 43200.0 SECOND (12 h).

Step 7 | RNA Pellet Collection Centrifugation
  Condition  : 12,000 x g, 4 C, 10 min
  Operation  : heat at 4c for 10m (IR: 600.0 SECOND)
  Notes      : RNA pellet collected; supernatant discarded.

Step 8 | 75% Ethanol Wash
  Reagents   : 75% ethanol, 2 mL
  Operation  : mix (30 s)
  Notes      : Washes residual isopropanol and salts from pellet. Salivary fluids
               are known PCR inhibitors (Ochert et al., PCR Methods Appl. 1994,
               cited as ref. 14 in paper); ethanol wash removes inhibitory substances.

Step 9 | Ethanol Wash Centrifugation
  Condition  : 7,500 g, 4 C, 5 min
  Operation  : heat at 4c for 5m (IR: 300.0 SECOND)
  Notes      : Re-pellets RNA; ethanol supernatant discarded.

Step 10 | Ethanol Evaporation
  Condition  : Room temperature (25 C), 5-10 min
  Operation  : heat at 25c for 10m (IR: 600.0 SECOND)
  Notes      : Upper bound (10 min) encoded. Over-drying reduces RNA solubility;
               under-drying causes ethanol carry-over PCR inhibition.

Step 11 | RNA Resuspension and QC
  Reagents   : RNase-free water (50 uL), Qubit RNA HS Kit
  Operation  : mix (2 min) + detect (30 s)
  Storage    : -80 C
  QC module  : ddPCR_RnaseP_Detection (proxy for Qubit fluorometric measurement)
  Notes      : Final RNA extract resuspended in 50 uL RNase-free water. Qubit RNA
               HS Kit used to assess quantity and quality before PCR. rnaQcMix
               disposed after QC signal detected; rnaExtract used in steps 12-18.
               TRE method relative efficiency vs QVK (QIAmp Viral RNA Mini Kit): 17%.
               High assay sensitivity (LOD: 1 copy/reaction) compensates for lower yield.

Step 12 | RT-PCR Reaction Assembly (N1, N2, RnaseP channels)
  Reagents   : TaqPath One-Step RT-PCR Master Mix (ThermoFisher), N1/N2/RnaseP probe/
               primer sets (IDT #10006770), Precision Blue Real-Time PCR Dye (Bio-Rad)
  Operation  : mix (sequential; 3 parallel channels)
  Reaction   : 20 uL total per channel
               5 uL TaqPath Master Mix
               2 uL probe/primer set (N1, N2, or RnaseP)
               0.1 uL Precision Blue Real-Time PCR Dye
               6.4 uL RNA extract
               6.5 uL PCR-grade water
  Controls   : Serial dilution of SARS-CoV-2 RNA Standard (Exact Diagnostics #COV019)
               run alongside each RT-PCR plate. DNA positive control: IDT #10006625.
               Negative control: Millipore nuclease-free water (extraction-processed).
  Notes      : Three parallel reactions targeting N1, N2, and RnaseP assembled from
               the same RNA extract. BioScript `dispense SalivaSpecimen` (x3) models
               aliquoting identical RNA into three separate reaction tubes.
               Assembly order: masterMix + primerSet (5s) -> add RT dye (5s)
               -> add RNA aliquot (10s).

Step 13 | RT-PCR Thermocycling
  Instrument : CFX384 Touch Real-time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad)
  Software   : CFX Maestro
  Protocol   : CDC 2019-nCoV RT-PCR diagnostic panel (Revision 3, 2020)
  Stages     :
               Stage 1: Reverse transcription    25 C / 2 min   (IR: 120.0 s)
               Stage 2: RT incubation            50 C / 15 min  (IR: 900.0 s)
               Stage 3: Polymerase activation    95 C / 2 min   (IR: 120.0 s)
               Stage 4: 45 cycles (compressed)   55 C / 45 min  (IR: 2700.0 s)
                 [actual cycle: 95 C / 3 s denaturation + 55 C / 30 s anneal/extension]
  Performance:
               Efficiency: >99.25% for N1 and N2 (Supplementary Fig. 1)
               LOD: 1 copy/reaction (Ct ~38.2 for N1, ~38.6 for N2)
  Notes      : 45-cycle PCR amplification compressed into a single `heat at 55c for 45m`
               block (2700 s in IR). BioScript has no cycle-loop primitive; each isothermal
               stage is emitted as a discrete heat instruction in the IR. This is the same
               thermocycler abstraction used in the moazami reference .bs file (heat-shock
               stages encoded as separate heat calls).

Step 14 | RT-PCR Fluorescence Readout and Positive Calling
  Modules    : rtPCR_N1_Detection, rtPCR_N2_Detection, rtPCR_RnaseP_Detection
  Duration   : 30 s per channel
  Channels   : FAM (N1, N2 TaqMan probes); RnaseP (internal extraction control)
  Rule       : Positive if Ct < 40 for N1 OR N2 (CDC recommendation)
               Ct = 45 assigned to undetermined (no amplification) reactions
  Results    :
               Curbside: 16/16 CML-positive samples detected (100%); +7 additional
               positives vs CML NS assay (Fig. 2c,d)
               Inpatients: 14/14 CML positives detected (100%); +9 additional (Fig. 5)
  CFG gate   :
               Pass (BB2, bsbbif_2_t): rtPCR_N1_Signal > 0
                 -> dispose(rtPCR_rxn_N1, N2, RnaseP)
               Fail (BB3, bsbbif_3_f): rtPCR_N1_Signal <= 0
                 -> drain(rtPCR_rxn_N1, N2, RnaseP)
               Join (BB4, bsbbif_4_j): dispose RT-PCR signals; enter ddPCR branch
  Notes      : N1 used as primary gate (stronger LOD). BioScript does not support compound
               OR conditionals in a single if statement. RnaseP confirms extraction quality
               but is not part of the positive/negative QC gate.

Step 15 | ddPCR Reaction Assembly (N1, N2, RnaseP channels)
  Reagents   : One-step ddPCR Supermix (Bio-Rad), Reverse Transcriptase + DTT (Bio-Rad),
               N1/N2/RnaseP probe/primer sets (IDT #10006770, same as RT-PCR)
  Operation  : mix (sequential; 3 parallel channels)
  Reaction   : 20 uL total per channel
               5 uL One-step ddPCR Supermix
               2 uL reverse transcriptase
               1 uL 300 mM DTT
               2 uL probe/primer set (N1, N2, or RnaseP)
               6.4 uL RNA extract
  Notes      : Identical IDT probe/primer sets used for both RT-PCR and ddPCR (same primers
               and probes combination per paper Methods). Assembly order: supermix + RT/DTT
               (5s) -> add primer set (5s) -> add RNA aliquot (10s).

Step 16 | Droplet Generation
  Equipment  : Bio-Rad QX200 droplet generator (manual or automated)
  Oil        : Bio-Rad ddPCR droplet generation oil for probes (#1863005)
  Output     : ~20,000 nanoliter-sized water-in-oil droplets per reaction
  Operation  : mix with DropletGeneratorOil for 2m (abstracts emulsification)
  Notes      : Each droplet contains zero or one target RNA molecule, enabling absolute
               digital quantification without a standard curve (Hindson et al., Nat.
               Methods 2013, ref. 19 in paper). After generation, emulsion transferred to
               sealed 96-well plate for thermocycling.

Step 17 | ddPCR Thermocycling
  Instrument : C-1000 Thermal Cycler (Bio-Rad)
  Ramping    : 2.5 C/s between all stages
  Stages     :
               Stage 1: Initial hold                25 C / 2 min    (IR: 120.0 s)
               Stage 2: Reverse transcription        50 C / 60 min   (IR: 3600.0 s)
               Stage 3: Polymerase activation        95 C / 10 min   (IR: 600.0 s)
               Stage 4: 45 cycles (compressed)       55 C / 45 min   (IR: 2700.0 s)
                 [actual cycle: 95 C / 30 s denaturation + 55 C / 60 s anneal/extension]
               Stage 5: Signal stabilisation         98 C / 10 min   (IR: 600.0 s)
               Stage 6: Hold                          4 C / 60 min   (IR: 3600.0 s)
  Performance:
               LOD (N1): 0.06 CN/uL (Supplementary Fig. 2)
               LOD (N2): 0.21 CN/uL
               Dilution LOD: N1 detected at 1:1,000,000 dilution in pool of negatives
               (10x more sensitive than RT-PCR at 1:100,000; Supplementary Table 1)
               Inhibitor resistance: ddPCR concentrates targets per droplet, reducing
               bias from salivary PCR inhibitors (refs 20-22)
  Notes      : 4 C hold approximated as 60 m (3600 s in IR); paper allows overnight hold.
               45-cycle PCR compressed to single heat block (2700 s). Ramping speed 2.5 C/s
               maintained between all stages per paper Methods.

Step 18 | Droplet Reading and Positive Calling
  Equipment  : Bio-Rad QX200 droplet reader
  Software   : Quantasoft (Bio-Rad); raw fluorescence amplitude -> CN/uL + droplet count
  Modules    : ddPCR_N1_Detection, ddPCR_N2_Detection, ddPCR_RnaseP_Detection
  Duration   : 30 s per channel
  Rule (NS)  : CN/uL > 0.1 AND > 2 positive droplets for N1 OR N2 (Bio-Rad recommendation)
  Rule (saliva): BOTH CN/uL AND positive droplet count must exceed empirical threshold
               (mean + 2*SD from 45 pre-operative SARS-CoV-2 negative individuals;
               Supplementary Table 2; data distribution in Supplementary Fig. 7)
  Results    :
               Curbside: 15/16 CML-positive samples detected (93.75%); 31/92 total
               positives vs 16/92 by CML NS assay (Fig. 2e,f)
               Inpatients: 11/14 CML positives; 17 total positives (Fig. 5)
               Reproducibility: 91.3% concordance on re-test of 46 saliva specimens
               (Supplementary Fig. 8); 4 discordant samples attributed to RNA
               degradation after ~5 months storage
  CFG gate   :
               Pass (BB5, bsbbif_5_t): ddPCR_N1_Signal > 0
                 -> dispose(ddEmulsion_N1, N2, RnaseP)
               Fail (BB6, bsbbif_6_f): ddPCR_N1_Signal <= 0
                 -> drain(ddEmulsion_N1, N2, RnaseP)
               Terminal (BB7, bsbbif_7_j): dispose(ddPCR signals, rnaQcSignal, rnaExtract)
                 -> NOP (compiler standard exit node)
  Notes      : N1 used as primary gate (LOD 0.06 CN/uL vs N2 0.21 CN/uL).
               Saliva shows more uniform viral load distribution vs nasal swabs (less
               operator variability in collection volume). Supercarriers (>10,000 CN/mL)
               and their link to superspreading identified as key clinical application
               (refs 32, 33 in paper; Fig. 4).
