PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Ultrasensitive Digital Quantification of Cytokines and Bacteria Predicts Septic Shock Outcomes
Source Link  : https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-16124-9
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-16124-9
Journal      : Nature Communications, 2020, Vol. 11, Article 2607
Platform     : Digital proximity ligation assay (dPLA) + droplet digital PCR (ddPCR) on Bio-Rad QX200


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a combined dPLA and ddPCR pipeline for simultaneous femtomolar-sensitivity
quantification of host cytokines (IL-6 and TNF-alpha) and bacterial DNA biomarkers (gram-negative
(GN) 16S rRNA gene, gram-positive (GP) 16S rRNA gene, and the antibiotic-resistance gene blaTEM)
from human biological samples (BALF and plasma). The method overcomes the limitations of conventional
ELISA (poor sensitivity, incompatibility with simultaneous nucleic acid measurement) by combining
proximity ligation assay (PLA) with droplet digital PCR (ddPCR), achieving sub-femtomolar protein
detection and single-copy DNA resolution.

The protocol is divided into three functional phases:
  1. DNA extraction (QIAamp cador Pathogen Mini Kit) from human BALF or plasma
  2. dPLA for IL-6 and TNF-alpha quantification with ddPCR readout
  3. Triplex ddPCR for simultaneous GN + GP + blaTEM quantification

Results are applied to two clinical cohorts:
  - Cohort 1 (BALF): 23 asthma patients vs 11 healthy subjects
    Finding: elevated GN bacteria and IL-6 in asthma vs healthy (p<0.01 and p<0.01)
  - Cohort 2 (Plasma/ICU): 32 septic shock patients at time points t1 and t2 (t3 for Group B)
    Finding: temporal changes in IL-6 and GN bacteria predict mortality; decision-tree
    classifier achieves >90% accuracy using only t1 measurements of GN and TNF-alpha

Assay performance outperforms many commercial ELISA kits:
  IL-6 LOD  : 0.02 pg/mL (0.73 fM) in BALF; 0.84 pg/mL (40 fM) in plasma (SDB-II)
  TNF-alpha LOD : 0.08 pg/mL in both BALF and plasma (HMB)
  GN LOD    : 0.06 CPN/uL (0.09 aM); GP LOD: 0.06 CPN/uL (0.09 aM)
  blaTEM LOD: 0.9 CPN/uL (1.57 aM); CV as low as 1.3%; stable >100 days

Primers and Probes (sequences in Supplementary Table 5):
  - Bac_fwd / Bac_rev: universal forward/reverse primers targeting 16S rRNA conserved region

  Simplex concentrations: 900 nM primers; 200 nM TaqMan probes
  Triplex concentrations: 900 nM all primers; 340 nM GN probe; 180 nM GP probe; 380 nM blaTEM probe

gBlock Standards (IDT): GN 16S rRNA, GP 16S rRNA, blaTEM; diluted in AVE + 0.1 mg/mL salmon sperm DNA


SAMPLE COHORTS
--------------

Cohort 1 — BALF (Asthma vs Healthy):
  Asthma (n=23): Average age 46 [38-53]; 53% female; 65% white, 30% black, 4% other.
                 Criteria: EPR 3 Guidelines on Asthma. Bronchoscopy within 4 weeks of visit.
  Healthy (n=11): No respiratory medication; good health.
  IRB: University of Chicago; written informed consent.

Cohort 2 — Plasma/ICU (Septic Shock):
  Protocol IRB: 18-1163 (University of Chicago); Declaration of Helsinki.
  Total (n=32): Adults >18 y; Medical ICU; vasoactive medication; suspected infection;
                enrolled within first 24h of Sepsis-3 shock diagnosis.
  Blood collection: 30 mL sodium citrate tubes; plasma isolated within 2h (500 g, 15 min);
                    stored at -80 C. All patients received antibiotics prior to collection.
  Time points:
    t1 = within first 24h of diagnosis (enrollment)
    t2 = 24-48h after t1 (all patients)
    t3 = upon resolution of septic shock (Group B only)
  Clinical scores: APACHE-II and SOFA on day of enrollment.
  Patient groups:
    Group A (n=7):  Early recovery. Shock resolved within 48h (no vasopressors; MAP >65 mmHg).
                    Samples at t1 and t2.
    Group B (n=13): Late recovery. Shock resolved after 48h.
                    Samples at t1, t2, and t3.
    Group C (n=12): Death. Never resolved shock. Died in ICU on vasopressors.
                    Samples at t1 and t2.


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | DNA Extraction from Human Sample
  Input          : 100 uL human plasma or BALF
  Kit            : QIAamp cador Pathogen Mini Kit (Qiagen #54104)
  Protocol       :
    1. Combine 100 uL sample + 100 uL PBS + 20 uL proteinase K
    2. Add 100 uL buffer VXL; incubate RT for 15 min
    3. Add 350 uL buffer ACB; mix thoroughly
    4. Load lysate to QIAamp Mini column; centrifuge 6000 g, 1 min
    5. Wash with AW1 buffer; centrifuge 6000 g, 1 min
    6. Wash with AW2 buffer; centrifuge 6000 g, 1 min
    7. Add 100 uL AVE elution buffer; incubate 1 min; centrifuge 20000 g, 1 min
    8. Collect eluate; store at -20 C until ddPCR
  Background correction: PBS blank extraction performed in parallel; average background
                         signal subtracted from all human sample measurements.
  Note           : Preconcentration of sample during DNA extraction further improves LOD.

Step 2 | IL-6 dPLA — Proximity Probe Preparation and Incubation
  Inputs         : Human plasma or BALF (diluted 5-fold in SDB-II or 0.1x TM buffer)
                   IL-6 proximity probes A and B (200 pM each; from TaqMan Protein Assays Open Kit)
  Probe prep     : 2 uL 1 mg/mL biotinylated antibody diluted to 200 nM in ADB; combine 5 uL
                   200 nM 5'/3' prox-oligos with 5 uL 200 nM antibody (1 h, RT); dilute to 10 nM.
  Reaction setup : 2 uL protein solution + 2 uL APS (200 pM/probe)
  Incubation     : 37 C, 60 min (antibody binds IL-6; oligonucleotides brought into proximity)
  Specificity    : IL-6-specific signal 1-2 orders of magnitude above cross-reactive IL-10 / TNF-alpha signal

Step 3 | IL-6 dPLA — Ligation and Stop
  Ligation mix   : 50 uL 20x ligation reaction buffer + 909 uL nuclease-free water + 1 uL DNA ligase (1x)
  Reaction       : Add 96 uL ligation solution to 4 uL protein/probe solution; 37 C, 10 min
  Stop           : Heat 95 C, 5 min (heat denaturation; IL-6 protocol)
  Output         : Ligated PLA product ready for ddPCR amplification

Step 4 | IL-6 dPLA — ddPCR Reaction Setup and Thermal Cycling
  Reaction setup : 9 uL PLA eluate + 10 uL 2x ddPCR Supermix for Probes + 1 uL 20x Universal PCR Assay
                   Total volume: 20 uL; emulsified per Bio-Rad QX200 protocol
  Thermal cycling:
    Preheat   : 95 C, 10 min
    Cycles    : 40x [94 C 30s / 60 C 1min]
    Final ext : 98 C, 10 min; ramp rate 2.5 C/s
  Readout        : Bio-Rad QX200 droplet reader; QuantaSoft Pro v1.0.596
  Quantification : CPN/uL converted to pg/mL via IL-6 calibration curve (R2 = 0.99)
  LOD            : 0.84 pg/mL (40 fM) in plasma SDB-II; 0.02 pg/mL (0.73 fM) in BALF
  Resolution     : ~0.04 pg/mL (patient-dependent)
  Total time     : ~2 h

Step 5 | TNF-alpha dPLA — Proximity Probe Incubation
  Buffer         : HMB (homemade Nong's buffer) — SDB-II incompatible with TNF-alpha
  Reaction setup : 2 uL plasma (5-fold diluted in HMB) + 2 uL APS (200 pM/probe)
  Incubation     : 4 C, overnight (~12 h; modelled as 720 min in BioScript)
  Critical note  : TNF-alpha degrades rapidly and is sensitive to storage and buffer conditions.
                   Overnight incubation required to maximise signal with HMB.
                   For BALF samples: 0.1x TM buffer + 0.5% gelatin used instead.

Step 6 | TNF-alpha dPLA — Ligation and Protease Stop
  Ligation       : 96 uL ligation solution added to 4 uL probe/sample; 37 C, 10 min
  Stop method    : Protease digestion (NOT heat, unlike IL-6)
                   2 uL 1x protease (in PBS) -> 37 C 10 min -> 95 C 15 min for inactivation
  Note           : Protease stop avoids non-specific signal from TNF-alpha antibody denaturation.

Step 7 | TNF-alpha dPLA — ddPCR Reaction Setup and Thermal Cycling
  Setup and cycling: Identical to IL-6 ddPCR (Step 4)
  LOD            : 0.08 pg/mL in plasma (HMB); 0.08 pg/mL in BALF (TM buffer); 4.58 fM undiluted
  Calibration    : R2 = 0.92
  Total time     : ~7 h (dominated by overnight probe incubation step)

Step 8 | Triplex ddPCR — GN Bacteria + GP Bacteria + blaTEM
  Inputs         : Extracted DNA (from Step 1)
                   GN primers/probe (Bac_fwd + Bac_rev + HEX-GN_probe)
                   GP primers/probe (Bac_fwd + Bac_rev + FAM-GP_probe)
                   blaTEM primers/probe (TEM_fwd + TEM_rev + FAM-blaTEM_probe)
  Reaction setup : 1x ddPCR Supermix for Probes; 900 nM each primer;
                   340 nM GN probe (HEX); 180 nM GP probe (FAM-medium);
                   380 nM blaTEM probe (FAM-high); total volume 20 uL
  Thermal cycling (GN/GP/blaTEM):
    Preheat   : 95 C, 10 min
    Cycles    : 40x [94 C 30s / 62 C 1min]
    Final ext : 98 C, 10 min; ramp rate 2.5 C/s
  Alternative cycling (blaTEM simplex):
    95 C 10 min; 40x [94 C 30s / 50 C 15s / 70 C 20s]; 98 C 10 min
  Cluster readout: 2D amplitude plot; 4 clusters:
    HEX-positive  = GN signal
    FAM-medium    = GP signal
    FAM-high      = blaTEM signal
    No fluorescence = empty droplets
  LODs           : GN 0.06 CPN/uL (0.09 aM); GP 0.06 CPN/uL (0.09 aM); blaTEM 0.9 CPN/uL (1.57 aM)
                   Bacteria: GN 1.76 CFU/uL; GP 12.19 CFU/uL
  Total time     : ~2 h (vs days for conventional blood culture)
  Note           : Detects both pathogenic bacteria and endogenous microbiota.
                   Duplex (GN+GP) also validated; agrees with triplex (Supplementary Fig. 6).
                   blaTEM detects 1-copy-number difference per uL.

Step 9 | Decision-Tree Classification of Patient Outcome
  Inputs         : GN bacteria (CPN/uL) and TNF-alpha (pg/mL) at t1
  Classifier     : DecisionTreeClassifier (scikit-learn, Python); max_depth=2
  Validation     : StratifiedKFold cross-validation (n_splits=5); standardised features
  Thresholds     : GN <85 CPN/uL at t1 -> Group C (death); 100% accuracy
                   GN >=85 CPN/uL + TNF-alpha threshold -> Group A or B
  Accuracy       : Group A 85.71%; Group B 92.31%; Group C 100%; k-fold average >90%
  Comparison     : Decision tree outperforms LDA and logistic regression (less overfitting
                   at n=32; controlled by max_depth)
  Key finding    : Counterintuitive relationship: lower GN at t1 predicts death.
                   Supports the hypothesis that mortality is driven by excessive host
                   cytokine response (increasing IL-6), not by direct bacterial burden.
                   IL-6 temporal change (not absolute level) is the strongest predictor:
                   89% of patients with IL-6 increase at t2 were in Group C (death);
                   85% of patients with IL-6 decrease at t2 were in Group A or B.
  Note           : Patient group classification was performed by independent clinicians
                   without access to molecular measurements (no classification-related confounders).


KEY BIOLOGICAL FINDINGS
-----------------------

BALF (Asthma):
  - Almost all asthma patients had elevated GN bacteria; only 55% of healthy individuals did.
  - IL-6 significantly elevated in asthma vs healthy (p<0.01); supports IL-6 as airway
    inflammation biomarker and potential asthma phenotype marker (IL-6-high phenotype).
  - TNF-alpha uniformly low in all BALF samples (<0.5 pg/mL, near assay LOD).
  - Two GN subpopulations observed in both groups (~20 CPN/uL and ~2 CPN/uL).

Plasma (Septic Shock):
  - GN: Group A decreased rapidly (avg -157 CPN/uL, t1->t2); Group B increased (avg +108 CPN/uL);
    Group C decreased slightly — counterintuitively predicts death.
  - GP: Groups A and C decreased to nondetectable at t2; Group B remained at moderate levels
    (13-36 CPN/uL) across all 3 time points — surprising since Group B ultimately recovered.
  - blaTEM: Most patients below LOD. One Group B patient showed blaTEM rise from 0 to >300
    CPN/uL at t3; confirmed by blood culture (beta-lactam-producing bacteria). Digital assay
    result available same day vs days for culture.
  - TNF-alpha: Group B had highest levels at t1 and t2; dropped to <10 pg/mL by t3 (resolution).
    Group C had very low TNF-alpha (progressed beyond initial cytokine secretion peak by t1).
  - IL-6 absolute levels not correlated with outcome; temporal changes strongly correlated.
    Increasing IL-6 -> death; decreasing IL-6 -> recovery (Fig. 5c-e).
  - dPLA resolution (~0.04 pg/mL) allows detection of IL-6 changes too small for conventional ELISA.
