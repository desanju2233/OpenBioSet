PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Enhancing Protease Activity Assay in Droplet-Based Microfluidics Using a Biomolecule Concentrator
Source Link  : https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3165005/pdf/nihms305699.pdf
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1021/ja2036628
Journal      : J. Am. Chem. Soc., 2011, Vol. 133, No. 27, pp. 10368-10371
Platform     : Integrated nanofluidic ICP biomolecule concentrator + flow-focusing droplet generator (PDMS chip)


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes an integrated microfluidic platform that combines a nanofluidic
biomolecule concentrator based on ion concentration polarization (ICP) with a flow-focusing
microdroplet generator on a single PDMS chip. The concentrator pre-concentrates low-abundance
enzyme molecules from a diluted physiological sample into a pL-scale plug before encapsulating
them into water-in-oil droplet microreactors. This addresses two fundamental limitations of
droplet-based enzyme assays: low assay sensitivity from low-abundance analytes, and long
reaction times required to turn over sufficient substrate for detection.

The platform was validated on:
  1. beta-galactosidase (platform characterisation)
  2. Kinase MK2 (platform characterisation)
  3. Recombinant MMP-9 at 0.2-3.2 nM (primary assay; kinetics determination)
  4. MMP activity in MEF cellular supernatant -- stimulated vs. untreated conditions

Key achievements vs. conventional droplet assays:
  - ~10-fold reduction in assay reaction time
  - ~100-fold reduction in sample volume (<25 uL total for up to 10 conditions)
  - ~100-fold concentration enhancement in the ICP plug (~20-fold at encapsulation)
  - kcat/Km = 7.81e4 M^-1 s^-1 for MMP-9, consistent with plate-reader and prior literature
  - Stimulated vs. untreated MEF differentiation in 5 min (concentrated) vs. >50 min
    (unconcentrated)


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Fabrication  : PDMS chip bonded to PDMS-coated glass slide; standard soft lithography.
               Three syringe pumps (Harvard PHD2000) drive fluid flow.

Concentrator section:
  - Polymeric nanoporous ion-selective junctions flanking the main sample channel
  - Buffer side channels on either side of the junction for voltage application
  - Applied voltage: ~50 V (main channel at +50 V, buffer channels at GND)
  - Sample flow rate: 0.03 uL/min (middle channel, pressure-driven)
  - Mechanism: electrokinetic force opposes pressure-driven flow; negatively charged
    molecules accumulate at depletion boundary into a continuously growing pL-scale plug
  - Enhancement: ~100-fold in plug; reduces to ~20-fold at encapsulation due to Gaussian
    diffusion during ~200 um transport to droplet generator
  - Enhancement factor tunable by adjusting accumulation time and flow rate

Mixing zone:
  - Released plug merges 1:1 with substrate from two flanking side channels
  - Enzyme:substrate ratio controlled by balancing three aqueous stream flow rates

Droplet generator section:
  - Flow-focusing geometry; hydrophobic PDMS surfaces cause aqueous phase to lift off
    into oil and encapsulate
  - Carrier fluid: HFE7500 fluorocarbon oil at 1.0 uL/min
  - Surfactant: biocompatible perfluorinated surfactant (synthesised in-house) in oil phase
  - Droplet volume: ~40 pL; generation rate: ~2.5 kHz; monodisperse W/O emulsion
  - Gaussian plug divided into train of droplets with graded enzyme concentrations
    (0.2-3.2 nM from tail to centre) enabling parallel kinetics screening in one run

Channels:
  - Middle channel   : Sample stream (enzyme + Alexa Fluor-546 tracer)
  - Side channels    : Substrate streams (two flanking); converge at mixing zone
  - Buffer channels  : PBS flanking main channel on either side of ion-selective membrane


REAGENTS
--------
Tracer dye       : Alexa Fluor-546 phalloidin; ex 561 nm / em 572 nm
                   Co-loaded with enzyme; monitors plug in real time; intensity ratio
                   vs. enzyme-free droplet infers local enzyme concentration per droplet.
Carrier oil      : 3M Novec HFE7500 fluorocarbon oil
Surfactant       : Synthesised biocompatible perfluorinated surfactant in oil phase
Priming agent    : Pluronics F-127 (2% in PBS)
MMP buffer       : Standard MMP assay buffer
Enzyme (recomb.) : MMP-9 (recombinant); 0.2 nM in MMP buffer; screened 0.2-3.2 nM
Substrate        : FRET-based polypeptide MMP substrate; 5 uM; fluoresces upon cleavage
Cellular samples : Mouse embryonic fibroblast (MEF) conditioned media;
                   0.5x dilution in MMP buffer; stimulated (cytokine-treated) and untreated;
                   <25 uL total consumed across both conditions


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Device Priming and Surface Passivation
  Reagents  : PBS, Pluronics F-127
  Mix       : 2% Pluronics F-127 in PBS
  Duration  : 5 min
  Purpose   : Reduces nonspecific adsorption of biomolecules to PDMS channel walls
              and to droplet interfaces; critical for low-abundance analyte recovery.

Step 2 | Enzyme Sample Stream Preparation
  Reagents  : MMP-9 (0.2 nM), Alexa Fluor-546 tracer, MMP buffer
  Operation : Mix enzyme with tracer, then dilute in MMP buffer
  Channel   : Middle channel, flow rate 0.03 uL/min
  Notes     : Tracer fluorescence (ex 561 nm / em 572 nm) monitors plug accumulation and
              allows per-droplet enzyme concentration to be inferred post-encapsulation
              by comparing to tracer intensity of enzyme-free droplet (0.2 nM reference).

Step 3 | Substrate Side-Channel Stream Preparation
  Reagents  : FRET MMP polypeptide substrate (5 uM in MMP buffer)
  Operation : Dispense into left and right flanking side channels
  Notes     : Substrate streams merge with enzyme plug at mixing zone just before droplet
              formation. Enzyme:substrate ratio set to 1:1 by balancing aqueous flow rates.

Step 4 | ICP Biomolecule Concentrator -- Accumulation Phase
  Operation : Electrokinetic trapping (voltage-on)
              Modelled as heat at 25 C (ambient; no external heating) in BioScript.
  Voltage   : ~50 V across ion-selective nanoporous junction
  Flow rate : 0.03 uL/min (sample); PBS in buffer side channels at GND
  Duration  : ~10 min (accumulation time; primary handle for tuning enhancement factor)
  Output    : ~100-fold concentration enhancement in the pL-scale plug
  Notes     : Voltage-off releases plug by pressure-driven flow. Negatively charged enzyme
              molecules and tracer trap at the ICP depletion boundary and accumulate
              continuously. Enhancement factor adjustable by concentration time.

Step 5 | Plug Transport and Substrate Mixing
  Operation : mix (plug + substrate)
  Distance  : ~200 um from concentrator to droplet generator
  Output    : ~20-fold enhancement at encapsulation point (Gaussian diffusion during transport
              reduces peak from ~100-fold). Enzyme and substrate mixed 1:1 in mixing zone.

Step 6 | Flow-Focusing Droplet Encapsulation
  Operation : mix assay plug with HFE7500 + surfactant carrier phase
  Oil flow  : 1.0 uL/min
  Droplets  : ~40 pL; ~2.5 kHz generation rate; monodisperse W/O emulsion
  Bins      : Gaussian plug divided into droplet train spanning 0.2-3.2 nM enzyme:
                High bin  : ~20-fold enhancement; ~3.2 nM MMP-9 (plug centre)
                Mid bin   : ~10-fold enhancement; ~1.6 nM MMP-9 (plug shoulder)
                Low bin   : ~1-fold;              ~0.2 nM MMP-9 (plug tail; baseline)
  Notes     : Vortices induced inside droplets ensure rapid enzyme-substrate mixing
              post-encapsulation. Surfactant stabilises emulsion; contents isolated for
              individual reactions. Simultaneous observation of multiple concentrations
              enables high-throughput kinetics determination in a single run.

Step 7 | Droplet Incubation and Fluorescence Monitoring (Recombinant MMP-9)
  Operation      : heat at 37 C for 35 min; detect mmpActivityDetection
  Readout        : Product fluorescence increases linearly with time per droplet (Fig. 2A)
                   Tracer channel reports local enzyme concentration per droplet
  Kinetics       : Reaction rates vs. concentration fitted to Michaelis-Menten (Fig. 2B)
                   kcat/Km = 7.81e4 M^-1 s^-1 (consistent with plate-reader and literature)
  Neg. control   : ~25 a.u. fluorescence change (no protease); flat baseline
  Enhancement    : Concentrated droplets show 15-fold higher reaction rate vs. unconcentrated
  QC gate        : assaySignal > 0 -> dispose (BB2); <= 0 -> drain (BB3); join at BB4

Step 8 | Cellular Supernatant Sample Preparation
  Reagents  : MEF conditioned media (stimulated and untreated), 0.5x in MMP buffer,
              Alexa Fluor-546 tracer, MMP buffer
  Operation : Mix each condition with tracer, then with buffer
  Volume    : <25 uL total across both conditions
  Notes     : Both conditions run in sequence on same chip platform.

Step 9 | ICP Concentration of Cellular Supernatant
  Operation : Electrokinetic trapping; same conditions as Step 4
              Modelled as heat at 25 C for 10 min in BioScript
  Output    : ~10-20x enhancement at encapsulation
  Notes     : Concentration step amplifies activity difference between stimulated and
              untreated ~10-fold relative to unconcentrated samples. Enables detection
              of biologically relevant MMP activity differences from physiological samples.

Step 10 | Cellular Assay Droplet Encapsulation
  Operation  : mix concentrated plugs with FRET substrate + carrier oil phase
  Substrate  : FRET MMP substrate (5 uM)
  Carrier    : HFE7500 + fluorocarbon surfactant
  Notes      : Same flow-focusing geometry as Step 6. Stimulated and untreated samples
               run as separate droplet populations.

Step 11 | Cellular Proteolytic Activity Detection
  Operation      : heat at 37 C for 5 min; detect mmpActivityDetection
  Key timepoint  : 5 min sufficient to differentiate stimulated vs. untreated (concentrated)
                   Concentrated  -- stimulated: slope 2.14 a.u./s (line-1)
                                 -- untreated:  slope 0.95 a.u./s (line-3)
                   Unconcentrated requires >50 min:
                                    stimulated: slope 0.32 a.u./s (line-2)
                                    untreated:  slope 0.22 a.u./s (line-4)  (Fig. 3A)
  Kinetics       : Linear relation between reaction rate and concentration (Fig. 3B)
                   Stimulated:  (kcat/KM)[E] = 1.61e-4 s^-1
                   Untreated:   (kcat/KM)[E] = 4.68e-5 s^-1
  QC gate        : cellSignal > 0 -> dispose (BB5); <= 0 -> drain (BB6); join at BB7

ICP concentrator abstraction:
  The electrokinetic trapping (voltage-on accumulation) step has no native BioScript
  primitive. It is modelled as heat at 25 C (room temperature; no external heating applied
  in the paper). Duration encodes accumulation time, which is the primary experimental
  handle for controlling the enhancement factor. Voltage-off plug release is implicit at
  the end of the heat block. Buffer side-channel flows are represented as dispense + mix
  steps converging at the junction, consistent with the three-stream chip geometry.

Droplet encapsulation abstraction:
  Flow-focusing encapsulation is modelled as mix with carrier oil phase. The Gaussian
  plug producing a graded enzyme concentration across the droplet train is represented
  as three discrete scalar droplet variables (high/mid/low bins). This encodes the
  parallel kinetics screening experiment (Fig. 2B) within BioScript's scalar variable model.

Compiler behaviour notes:
  - drain statements are lowered to dispose at IR level; expected BioScript behaviour,
    not a source error.
  - BB5 (pass branch of second if gate) has no explicit jump instruction in IR; the
    compiler infers the edge 5->7 via fall-through. The DAG edge is present and correct.
    No source fix is required; behaviour is consistent with reference files
    (samlali_hybrid_microfluidics_isoclone.bs, moazami_world_to_chip_dmf.bs).
