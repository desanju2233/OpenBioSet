PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Droplet On Tape: Protocol
Source Link  : https://www.protocols.io/view/droplet-on-tape-protocol-n2bvj9wzblk5/v1
DOI Link     : https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/protex.2017.017
Journal      : Nature Protocols Exchange, February 28, 2017
Platform     : Acoustic Droplet Ejection (ADE) onto Kapton conveyor belt (DOT system) for XFEL sample delivery
Associated   : Fuller et al., Nature Methods, 2017; DOI: 10.1038/nmeth.4195


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes the Droplet On Tape (DOT) system, a flexible sample delivery platform
coupling Acoustic Droplet Ejection (ADE) with a moving Kapton conveyor belt for time-resolved
X-ray studies at X-ray Free Electron Lasers (XFELs). DOT enables simultaneous X-ray Diffraction
(XRD) and X-ray Emission Spectroscopy (XES) from protein microcrystal slurry or protein
solution, along with ancillary signals (transmission, fluorescence). It supports multiple
reaction initiation schemes: laser flash photolysis (single-excitation free-space timing and
multi-step fiber excitation) and gas-phase activation (differential pumping of reactive gas).

The platform is designed for restricted-access beamline environments and is operated entirely
via remote control (EPICS framework). The DOT system is comprised of five main sub-systems:
the ADE injector, control systems, the conveyor belt, the sample environment (Helium enclosure),
and reaction initiation systems.

Typical beamline deployment: 5 days setup (3 people); 4-5 days data collection per experiment.
Custom fabrication of all components: ~1 month (machining) + ~1 month (assembly, 2 people).


SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Five integrated sub-systems:

Sub-system 1 | ADE Injector
  Transducer   : Olympus V319-SU, 1 inch spherical focus, 15 MHz resonance frequency
                 (developed for ultrasonic imaging applications)
  Waveform gen.: Agilent 33612A; tone-burst at 15 MHz, controllable amplitude and duration
  Amplifier    : Amplifier Research 500A250C RF amplifier; final driving voltage ~40 V
  Ejection     : Tone-burst <100 us ejects nano-liter droplet from sample-air interface
  Coupling     : BNC cable -> bi-directional coupler (30 dB) -> transducer
                 Bi-directional coupler samples forward and reverse signals
  Echo probe   : "probe" spike (few hundred ns) sent a few ms before ejection burst
                 Sonar principle: echo return at ~35 us delay (1 inch focal length transducer)
                 at correct focus; fluid level monitored with <50 um accuracy
                 Oscilloscope monitored remotely via remote desktop (critical at beamline hutch)
  Sample path  : Polymicro tubing (360 um O.D., 180-250 um I.D.) from Legato 130 syringe pump
                 to ADE reservoir; coupling water column isolates sample from transducer

Sub-system 2 | Control Systems
  Framework    : EPICS (Experimental Physics Industrial Control Software)
  Hardware     : Syringe pump (Legato 130), Waveform generator (Agilent 33612A),
                 Actuator motors (Newport XPS-Q8), Tape drive motor (Lexium M-Drive),
                 Cameras (Allied Vision Manta-style)
  Custom IOCs  : Syringe pump: https://github.com/fullerf/LEGATO130
                 Waveform generator: https://github.com/fullerf/Agilent33600A
  Dependencies : EPICS base, StreamDevice, Asyn
  Tape drive   : Lexium M-Drive connected via built-in serial terminal; telnet from Linux/Mac
                 Speed controlled via motor slew rate adjustments (infrequently needed after
                 initial calibration)

Sub-system 3 | Conveyor Belt System
  Belt type    : Seamless Kapton (polyimide, Kapton 100H, C22H10O2N5, DuPont)
                 0.063-0.125 inch width; 31-36 inch diameter; 50 micron thickness
                 Supplier: BPB Inc., Bay Area, CA (custom request)
  Why seamless : Ultrasonically welded belts skip at weld over rollers, disrupting XFEL
                 synchronisation. Seamless belts eliminate this problem.
  Alternative  : Mylar film (used early in development; inferior x-ray scattering vs Kapton)
  Consumable   : Yes; one belt lasts several shifts if XFEL beam does not directly hit it
  Surface tmt  : FAS (1H,1H,2H,2H-Perfluorodecyltriethoxysilane, 97%, Sigma-Aldrich) in
                 n-octane; WCA increased from ~60 deg to 100 +/- 5 deg (see protocol below)
  Tensioner    : Hanging weight on rotating arm; two crowned rollers; adjustable by adding or
                 removing weights; over-tension indicator: belt curls when tension released
  Rollers      : ABEC 5 low-friction ball bearings with CNC-machined crown cap (3 inch radius
                 of curvature); crown keeps belt centred during operation
  Electrostatic: Passive: steel anti-static brushes near drive mechanism
                 Active: Transforming Technologies IN3425 static gun (ionised air) near ADE
                 deposition point; both required to avoid disrupting ADE ejection
  Mounting     : Newport UTS-100 motorised stage; modified Thorlabs aluminium breadboards
  Drive motor  : Lexium M-Drive LMDCE423 micro-stepping stepper motor; direct drive to 1 inch
                 stainless steel shaft; <4 revolutions/s typical operating speed
                 (geared mechanism abandoned due to velocity error introduction)

Sub-system 4 | Sample Environment
  Enclosure    : Custom acrylic (0.5 inch) helium chamber; Professional Plastics Inc.
  Door         : Large inflatable door with wide aperture at X-ray interaction region
  Joints       : Solvent welded (where possible); rubber foam gaskets elsewhere
  Feed-through : 0.125 inch aluminium panel, water-jet cut; handles all electronic, gas,
                 water, and fibre connections into the chamber
  Helium path  : Thin inflatable polyethylene bags extend helium environment from sample to
                 detector; second bag between XES crystal spectrometer and main chamber;
                 minimises scatter from heavier atoms in air during XRD/XES

Sub-system 5 | Reaction Initiation Systems
  Laser (fiber): High-precision CNC-machined grid (tolerance 0.0005 inch); holds 3 excitation
                 fibre-coupled laser inputs and 2 IR gate fibres
                 IR gate separation: 60 mm; each gate 30 mm from nearest excitation point
                 Each excitation fibre: 5 degrees of freedom for coupling optimisation
                 Droplet arrival monitored via IR scatter onto photodiode
                 All 5 fibres monitored on remote-accessible oscilloscopes
  Laser (free) : Single-excitation; movable 1 m focal-length motorised lens upstream of XFEL
                 Max laser advance: 6 mm upstream of XFEL interaction
                 Time delay range: 20-100 ms (at 300-60 mm/s belt speed)
                 Spatial overlap: 100 um accuracy (laser spot: ~400 um diameter)
                 Temporal overlap: 200 us accuracy (camera exposure); ns via photodiode
  Gas reaction : Conveyor belt passes through limited-extent reactive gas region (O2 example)
                 Confinement: differential pumping via series of small orifices
                 Exposure time: L / v_tape; Dead time: L_xfel / v_tape
                 Sealing: vacuum grease on all openings except belt entry and exit holes


BELT SURFACE TREATMENT PROTOCOL
---------------------------------

Step 1 | Belt Cleaning
  Reagents  : Milli-Q water, isopropanol
  Action    : Sonicate in Milli-Q water for 10 min; then sonicate in isopropanol for 10 min
  Notes     : Removes surface contaminants before hydrophobicity treatment

Step 2 | Vacuum Drying (pre-FAS)
  Equipment : Precision vacuum oven, model 19
  Condition : 50 C, 1 hour
  Notes     : Belt must be fully dry before FAS treatment; moisture interferes with silane
              bonding chemistry

Step 3 | FAS Hydrophobicity Treatment
  Reagent   : 0.25% (w/v) FAS (1H,1H,2H,2H-Perfluorodecyltriethoxysilane, Sigma-Aldrich)
              in n-octane
  Action    : Reflux belt in FAS/n-octane solution for 2 hours
  Result    : Water contact angle (WCA) increases from ~60 deg to 100 +/- 5 deg
  Purpose   : Aqueous droplets bead up on belt surface and do not spread; critical for
              maintaining droplet integrity during transport to XFEL interaction region

Step 4 | Vacuum Drying (post-FAS)
  Equipment : Precision vacuum oven, model 19
  Condition : 150 C, 3 hours
  Notes     : Removes all residual unreacted FAS from belt surface


BELT CLEANING PROCEDURE (in-experiment)
-----------------------------------------
  Method    : Drive belt through 4 high-pressure water jets (200+ psi, 500 um I.D.)
  Drying    : 2 compressed Helium blow-off nozzles (top and bottom of belt)
  Gas supply: 3 x KNF Neuberger N145.1.2AN.18 gas-tight diaphragm pumps draw Helium from
              the Helium chamber and recirculate it to power the blow-off nozzles
  Residue QC: Run belt dry (no sample injected); probe XES signal; accumulated residue
              detected as XES background above baseline; performed periodically during beamtimes


FIBER EXCITATION TIMING (Multi-excitation, e.g. Photosystem II)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  Principle : Space-time diagram representation; constant belt velocity -> trajectories are
              straight lines with slope = velocity; excitation positions fixed in space/time
  Two DOF   : (1) deposition delay (Delta_tau_dep): when droplet is ejected onto belt
              (2) laser delay (Delta_tau_laser): when each fibre excitation fires
  Setup     :
    1. Adjust deposition delay until droplet appears in XFEL crosshairs (stroboscopic monitor)
    2. Fine-adjust deposition delay until droplet observed to explode (camera synced to XFEL)
    3. Confirm via solvent scatter in forward detector or emission signal in XES detector
    4. Adjust belt velocity until IR gate signals coincide in time
       (confirms droplets simultaneously present on all excitation points)
    5. Laser delay must be time-coincident with IR gate signals
  Allowed speeds at 10 Hz rep rate: 30, 60, 150, 300 mm/s
  (droplet spacing must be integer multiple or divisor of both 30 mm and 60 mm)
  Note      : Deposition delay and belt speed are coupled; adjust both incrementally


PROTOCOL STEPS (BioScript encoding)
--------------------------------------

Step 1 | Kapton Belt Cleaning (BB1 / main)
  Inputs    : MQWater, Isopropanol
  Operation : mix (sonication abstracted); 10 min
  Notes     : Sequential sonication in Milli-Q water then isopropanol

Step 2 | Vacuum Drying Before FAS (BB1 / main)
  Operation : heat at 50 C for 60 min (3600 s in IR)
  Equipment : Precision vacuum oven

Step 3 | FAS Reflux Treatment (BB1 / main)
  Inputs    : FAS_NOctane (0.25% w/v FAS in n-octane)
  Operation : mix (reflux abstracted); 120 min (7200 s in IR)

Step 4 | Vacuum Drying After FAS (BB1 / main)
  Operation : heat at 150 C for 180 min (10800 s in IR)
  Notes     : Residual FAS removal

Step 5 | ADE Injector Setup and Sample Loading (BB1 / main)
  Inputs    : SampleSolution, CouplingWater
  Operation : mix (reservoir loading abstracted); 2 s
  Notes     : Legato 130 syringe pump controls sample influx rate; coupling water column
              isolates sample from transducer

Step 6 | Acoustic Echo Probe — Fluid Level QC (BB1 -> BB2/BB3)
  Operation : detect echoLevelDetection for 10 s
  Pass (BB2): echoSignal > 0 -> fluid level and focus confirmed; eject droplet; dispose
  Fail (BB3): echoSignal <= 0 -> echo out of range; drain; re-adjust sample influx rate
  Join (BB4): dispose(echoSignal)

Step 7 | Laser Zone Droplet Transport (BB4 / echoJoin)
  Inputs    : ProteinCrystalSlurry, HeliumGas
  Operation : mix (belt transport through Helium enclosure abstracted); 5 s
  Notes     : Belt carries droplet from ADE deposition point through Helium chamber to laser
              excitation zone; belt speed 30-300 mm/s; time delays 20-100 ms

Step 8 | Droplet-Hit and Timing Confirmation (BB4 -> BB5/BB6)
  Operation : detect dropletHitDetection for 30 s
  Methods   : stroboscopic camera (sync'd to XFEL), forward scatter detector, XES detector,
              IR gate photodiode coincidence
  Pass (BB5): laserHitSignal > 0 -> overlap confirmed; dispose; proceed to XRD
  Fail (BB6): laserHitSignal <= 0 -> droplet missed; drain; re-adjust delay/belt speed
  Join (BB7): dispose(laserHitSignal)

Step 9 | XFEL Data Collection - XRD (BB7 -> BB8/BB9)
  Inputs    : ProteinCrystalSlurry, HeliumGas
  Operation : detect xrdSignalDetection for 30 s
  Motor     : Lexium M-Drive LMDCE423 direct drive; <4 rps
  Pass (BB8): xrdSignal > 0 -> valid diffraction; dispose; proceed to XES
  Fail (BB9): xrdSignal <= 0 -> beam miss or crystal failure; drain
  Join(BB10): dispose(xrdSignal)

Step 10 | Simultaneous XES Data Collection (BB10 -> BB11/BB12)
  Inputs    : ProteinCrystalSlurry, HeliumGas
  Operation : detect xesSignalDetection for 30 s
  Notes     : Second Helium bag between XES crystal spectrometer and main Helium chamber
  Pass(BB11): xesSignal > 0 -> valid emission spectrum; dispose; proceed to belt residue check
  Fail(BB12): xesSignal <= 0 -> no emission; drain
  Join(BB13): dispose(xesSignal)

Step 11 | Belt Cleaning Run (BB13 / xesJoin)
  Inputs    : MQWater, HeliumGas
  Operation : mix (water jet pass abstracted); 5 min cleaning; 2 min blow-off drying
  Water jets: 4 x 200+ psi jets; 500 um I.D.
  Drying    : 2 compressed Helium blow-off nozzles (KNF Neuberger pump system)

Step 12 | Belt Residue QC Check (BB13 -> BB14/BB15)
  Operation : detect residueDetection for 30 s
  Method    : dry belt run; XES background monitoring
  Pass(BB14): residueSignal > 0 -> residue above baseline; dispose; repeat cleaning cycle
  Fail(BB15): residueSignal <= 0 -> belt clean; dispose; ready for next shift
  Join(BB16): dispose(residueSignal); NOP [terminal]
