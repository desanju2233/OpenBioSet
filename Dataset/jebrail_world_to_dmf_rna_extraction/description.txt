PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : World-to-Digital-Microfluidic Interface Enabling Extraction and Purification of RNA from Human Whole Blood
Source Link  : https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ac404085p
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1021/ac404085p
Journal      : Analytical Chemistry, 2014, Vol. 86, pp. 3856-3862
Authors      : Mais J. Jebrail, Anupama Sinha, Samantha Vellucci, Ronald F. Renzi, Cesar Ambriz,
               Carmen Gondhalekar, Joseph S. Schoeniger, Kamlesh D. Patel, Steven S. Branda
Institution  : Sandia National Laboratories, Livermore, California
Platform     : World-to-DMF (macroscale Extraction Module + DMF-based Purification Module,
               peristaltic pump driven, 10 uL Bridging Droplet interface)


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes the first "world-to-DMF" interface: an engineered system that drives
a large-volume real-world sample (human whole blood lysate, 110-380 uL) repeatedly through a
10 uL droplet region on a digital microfluidic (DMF) device, enabling inline concentration of
RNA-bound magnetic beads from the macroscale volume directly onto the DMF chip surface.
Downstream purification (two washes + elution) is then performed entirely in microscale volumes
(5-15 uL) within the DMF Purification Module.

The protocol achieves >2-fold faster processing and 12-fold reagent reduction compared to
conventional bench-scale preparations, while producing RNA of equivalent yield and quality
(A260/A280, RIN) suitable for qRT-PCR and RNA-Seq analyses. The BioScript file encodes
the 50 uL blood specimen arm as the representative protocol (intermediate of the three
volumes tested: 25, 50, and 100 uL).

Key innovation: Inline (on-DMF-device) concentration of sample analytes, as opposed to
off-device pre-concentration, eliminates manual handling steps, reduces contamination risk,
and removes worker exposure to biological samples.


SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
The world-to-DMF system has three components:

Extraction Module:
  - Two 500 uL microcentrifuge tubes: Reaction Chamber and Waste Chamber
  - One peristaltic pump connected via Connector Tubes to both chambers
  - Transfer Tube 1 and Transfer Tube 2: conduct fluid between Reaction Chamber
    and the Module Interface (Bridging Electrode)
  - Transfer Tube 1 preloaded: Lysis Solution (130 uL) | 20 uL air gap | RNA Binding Beads (20 uL)
  - Pump drives pressure-driven convective flow of the full 200 uL lysate through the
    10 uL Bridging Droplet (3 passes); convective transport increases bead capture efficiency
    vs. diffusion-only conventional method

Purification Module (DMF device, fabricated in-house):
  - Bottom plate: 40 chromium electrode pads (6x6 mm, 40 um interelectrode gaps) on glass;
    photolithography + etching
  - Dielectric: 7 um Parylene-C (vapor deposition)
  - Hydrophobic: 50 nm Teflon-AF (spin-coat)
  - Top plate: ITO-coated glass + 50 nm Teflon-AF
  - Air gap: 300 um (three layers double-sided tape spacer)
  - Actuation: AC electrowetting, 100 Vrms
  - On-device reservoirs preloaded: Wash 1 (15 uL), Wash 2 (15 uL), Elution Buffer (10 uL)

Module Interface (Bridging Droplet):
  - Bridging Electrode: working electrode pad at the periphery of the DMF device; top plate
    recessed to expose ~1 mm x 6 mm of the electrode surface
  - Transfer Tubes contact the Bridging Electrode at ~70 degrees (adjustable magnetic stand +
    custom spacing manifold)
  - Bridging Droplet (10 uL): formed by coordinating three forces simultaneously --
    dispensation from Transfer Tube 1, aspiration into Transfer Tube 2, and AC actuation of
    the Bridging Electrode (100 Vrms)
  - External movable magnet positioned directly beneath Bridging Electrode to immobilise
    bead-bound analytes as the macroscale volume passes through the droplet
  - Three strategies for reliable Bridging Droplet formation:
      (1) Organic solvents (>=15%; ethanol/isopropanol in lysis buffer) reduce surface tension
          to allow fluid entry into the air gap
      (2) Top plate recessed 1 mm to expose Bridging Electrode for direct Transfer Tube access
      (3) Coordinated choreography of pump and electrode actuation


REAGENTS
--------
Whole blood         : 50 uL (healthy volunteer donors, ProMedDx, Norton MA)
Lysis Solution      : MagMAX-Blood lysis buffer, 130 uL (Life Technologies, Grand Island NY);
                      contains chaotropic salts; prepared per manufacturer's instructions
RNA Binding Beads   : MagMAX silica-coated magnetic beads, 20 uL (MagMAX-Blood RNA Isolation Kit)
Wash 1 Solution     : MagMAX Wash 1 Solution, 15 uL (on-device reservoir)
                      [conventional method uses 150 uL per wash; 10-fold reduction]
Wash 2 Solution     : MagMAX Wash 2 Solution, 15 uL (on-device reservoir)
                      [removes residual chaotropic salts and ethanol from Wash 1]
Elution Buffer      : MagMAX Elution Buffer + 0.1% w/v Pluronic F127, 10 uL (on-device)
                      [Pluronic F127 added per ref 20 to maintain droplet actuation fidelity
                      on Teflon-AF surface; conventional method uses 30 uL]
Filler fluid        : Fluorinert FC-40 (Sigma Chemical, St. Louis MO)
Surfactant          : Pluronic F127 (Sigma Chemical); 0.1% w/v in Elution Buffer;
                      also mixed with FC-40 to prime DMF air gap
Parylene C dimer    : Specialty Coating Systems, Indianapolis IN (dielectric deposition)
Teflon-AF           : DuPont, Wilmington DE (hydrophobic coating)


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 0 | Device Initialisation
  Reagents  : FluorinertFC40, PluronicF127, Wash1Solution, Wash2Solution, ElutionBuffer
  Operation : Dispense FC-40 + Pluronic F127; mix 30 s to prime DMF air gap.
              Dispense Wash 1, Wash 2, and Elution Buffer into on-device reservoirs.
  Setup     : Adjust magnetic stand so Transfer Tubes contact Bridging Electrode at ~70 degrees.
              AC 100 Vrms actuation enabled.
  Notes     : BioScript variable `devicePrime` (FC-40 + Pluronic F127 mix) is reused throughout
              the protocol as the second argument to `mix` calls that abstract magnet
              engage/disengage steps and bead pellet progression. This is a compiler-driven
              abstraction; physically, these steps involve only the bead pellet and the
              immediately preceding droplet.

Step 1 | Blood Specimen Introduction
  Reagent   : WholeBlood
  Volume    : 50 uL
  Operation : Dispense into Reaction Chamber (500 uL microcentrifuge tube) by pipetting.
  Notes     : Specimens from healthy volunteer donors (ProMedDx, Norton MA).
              Other arms: 25 uL (total lysate 110 uL) or 100 uL (total lysate 380 uL).

Step 2 | Lysis and Mixing
  Reagent   : LysisSolution (130 uL for 50 uL blood; 2.6:1 v/v lysis:blood ratio)
  Duration  : 30 s
  Mechanism : Peristaltic pump aspirates Lysis Solution from Transfer Tube 1 into Reaction
              Chamber; bolus shuttled rapidly between Transfer Tube 1 and Reaction Chamber
              for 30 s to mix thoroughly.
  Output    : bloodLysate (homogeneous blood + lysis buffer mixture)

Step 3 | RNA Binding Bead Addition and Incubation
  Reagent     : RNABindingBeads (20 uL MagMAX silica-coated magnetic beads)
  Mix         : 30 s (shuttle bolus between Transfer Tube 1 and Reaction Chamber)
  Incubation  : 5 min (300 s) at room temperature (25 C) -- RNA binds to bead surface
  Total volume: 200 uL (50 + 130 + 20 uL)
  BioScript   : `heat beadLysateMix at 25c for 300s` models the bead-binding incubation.

Step 4 | World-to-DMF Transition: Bridging Droplet Formation and Bead Capture (3 Passes)
  Operation : Pressure-driven convective flow-through (abstracted as `mix` in BioScript)
  Passes    : 3 (bidirectional: Transfer Tube 1 -> Bridging Droplet -> Transfer Tube 2, then back)
  Per-pass  :
    - Pump drives full 200 uL lysate through the 10 uL Bridging Droplet on Bridging Electrode
    - AC 100 Vrms applied to Bridging Electrode to pin the droplet (prevent spread)
    - External magnet beneath Bridging Electrode engaged to immobilise bead-bound RNA
    - `heat at 25c for 60s` models magnet engagement per pass
  After 3 passes:
    - RNA Binding Beads with bound RNA are immobilised on Bridging Electrode surface
    - Lysate supernatant is directed to Waste Chamber
    - `beadCapture` is the BioScript handle for the bead pellet going forward
  Notes     : Convective (pressure-driven) transport is more efficient than the diffusion-only
              transport in the conventional method; accounts for the slightly better RNA yield
              observed at 100 uL blood volume (p=0.03, Student t-test).
              bridgingDroplet1/2/3 each consumed as input to successor mix call; must not
              be re-read. beadCapture is introduced as a clean handle post-disposal.

Step 5 | Wash 1
  Reagent   : Wash1Solution (15 uL, on-device reservoir)
  Operation :
    1. heat wash1Reservoir at 25c for 10s -- models magnet disengage (bead resuspension)
    2. mix wash1Reservoir with beadCapture for 30s -- bead-wash mixing via Transfer Tube shuttle
    3. heat wash1Mix at 25c for 30s -- models mixing/washing contact time
    4. wash1Beads = mix wash1Mix with devicePrime for 5s -- magnet re-engage; bead pellet captured
    5. dispose wash1Mix -- wash fluid sent to Waste Chamber
  Output    : wash1Beads (bead pellet proceeding to Wash 2)
  Note      : 15 uL vs. 150 uL in conventional method (10-fold reduction per wash).

Step 6 | Wash 2
  Reagent   : Wash2Solution (15 uL, on-device reservoir)
  Operation : Identical four-step sequence as Wash 1 (disengage -> mix -> re-engage -> dispose).
  Purpose   : Removes residual chaotropic salts and ethanol carried over from Wash 1.
  Output    : wash2Beads (bead pellet proceeding to Elution)

Step 7 | Elution
  Reagent       : ElutionBuffer (10 uL + 0.1% Pluronic F127, on-device reservoir)
  Operation     :
    1. heat elutionReservoir at 25c for 10s -- magnet disengage; beads resuspend
    2. mix elutionReservoir with wash2Beads for 30s -- bead-eluate mixing
    3. heat elutionMix at 25c for 120s -- 2 min incubation; RNA released from bead surface
    4. heat elutionMix at 25c for 30s -- magnet re-engage; eluate separated from beads
  Collection    : RNA-containing eluate driven to recovery site within Purification Module;
                  collected by pipetting for off-device analysis.
  Eluate volume : 10 uL (vs. 30 uL conventional; 3-fold reduction in elution volume)
  Total time    : ~20 min (vs. 45 min conventional; >2-fold faster)


QC AND DOWNSTREAM ANALYSIS
---------------------------

Step 8 | RNA Yield QC  [BioScript: BB1 -> BB2/BB3 -> BB4]
  Module    : rnaYieldDetection
  Method    : NanoDrop 2000 UV-vis spectrophotometry (A260) or Qubit 2.0 fluorometry (off-device)
  Duration  : 30 s detection
  Results   :
    25 uL blood: comparable yield (p=0.16, World-to-DMF vs. conventional)
    50 uL blood: comparable yield (p=0.45)
    100 uL blood: slightly better yield with World-to-DMF (p=0.03)
    All yields within literature range for fingerstick blood volumes.
  BioScript gate (BB2/BB3):
    - Both pass and fail branches: dispose(yieldSignal)
    - elutionMix NOT disposed in this gate; must remain live for purity detect in BB4
    - 'drain' keyword not emitted by lilott8/BioScript compiler; both branches use dispose

Step 9 | RNA Purity and Integrity QC  [BioScript: BB4 -> BB5/BB6 -> BB7]
  Module    : rnaPurityDetection
  Methods   :
    - A260/A280 ratio (NanoDrop 2000, off-device); acceptable >= 1.8
    - RNA integrity number (RIN; Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer, RNA 6000 Pico Chip)
    - Fragment size distribution (28S and 18S rRNA peaks confirmed by Bioanalyzer)
  Duration  : 30 s detection
  Results   : All preparations (World-to-DMF and conventional) comparably high quality
              (Table S-1, paper); RIN values comparable; 28S and 18S peaks present in all samples.
  BioScript gate (BB5/BB6):
    - Pass: puritySignal > 0 -> dispose(elutionMix); RNA preparation verified
    - Fail: puritySignal <= 0 -> dispose(elutionMix); low purity (salt/protein carryover)
    - Terminal BB7: dispose(puritySignal); NOP

Downstream Validation:
  qRT-PCR:
    Targets     : B2M, GAPDH, PPIB (housekeeping genes)
    Template    : Total RNA from 50 uL blood; SuperScript III reverse transcriptase;
                  TaqMan reagents + SsoFast Probes Supermix (BioRad)
    Replicates  : 4 independent experiments x 3 reactions per target per RNA sample (N=3 donors)
    Result      : Ct values essentially identical regardless of preparation method (p >= 0.07);
                  no statistically significant difference; expression levels match literature.

  RNA-Seq:
    Library     : Peregrine cDNA library preparation; HAC molecular normalisation (rRNA depletion)
    Sequencer   : Illumina MiSeq, 50 bp single-end, 15 pM, duplicated run
    QC          : 95-96% reads passed Qfilter; ~99% reads mapped to hg19 (UCSC) via TopHat v2.0.9
    Abundance   : FPKM calculated by Cufflinks v2.1.1
    Similarity  : R^2 ~0.85 (Log10 FPKM scatter) between World-to-DMF and conventional;
                  no strong bias imposed by RNA preparation method (Table S-2, Figures 4 and S-4)
    Conclusion  : World-to-DMF RNA is fully compatible with both PCR-based and non-PCR-based
                  analytical methods; PCR inhibitors present in blood are eliminated/inactivated.

Bugs fixed across three compiler iterations:
  Bug 1 -- Stale variable reuse (Iteration 1 -> Iteration 2):
    wash1Mix mixed with bridgingDroplet2[0] and wash2Mix/elutionMix mixed with bridgingDroplet1[0]
    after those variables had already been consumed as inputs to their successor mix calls.
    Fix: introduced beadCapture after dispose of bridgingDroplet3; threaded wash1Beads ->
    wash2Beads as linear chain through the bead-pellet progression.

  Bug 2 -- Phantom SSA variable elutionMix2[0] (Iteration 2 -> Iteration 3):
    elutionMix disposed inside both yield QC branches (BB2/BB3); then re-read by purity
    detect in BB4 after the join. Compiler created unreachable SSA version elutionMix2[0].
    Fix: removed dispose(elutionMix) from yield gate entirely; only yieldSignal disposed in
    BB2/BB3; elutionMix kept live across the join into BB4 purity detect.

  Bug 3 -- drain not emitted by compiler (Iteration 2 -> Iteration 3):
    BioScript compiler converts drain to dispose in IR output regardless of source keyword.
    Source used drain in else branches causing source/IR semantic mismatch.
    Fix: replaced all drain with dispose in both yield and purity QC false branches.
