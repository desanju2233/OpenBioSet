PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Purification of High Salinity Brine by Multi-Stage Ion Concentration Polarization Desalination
Source Link  : https://www.nature.com/articles/srep31850
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1038/srep31850
Journal      : Scientific Reports, 2016, Vol. 6, Article 31850
Authors      : Bumjoo Kim, Rhokyun Kwak, Hyukjin J. Kwon, Van Sang Pham, Minseok Kim,
               Bader Al-Anzi, Geunbae Lim, Jongyoon Han
Platform     : PDMS-based microfluidic ICP (Ion Concentration Polarization) desalination device
               with cation exchange membranes (CEMs) only — unipolar conduction


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a multi-stage Ion Concentration Polarization (ICP) electrical desalination
method for treating high-salinity brine (>35,000 ppm TDS), including produced water from shale
oil/gas development and brine concentrate from seawater desalination plants. ICP desalination
utilises two cation exchange membranes (CEMs) of the same polarity for unipolar ion conduction,
unlike conventional electrodialysis (ED) which uses both AEM and CEM in bipolar configuration.

The protocol is divided into three characterisation branches:
  1. Multi-stage trifurcated ICP desalination — 3 sequential ICP stages with intermediate
     stream recirculation; salt removal QC by conductivity and ICP-AES measurement
  2. Suspended solids removal — simultaneous TDS and TSS reduction from brine containing
     E. coli bacteria and crude oil emulsions; visualised by fluorescence microscopy
  3. Divalent cation removal — quantitative Na+ and Ca2+ removal confirmed by ICP-AES;
     relevant for produced water recycling and scaling prevention in wellbores

Key advantages of ICP over conventional technologies:
  - Treats brine up to 100,000 ppm TDS with salt rejection up to 70%
  - Removes both dissolved salts and suspended solids simultaneously (no pretreatment)
  - Less susceptible to membrane fouling/scaling (no AEM; pH shift occurs near AEM not CEM)
  - Partial desalination (50-75%) sufficient for EOR recycling and discharge
  - Cost < $1/bbl for source water up to 70,000 mg/L TDS; competitive with MSF and MVR


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Fabrication   : PDMS (Polydimethylsiloxane)-based microfluidic model system
                (for visualisation and concentration distribution tracing)
Membrane gap  : 1.5-2.0 mm
Channel depth : 0.2-0.6 mm
Channel length: 10-100 mm

CEMs          : Fumasep FTCM-E (FuMA-Tech GmbH, Germany)
                Area resistance: ~13.3 Ohm.cm2
                Note: High-resistance membrane used; significant improvement expected with
                lower-resistance membranes (power consumption strongly dependent on resistance)
AEM           : Fumasep FTCM-A (used in electrodialysis comparison experiments only)
Electrodes    : Carbon paper (Fuel Cell Store, Inc., Boulder, CO)
Ref electrodes: Ag/AgCl (A-M Systems, WA); penetrate channels vertically; measure
                membrane voltage drop per cell only (excludes electrode/reaction overpotential)

Plastic scale-up device: Assembled from CEMs, AEMs (ED only), carbon electrodes, and
                          laser-cut plastic components; channel depth 3 mm.

Output streams (trifurcated):
  Concentrate  : Top stream — ion-enriched; receives negatively charged suspended particles
  Intermediate : Middle stream — concentration ~C0; majority fluid; re-fed to next ICP stage
  Diluate      : Bottom stream — ion-depleted; final treated water product


REAGENTS
--------
Feed solutions:
  NaCl brine             : 0.1-1.7 M NaCl (5,800-100,000 mg/L TDS)
  Natural seawater       : Revere Beach, MA; general precipitation only, no prefiltration
                           (used for fouling/scaling durability experiments)

Electrode rinse          : Na2SO4 at same normality as feed NaCl solution
                           Purpose: Exclude electrode/reaction overpotential from
                           cell voltage measurement

Fluorescent tracers:
  ICP zone visualisation : Alexa Fluor 488, 1.5 uM (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA)
  Fluid tracing          : Fluorescent color coding red dye (Kingscote Chemicals, Inc., OH);
                           1:1000 volumetric ratio
  pH visualisation       : pH indicator dye (Micro Essential Laboratory, Inc., NY); 5 wt%

Bacterial model:
  Strain                 : E. coli K-12 MG1655
  Reporter               : Constitutively expressed GFP
  Purpose                : Visualise bacterial removal from diluate (Fig 7b)

Oil emulsion model:
  Oil                    : Crude oil (Greene, Co., PA)
  Buffer                 : 0.1 M NaCl
  Ratio                  : 1:50 (oil:buffer), sonicated 2 h
  Tracer                 : Fluorescent red dye 1:1000
  Purpose                : Visualise crude oil removal from diluate (Fig 7c)

Demineralised water      : CEM pre-conditioning soak (minimum 48 h before experiments)


EFFICIENCY METRICS
------------------
Current Utilization (CU):
  Formula : CU = zFQ_diluate(C0 - C_diluate) / NI
  Meaning : Fraction of applied current used for salt reduction in diluate flow
            (Ideally unity in ED with no current loss)
  ICP note: CU > 1 achievable with NaCl feed due to diffusivity difference
            DNa+ = 1.33e-9 m2/s; DCl- = 2.03e-9 m2/s; ICP with CEMs enhances
            salt removal ratio up to 20% compared to ED under same current

Energy per Ion Removal (EPIR):
  Formula : EPIR = (IV/Q_diluate) / (zk_BT(C0 - C_diluate)) proportional to V/CU
  Meaning : Specific energy consumed to remove one ion; proportional to voltage/CU
  Key finding: EPIR linearly proportional to salt removal ratio regardless of
               current density, flow velocity, or membrane length (Fig 3c)
               -> Optimal energy efficiency at minimum incremental salt removal ratio
               -> Drives multi-stage (batch) design philosophy


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | CEM Membrane Pre-Conditioning
  Reagents    : Demineralised water
  Operation   : Passive soak (abstracted as mix in BioScript)
  Duration    : Minimum 48 h at room temperature (25 C) = 2880m = 172800 s
  Purpose     : Equilibrate Fumasep FTCM-E CEMs before experiments
  BioScript   : mix cem_membrane with demineralizedWaterPrep for 2880m

Step 2 | Brine Feed Dispense
  Reagent     : NaCl brine, 0.1-1.7 M (5,800-100,000 ppm TDS)
  Flow rate   : ~20 uL/min via syringe pump (Harvard Apparatus PHD 2200), negative pressure
  Note        : Na2SO4 electrode rinse dispensed per-stage at point of use to avoid
                dead-code tokens in BioScript IR (rinse1, rinse2, rinse3)

Step 3 | Stage 1 — ICP Desalination (First Pass)
  Inputs      : Brine feed + conditioned CEM + Na2SO4 rinse (rinse1)
  ICP mix     : 10 s (flow-through separation abstraction)
  Actuation   : 25 C, 60 min = 3600 s (constant-current electrical actuation abstraction)
  Current     : 125-375 A/m2 constant; Keithley 236 source measurement unit
  Mechanism   : Ion depletion zone adjacent to CEMs; Na+ and Cl- separated into
                concentrate (top, enriched) and diluate (bottom, depleted)
  Trifurcated : Small diluate + majority intermediate (~C0) + small concentrate
  Salt removal: 5-20% per stage (optimal EPIR regime; Fig 3c)
  Rinse mix   : 10 s; stage1WithRinse fed to Stage 2
  Monitoring  : Conductivity probe (Microelectrode Inc.); Ag/AgCl reference electrodes

Step 4 | Stage 2 — ICP Desalination (Second Pass)
  Inputs      : stage1WithRinse (intermediate stream ~C0) + conditioned CEM + rinse2
  ICP mix     : 10 s
  Actuation   : 25 C, 60 min = 3600 s
  Current     : Adjusted per dilute production ratio p (506-1175 A/m2; Fig 5a table)
  Key feature : Feed concentration constant at ~C0 at every stage; system always in
                economically favourable high-salinity low-EPIR regime
                (unlike multi-stage ED where concentration drops stage-to-stage)
  Output      : stage2WithRinse fed to Stage 3

Step 5 | Stage 3 — ICP Desalination (Third Pass)
  Inputs      : stage2WithRinse (intermediate stream ~C0) + conditioned CEM + rinse3
  ICP mix     : 10 s
  Actuation   : 25 C, 60 min = 3600 s
  Output      : treatedDiluate (cumulative 50-75% TDS reduction)
  Overall     : System recovery fixed at 50% for cost comparison (SI-section 3.2.1)
  Justification: 3 stages is representative optimised configuration for high-salinity feed;
                 saltier feedwater requires more stages (SI-section 3.2.3)

Step 6 | Salt Removal QC Detection
  Input       : treatedDiluate
  Module      : saltRemovalDetection
  Duration    : 30 s
  Methods     : Flow-through conductivity probe (Microelectrode Inc., NH) for bulk TDS;
                CCD-based ICP-AES (ACTIVA-S, Horiba, Ltd., Japan) for cation quantification
  Pass (BB2)  : saltSignal > 0 -> TDS reduced 50-75%; dispose treatedDiluate
  Fail (BB3)  : saltSignal <= 0 -> TDS target not met; drain treatedDiluate
  Join (BB4)  : dispose saltSignal; proceed to TSS branch

Step 7 | Suspended Solids Removal Characterisation
  Inputs      : E. coli K-12 MG1655 (GFP-expressing) + crude oil emulsion (1:50, sonicated)
                + fresh NaCl brine + demineralised water
  Contaminant mix : 2 s each (sequential)
  ICP mix     : 10 s
  Actuation   : 25 C, 30 min = 1800 s
  Mechanism   : Negatively charged particles (negative zeta potential) migrate en bloc to
                concentrate stream (anode direction) by electrophoretic force + electroconvection
                Diluate exits particle- and salt-free simultaneously
  Module      : suspendedSolidsDetection
  Duration    : 30 s
  Detection   : Fluorescence microscopy (Olympus IX-71)
  Pass (BB5)  : solidsSignal > 0 -> TSS cleared from diluate; dispose tssSeparation
  Fail (BB6)  : solidsSignal <= 0 -> particles remain; drain tssSeparation
  Join (BB7)  : dispose solidsSignal; proceed to divalent cation branch
  Note        : Majority of bio-contaminants and colloidal particles in natural water carry
                negative zeta potentials; simultaneous TDS+TSS removal is key ICP advantage
                over conventional ED (requires separate pretreatment steps for TSS)

Step 8 | Divalent Cation Removal Characterisation
  Inputs      : Fresh NaCl brine + Na2SO4 rinse (rinseDivalent) + demineralised water
  Feed mix    : 5 s
  ICP mix     : 10 s
  Actuation   : 25 C, 30 min = 1800 s
  Target      : Na+ (monovalent) and Ca2+ (divalent) quantified in diluate
  Module      : divalentCationDetection
  Duration    : 30 s
  Method      : CCD-based ICP-AES (ACTIVA-S, Horiba, Ltd., Japan)
  Result      : Divalent cations removed proportionally or in excess of monovalent cations
                (SI-section 8); consistent with electrodialysis literature (refs 41-43)
  Significance: Prevents Ca2+/Mg2+/Ba2+/Sr2+ carbonate/sulfate precipitation in wellbore
                during produced water recycling for hydraulic fracturing reuse
  Pass (BB8)  : divalentSignal > 0 -> Na+/Ca2+ removal confirmed; dispose divalentSeparation
  Fail (BB9)  : divalentSignal <= 0 -> residual divalent ions above threshold; drain
  Terminal (BB10): dispose divalentSignal; NOP


MEMBRANE FOULING AND SCALING
-----------------------------
ICP advantage : Operates without AEMs; pH shift and scale formation (calcium deposits)
                occur near AEM due to water splitting (H+ and OH- production) not near CEM.
                ICP voltage largely constant over extended operation (natural seawater, 3 h,
                500 A/m2, no prefiltration) while ED voltage increases due to organic fouling.
ED comparison : CU reduction ratio higher in ED than ICP (Fig 8e); membrane fouling
                disrupts counter-ion transport and raises resistance.
pH observation: Significant acidification in dilute stream and basification in concentrate
                stream observed near AEM in ED (Fig 8a/b); not observed near CEM in ICP.
Test conditions: Natural seawater (Revere Beach, MA); no prefiltration or chemical
                 precipitation; constant current 500 A/m2; 3-channel stacked devices.


COST ANALYSIS
-------------
ICP target cost : < $1/bbl for source water up to 70,000 mg/L TDS at 70% salt removal;
                  linear dependence of overall cost on feed water salinity (Fig 6)
Capital cost    : Sum of membrane/equipment expenses and maintenance cost
                  (dominated by membrane area; ref 17, 25)
Operating cost  : Power consumption (other minor costs neglected; refs 17, 27, 28)
Optimal staging : More stages -> lower energy cost but higher capital cost;
                  optimum total cost at limit of many stages (incremental desalination)
                  Saltier feedwater -> more stages required (SI-section 3.2.3)

Competitor comparison (Fig 6):
  RO  : Practical limit ~45,000 ppm TDS; not viable for brine; pretreatment > $1/bbl
  MSF : Significant infrastructure; not competitive for partial desalination
  MVR : $3.50-6.25/bbl ($22-39/m3) total; $2-3/bbl operating only; complete desalination
  ICP : Cost (green zone, Fig 6) compares favourably over MSF and MVR for partial
        desalination target (50-75% salt removal)
