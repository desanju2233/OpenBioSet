PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Massively Parallel Concentration Device for Multiplexed Immunoassays
Source Link  : https://pubs.rsc.org/lc/article/11/7/1351/336806/Massively-parallel-concentration-device-for
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1039/c0lc00349b
Journal      : Lab on a Chip, 2011, Vol. 11, pp. 1351-1358
Platform     : PDMS-glass nanofluidic concentration device with Nafion nanoporous junction


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a massively parallel nanofluidic concentration device for multiplexed
and high-throughput biomolecule detection. By integrating a Nafion nanoporous junction between
a main sample microchannel and a buffer microchannel, ion concentration polarization (ICP) is
generated under an applied dc voltage. This creates an ion depletion zone on the anodic side of
the nanojunction and strongly repels charged biomolecules (CRP, fluorescein) into a concentrated
plug within each microchannel. The key innovation is that a single-inlet/single-outlet reservoir
design allows the device to be massively parallelized: up to 128 individually controlled
concentration channels are operated simultaneously from a single applied voltage bias, without
scaling the number of fluidic connections.

The protocol is divided into two functional sub-protocols:

  1. Multiplexed preconcentration characterisation: up to 128 microchannels operated
     at the same ET (uniform concentration factor, ~4000x in 30 min at 50-100 V) or at
     graded ET values via varying inlet microchannel length (serial concentration, 100-4000x
     in a single 16-channel device). Fluorescein is used as the fluorescent tracer.

  2. Bead-based multiplexed immunoassay: streptavidin microsbeads functionalized with
     biotin-anti-CRP antibody are trapped in DRIE pillar arrays within each microchannel.
     Alexa-488-labeled CRP is loaded into the device and electrokinetically concentrated
     at the bead trap for 30 min (70 V). After buffer wash, fluorescence signal on the
     beads is measured. Serial concentration (ch#1 to ch#16, graded factors) generates
     16 distinct binding curves from a single inlet CRP concentration, extending the
     dynamic range from 44.66 to 2874.60 (ULD/LOD) and improving the LOD from
     162.2 ng/mL (reference) to 2.52 ng/mL (ch#16).


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Fabrication:
  - Positive master mold: SU-8 photoresist patterned on silicon wafer; PDMS cast and cured
    at 65 C for 3 h; peeled from master.
  - Nanojunction: 20 wt% Nafion perflourinated ion-exchange resin (Sigma Aldrich) patterned
    by microflow patterning onto glass using a reversibly bonded PDMS channel
    (200 um wide x 50 um deep x 1 cm long); cured at 95 C for 5 min; PDMS removed.
  - Bonding: PDMS chip with concentration microchannels plasma-bonded onto the Nafion-
    patterned glass substrate.
  - Bead trap: DRIE (deep reactive-ion etching) fabricated pillar array for the bead-based
    immunoassay device (SU-8 resolution insufficient for pillar features).

Channel dimensions : 50 um wide x 15 um deep (sample microchannels)
Bead trap          : 100 um wide x 15 um deep; PDMS micro-pillar array
Nanojunction       : Nafion strip patterned perpendicular to microchannels
Electrodes         : Ag/AgCl (A-M Systems, Inc.)
Power supply       : Keithley 6487 dc power supply
Imaging            : Inverted fluorescence microscope (Olympus IX-51) + SensiCam CCD
                     (Cooke corp.); IPLab 3.6 image analysis (Scanalytics, Fairfax VA)

Multiplexing geometry:
  - Same-length channels (Fig. 2a): all microchannels have identical inlet length;
    same ET across all channels; uniform concentration factor.
  - Variable-length channels (Fig. 2b): inlet channels extended in zigzag pattern with
    different lengths; lower ET for longer channels; serial concentration factors 100-4000x
    across ch#1 (longest) to ch#16 (shortest).
  - Splitting/merging scheme at inlet/outlet matches hydraulic flow resistance.
  - Up to 128 channels demonstrated; single V_H and GND connection only.


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Bead Functionalization (off-chip)
  Reagents      : Streptavidin beads (Applied Biosystems, 6-8 um, 35e6 beads/mL),
                  biotin-conjugated anti-CRP (Abcam ab86246)
  Operation     : Mix beads with antibody; stir 18 h at room temperature
  Duration      : 18 h (= 1080 min; encoded as 1080m; h token unsupported in compiler)
  Post-process  : Centrifuge to remove excess antibody; wash 3x with 1x PBS;
                  dilute tenfold before loading
  Notes         : Biotin-streptavidin conjugation links anti-CRP to bead surface.
                  Bead diameter 6-8 um provides optimal trap fit (1-2 beads vertically)
                  and avoids blocking sample flow or requiring sub-micron pillar geometry.

Step 2 | Device Blocking with BSA
  Reagents      : 1% BSA in buffer
  Operation     : Flush through microchannel for 10 min at room temperature
  Duration      : 10 min (600 s)
  Purpose       : Passivates PDMS channel walls; prevents nonspecific CRP adsorption
                  that would produce false-positive fluorescence signal on beads.

Step 3 | Pre-loading Buffer Wash (3x)
  Reagents      : 10 mM sodium phosphate dibasic buffer (pH 8.4)
  Operation     : Positive pressure applied 5 min per wash; 3 cycles
  Purpose       : Remove BSA from channel before bead loading; establish buffer baseline.

Step 4 | Bead Loading into Pillar-Array Traps
  Reagents      : Functionalized bead suspension (diluted tenfold), 10 mM buffer
  Loading       : Positive pressure at anodic reservoir; beads flow into pillar trap zone
  Trap geometry : DRIE pillar array, 100 um x 15 um; traps 6-8 um beads between pillars;
                  1-2 beads per trap stack vertically
  Post-loading wash : Positive pressure at cathodic reservoir; 3x wash to remove clogged
                      (non-trapped) beads from pillar zone; equal bead number per channel
                      confirmed by bright-field microscopy before CRP loading
  Final wash    : 3x additional buffer wash after clog removal
  Preparation time : ~30 min total before CRP concentration step
  BioScript note: `mix` abstracts pressure-driven flow-through bead trapping;
                  no DMF electrode mixing occurs at this step.

Step 5 | Channel Fill with Alexa-488-Labeled CRP
  Reagents      : Alexa-488-labeled CRP (Invitrogen A10235 labeling kit) in 10 mM
                  sodium phosphate dibasic buffer, pH 8.4
  Concentrations tested (5 independent runs):
    run1 : 1 ug/mL
    run2 : 100 ng/mL
    run3 : 10 ng/mL    [representative concentration encoded in BioScript]
    run4 : 1 ng/mL
    run5 : 100 pg/mL
  Operation     : Fill microchannels via positive pressure at anodic reservoir
  Notes         : Two independent channel-arm droplets dispensed in BioScript:
                  ch16 (high-ET arm) and ch1 (low-ET arm), each CRP + buffer.

Step 6 | Electrokinetic Concentration - Channel 16 (ch16, ~4000x factor)
  Voltage       : 70 V dc (also tested at 50 V and 100 V; 70 V for immunoassay runs)
  Duration      : 30 min (1800 s)
  Mechanism     : Applied voltage across Ag/AgCl electrodes drives perm-selective ion
                  current through Nafion nanojunction; ion concentration polarization
                  depletes ions from anodic side, strongly repelling CRP toward anodic wall
                  and forming a concentrated CRP plug at the bead trap.
  Channel       : Ch16 has the shortest inlet microchannel; ET = deltaV / length is
                  maximised; ~4000x concentration factor achieved in 30 min.
  BioScript note: Encoded as `heat ... at 25c for 1800s`; standard abstraction for
                  voltage-driven concentration with no thermal component; 25c = ambient.

Step 7 | Electrokinetic Concentration - Channel 1 (ch1, ~100x factor)
  Voltage       : 70 V dc
  Duration      : 30 min (1800 s)
  Channel       : Ch1 has the longest inlet microchannel; lowest ET; ~100x concentration
                  factor. Represents the low-sensitivity end of the serial concentration
                  gradient and the low end of the extended dynamic range.
  Notes         : In the real device both arms concentrate simultaneously under the same
                  single applied voltage; serial modelling in BioScript reflects CFG
                  linearity constraint (no parallel execution primitives).

Step 8 | Post-Concentration Buffer Wash (3x per channel arm)
  Reagents      : 10 mM sodium phosphate dibasic buffer
  Operation     : Flush buffer through channel after concentration step
  Wash cycles   : 3 per arm (ch16 and ch1 washed independently in BioScript)
  Purpose       : Remove unbound bulk CRP from channel before fluorescence readout;
                  only bead-bound CRP signal is measured.
  Paper quote   : "the sample containing Alexa-488 labeled CRP was removed by flushing
                  sodium phosphate dibasic buffer solution through the device."

Step 9 | Fluorescence Detection of Bead-Bound CRP
  Instrument    : Inverted fluorescence microscope (Olympus IX-51) + SensiCam CCD;
                  IPLab 3.6 image analysis
  Fluorophore   : Alexa-488 (excitation ~490 nm, emission ~525 nm)
  Detection module : crpBindingDetection (BioScript)
  Detection duration : 30 s per channel arm
  Readout       : Fluorescent intensity on trapped beads; signal proportional to
                  amount of CRP bound to anti-CRP antibody on bead surface
  Background    : 40-50 AU (system baseline)
  Signal range  : 56-66 AU (run5, 100 pg/mL) to ~800 AU (run1, 1 ug/mL) at ch16
  QC gate (ch16): bindingSignal > 0 -> dispose; bindingSignal <= 0 -> drain
  QC gate (ch1) : ch1Signal > 0 -> dispose; ch1Signal <= 0 -> drain
  Notes         : Ch16 (shortest channel, ~4000x) gives highest signal and lowest LOD.
                  Ch1 (longest channel, ~100x) gives weakest signal; confirms low end
                  of dynamic range. Five repeats per run for statistical reliability.
                  Data from run2 and run4 omitted from binding curve plot as signal
                  ranges overlapped with neighbour sets.


RESULTS SUMMARY
---------------

Concentration Characterisation (fluorescein tracer, 0.01 ug/mL):
  16-channel uniform (50 V, same-length):
    Concentration factor : ~4000x in 30 min
    Median signal        : 1875.3 AU; SD 66.5 AU; uniformity error <5%
  128-channel uniform (100 V):
    Mean signal          : 1581.3 AU; SD 170.5 AU
  16-channel serial (50 V, variable-length):
    Factor range         : ~100x (ch#1) to ~4000x (ch#16)

Immunoassay Performance (4-parameter logistic fit, R2 > 0.95 for all curves):
  Without concentration (reference):
    LOD    : 162.2 ng/mL
    ULD    : 7244 ng/mL
    Dynamic range (ULD/LOD) : 44.66

  With concentration (ch#16, ~4000x factor):
    LOD    : 2.52 ng/mL
    ULD    : 7244 ng/mL
    Dynamic range (ULD/LOD) : 2874.60
    LOD improvement : ~64x vs reference

  Fitting equation : y = y0 + a / (1 + (x/x0)^b)
    Parameters a, b, x0 within 99% confidence; y0 > 98% confidence (Sigma Plot LSQ fit)

  - `heat` is void/in-place; does not consume the fluid variable; fillCh161 and

Serial concentration modelling note:
  The paper operates all 16 channels simultaneously under a single applied voltage.
  BioScript has no parallel execution or loop construct. Two representative channels
  are encoded explicitly: ch16 (shortest inlet, ~4000x, lowest LOD) and ch1 (longest
  inlet, ~100x, highest LOD). These bracket the dynamic range endpoints reported in
  Fig. 5b and Table 1. The QC gate is keyed on ch16 (bindingSignal), consistent with
  the paper's primary performance metric (LOD = 2.52 ng/mL at ch16).

Pellet re-suspension modelling note:
  After each centrifuge wash step the bead pellet is re-suspended in fresh buffer.
  The compiler has no native magnetic-separation or pellet primitive. Each re-suspension
  is modelled as a fresh dispense of StreptavidinBeads, consistent with the established
  convention used across this dataset.
