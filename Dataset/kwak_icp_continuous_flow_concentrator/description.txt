PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Continuous-Flow Biomolecule and Cell Concentrator by Ion Concentration Polarization
Source Link  : https://pubs.acs.org/ancham/article-pdf/83/19/7348/15036094/ac2012619.pdf
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1021/ac2012619
Journal      : Analytical Chemistry, 2011, Vol. 83, pp. 7348-7355
Platform     : Nanofluidic PDMS microfluidic chip with Nafion nanojunction (cation-selective membrane)
               coupled to syringe pump and high-voltage DC supply


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a continuous-flow nanofluidic concentrator for charged biomolecules
and cells, operating on the principle of ion concentration polarization (ICP) at a Nafion
cation-selective nanojunction. Unlike batch-type preconcentrators that trap analytes in a
stationary plug, this system achieves a steady-state concentrated stream that can be
delivered continuously to downstream sensors or reservoirs.

The device consists of a PDMS microchannel bonded to a Nafion-patterned glass substrate.
The main channel bifurcates into a narrow concentrated channel and a wider filtered channel.
A Nafion line is patterned at 30 degrees along the concentrated channel. When a voltage is
applied across the Nafion (V+) combined with Poiseuille flow (delta-P from a syringe pump),
an ICP depletion zone forms near the nanojunction. Charged biomolecules and cells are
blocked electrostatically by this depletion zone and guided hydrodynamically into the
concentrated channel, while the filtered channel carries essentially target-free buffer.

Concentration factors from 1 to ~200 are achievable by tuning the channel geometry:
  w' = (w1+w2)/w1  (width ratio; tested: 10, 20, 50, 100)
  l' = L1/L2       (length ratio; tested: 0.3, 0.7, 2)
  r  = 1 + R1/R2   (theoretical concentration factor from hydrodynamic resistance ratio)

The protocol is divided into three experimental arms, encoded sequentially in the BioScript:
  1. Fluorescein sodium salt / rGFP continuous-flow concentration demonstration
  2. FITC-lectin polarity assay (negatively charged at pH 8.7; positively charged at pH 7.4)
  3. Red blood cell (RBC) and E. coli co-concentration demonstration


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Substrate    : Clean glass slide; Nafion perfluorinated ion-exchange resin (20 wt%, Sigma-Aldrich)
               patterned using PDMS line mold (width 100 um, height 50 um).
PDMS layer   : Bonded to Nafion-patterned glass by plasma treatment.
               Fabricated by standard photolithography (SU-8 master) and soft lithography.

Channel dimensions (fluorescein/rGFP/cell experiments):
  Main channel width        : 200, 400, 1000, or 2000 um (varied across devices)
  Main channel height       : 15 um
  Inlet length              : 1 cm (inlet to bifurcated point)
  Concentrated channel width: 20 um (fixed; 10 um for FITC-lectin high-factor tests)
  Filtered channel width    : Determined by width ratio w' = (w1+w2)/w1

Channel dimensions (FITC-lectin experiments):
  Concentrated channel width: 10 um
  Concentrated channel height: 15 um
  Main channel width         : 200 or 500 um
  Channel lengths            : 5-10 mm (outlet reservoir location controls length)

Nafion junction angle        : 30 degrees to main channel axis
  Purpose: Generates a straight (not curved) ICP boundary along the junction,
           enabling predictable ICP boundary position and concentrated plug shape.

Voltage application:
  Inlet of main channel (0-150 V) to buffer channel (electrical ground) across Nafion junction.
  Concentrated channel and filtered channel outlets are electrically floated.
  Maximum voltage: 150 V (= 150 V/cm; ~1000x below PDMS breakdown voltage of ~25 V/um).
  Voltage/flow rate values adjusted to balance depletion force and hydrodynamic force.

Flow control:
  Syringe pump: Harvard Apparatus PHD 2200
  Flow rate range: 0-5 uL/min


TARGETS AND REAGENTS
--------------------

Fluorescein sodium salt
  Concentration  : 3 pM
  Buffer         : 1 mM dibasic sodium phosphate (pH 8.7)
  Supplier       : Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO
  Charge         : Negative at pH 8.7
  Calibration    : 0.03, 0.3, 3, 30 pM and 0.3, 3, 30 nM used as internal references

Recombinant GFP (rGFP)
  Concentration  : 3 pM
  Buffer         : 1 mM dibasic sodium phosphate (pH 8.7)
  Supplier       : BD Bioscience, Palo Alto, CA
  Charge         : Negative at pH 8.7
  Calibration    : 0.3, 3, 30 nM used as internal references

FITC-conjugated lectin from Lens culinaris (lentil)
  Concentration  : 5 ug/mL
  MW             : ~49 kDa
  pI             : ~8.0-8.8
  Supplier       : Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO
  Zeta potential measurement: Zetasizer nano ZS (Malvern)
  Calibration    : 0.5, 5, 50, 100, 500 ug/mL used as internal references

  Condition A (negatively charged):
    Buffer              : 1 mM dibasic sodium phosphate (pH 8.7)
    Zeta potential      : -18.8 mV
    Electrophoretic mob.: -1.464e-8 m2/V.s
    Charge state        : Negative (lectin is above pI)

  Condition B (positively charged):
    Buffer              : 1 mM dibasic sodium phosphate (pH 7.4)
    Zeta potential      : +3.35 mV
    Electrophoretic mob.: +0.263e-8 m2/V.s
    Charge state        : Positive (lectin is below pI)
    Additive            : 0.1% BSA (Sigma-Aldrich) to prevent nonspecific electrostatic
                          binding to negatively charged PDMS walls and Nafion

Red blood cells (RBCs)
  Source         : Human whole blood (Hoechst, 2000x dilution, Innovative Research)
  Diluent        : 1x PBS (~150 mM)
  Additive       : 0.2% Pluronic F108 (BASF, Cumberland, RI) to reduce osmosis hemolysis,
                   nonspecific binding, and cell aggregation
  Tracker dye    : Cell Tracker Orange CMRA (Molecular Probes, Eugene, OR) at 3 uM
                   (used for fluorescence tracking in Supporting Information video)
  Zeta potential : -13.3 mV (Zetasizer nano ZS, Malvern)
  Input density  : ~1.9e6 cells/mL (RBC-only experiment); ~7.3e5 cells/mL (co-experiment)

E. coli ER2738
  Supplier       : New England BioLabs Inc.
  Culture        : Single colony in LB-Tet medium (50 mL LB + 50 uL tetracycline 50 ug/mL)
                   at 37 C for 24 h; LB-Tet contains ~10 g/L NaCl (~171 mM)
  Buffer on chip : Mixture of RBCs in 1x PBS + E. coli in LB-Tet (1:10 v/v, ~168 mM)
  Zeta potential : -15.5 mV (Zetasizer nano ZS, Malvern)
  Input density  : ~3.2e7 cells/mL
  Note           : PDMS and E. coli share same polarity; nonspecific binding confirmed
                   negligible under experimental conditions

Phosphate buffer (ionic strength series)
  Concentrations tested: 1 mM, 10 mM, 100 mM (dibasic sodium phosphate)
  Purpose: Characterise ICP stability vs. ionic strength (Fig. 3)
  Note: Higher ionic strength expands ICP instability regime; higher electric field
        required to generate ICP boundary at high molar concentrations


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Sample Preparation (fluorescein arm)
  Reagents  : FluoresceinSodiumSalt, Phosphate_Buffer_1mM_pH87
  Operation : mix, 5 s
  Output    : sampleMix (3 pM fluorescein in 1 mM phosphate buffer pH 8.7)
  Note      : rGFP arm is identical; this file encodes fluorescein as primary demonstration.

Step 2 | Device Priming and Channel Equilibration
  Reagents  : Phosphate_Buffer_1mM_pH87, sampleMix
  Operation : mix (abstracts buffer flushing through microchannel), 2 min
  Flow rate : ~2 uL/min
  Purpose   : Equilibrate Nafion nanojunction; remove air bubbles; no chemical reaction.

Step 3 | ICP Continuous-Flow Concentration (fluorescein/rGFP)
  Operation : heat at 25 C for 30 min (models room-temperature voltage operation) +
              mix for 30 min (abstracts electrokinetic + hydrodynamic force balance)
  Voltage   : 80 V
  Flow rate : 5 uL/min
  Geometry  : w' = 50, l' = 0.7 (w1=20 um, w2=980 um, L1=6 mm, L2=9 mm)
  r_theor   : 62
  Results   : Fluorescence intensity 300 -> 800 AU (~50-fold enhancement for fluorescein)
              Filtered channel drops to ~260 AU (near noise; essentially zero target)
              Concentrated plug visible; ICP boundary stable >30 min of operation
  Note      : Experimental factor always lower than theoretical due to ICP-induced
              electroosmotic flow changes in concentrated stream (second electroosmotic
              component boosts concentrated flow velocity; accurate modeling not yet available).

Step 4 | Fluorescence Detection and Concentration Quantification
  Operation : detect (concentrationDetection), 30 s
  Instrument: Olympus IX-71 inverted epifluorescence microscope +
              Hamamatsu thermoelectrically cooled CCD camera
  Software  : Image Pro Plus 5.0 (Media Cybernetics)
  QC gate   : concSignal > 0 -> dispose (BB2, pass)
              concSignal <= 0 -> drain (BB3, fail; ICP unstable or no enhancement)

Step 5 | FITC-Lectin Sample Preparation (both polarities)
  Reagents  : FITC_Lectin_pH87 + Phosphate_Buffer_1mM_pH87 (negative arm)
              FITC_Lectin_pH74 + BSA_Solution + Phosphate_Buffer_1mM_pH74 (positive arm)
  Operation : mix, 5 s each
  Note      : BSA added only for positive (pH 7.4) arm to prevent nonspecific binding.

Step 6 | ICP Concentration of FITC-Lectin (both polarities)
  Operation : mix for 30 min (abstracts ICP concentration for each polarity arm)
  Flow rate : 1 uL/min
  Voltage   : 0-150 V (adjusted per geometry to maintain stable ICP boundary)
  Height    : 15 um (main channel), 10-20 um (concentrated channel)
  Geometries tested:
    12-fold : w'=10, l'=0.7, w1=20 um
    46-fold : w'=20, l'=0.7, w1=10 um  (encoded as representative condition)
    130-fold: w'=50, l'=0.7, w1=10 um
  Key finding:
    Positive FITC-lectin (pH 7.4) shows larger shortfall from theoretical factor than
    negative lectin (pH 8.5), especially at low concentrated flow velocity (130-fold case).
    Cause: Electrostatic nonspecific binding to negatively charged PDMS and Nafion.
    BSA partially mitigates but cannot fully suppress at slow flow rates.
    At faster flow (12-fold case): no significant difference between polarities.
  QC gate   : polaritySignal > 0 -> dispose both (BB5, pass)
              polaritySignal <= 0 -> drain both (BB6, fail)

Step 7 | RBC and E. coli Sample Preparation
  Reagents  : RedBloodCells, EColi_ER2738, PBS_150mM, PluronicF108, LB_Tet_Medium
  Operation : Sequential mix steps, 5 s each
  Outputs   : rbcMix (RBCs in 1x PBS + 0.2% Pluronic F108)
              ecoliMix (E. coli in 1x PBS + LB-Tet 1:10)
              cellMix (RBCs + E. coli co-mixed)
  Input densities: RBC ~7.3e5 cells/mL; E. coli ~3.2e7 cells/mL

Step 8 | ICP-Based Continuous-Flow Cell Concentration
  Operation : heat at 25 C for 2 min + mix for 30 min
              (abstracts ICP concentration of cell stream at Nafion junction)
  Voltage   : 14 V (co-experiment, Fig. 6); 90 V (RBC-only, Fig. 5)
  Flow rate : 0.1 uL/min (co-experiment); 5 uL/min (RBC-only)
  Geometry  : w'=10, l'=1.2 (20-fold concentrator)
  Results (co-experiment, Fig. 6):
    RBC  : 7.3e5 -> 1.4e7 cells/mL (19-fold; matches r_theoretical = 20)
    E. coli: 3.2e7 -> 6.5e8 cells/mL (20-fold; matches r_theoretical = 20)
    Leakage rate: 0% over 120 s live imaging
  Quantification:
    Hemocytometer (SKC Inc.): sample flow and filtered flow density
    ImageJ phase contrast: concentrated channel cell counting
    (Hemocytometer not usable for concentrated channel: ~0.05 uL/min output;
     200 min needed to collect 10 uL minimum volume)
  QC gate   : cellSignal > 0 -> dispose concentratedCells (BB8, pass)
              cellSignal <= 0 -> drain concentratedCells (BB9, fail)


IONIC STRENGTH CHARACTERISATION
--------------------------------
Buffers tested  : 1 mM, 10 mM, 100 mM dibasic sodium phosphate
Analyte         : 3 pM fluorescein sodium salt
Geometry range  : w' = 10, 20, 50, 100; l' = 0.3, 0.7, 2 (30 deg inclined Nafion)
Finding         : Concentration factors proportional to w' and l' as expected.
                  Experimental factors always below theoretical (eq. 3).
                  Higher ionic strength -> ICP instability regime expands (gray zone, Fig. 3).
                  Instability: ICP boundary fluctuations increase with electric field and
                  concentration gradient; disrupts continuous concentrated flow at high w'/l'.
                  1 mM buffer recommended for stable high-factor operation.


COMPARISON: BATCH-TYPE vs CONTINUOUS-FLOW
------------------------------------------
Batch-type preconcentrator:
  Max factor       : ~1e6-fold (Wang et al., 2005)
  Limitation       : Low throughput; unstable after concentration stops; diffusive dispersion
                     makes plug delivery to downstream sensors difficult.

This continuous-flow system:
  Max factor       : ~200-fold (experimental)
  Advantages       : Steady-state concentrated stream maintained continuously in downstream
                     channel or reservoir; no dispersion loss; seamless sensor integration;
                     target-independent (concentration factor set only by channel geometry);
                     sample flow rate ~few uL/min without downstream limitation.

IR output       : 10 basic blocks (BB1-BB10), 3 if/else QC gates, 12 CFG edges
DAG             : kwak_icp_continuous_flow_concentrator_main_dag.dot
Duration units  : All durations in seconds (s) or minutes (m) per compiler spec.
                  30 min ICP operation -> 30m (IR: 1800.0 SECOND)
                  2 min priming        -> 2m  (IR: 120.0 SECOND)
Modules         : concentrationDetection, icpBoundaryDetection, cellCountDetection
Abstraction notes:
  `mix`  abstracts hydrodynamic + electrokinetic force balance at Nafion junction
         (continuous-flow ICP concentration event; no reagent mixing in DMF sense).
  `heat at 25c` models room-temperature operation during voltage application
         (no actual thermal step in paper; used to anchor the ICP operation time window).
  `detect` abstracts epifluorescence CCD readout (Olympus IX-71 + Hamamatsu CCD)
         and ImageJ / hemocytometer cell counting.
Protocol scope  : Three sequential experimental arms encoded in one .bs file:
                  (1) Fluorescein/rGFP ICP concentration (primary demonstration)
                  (2) FITC-lectin polarity assay (pH 8.7 negative + pH 7.4 positive)
                  (3) RBC + E. coli co-concentration
                  rGFP arm follows identical protocol to fluorescein; not separately encoded.
                  RBC-only experiment (Fig. 5) shares same geometry as co-experiment (Fig. 6);
                  co-experiment (Fig. 6) encoded as the representative cell concentration arm.
