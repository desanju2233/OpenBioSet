PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Multiplexed Proteomic Sample Preconcentration Device Using Surface-Patterned Ion-Selective Membrane
Source Link  : https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2394188/pdf/nihms46491.pdf
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1039/b717900f
Journal      : Lab Chip, 2008, Vol. 8, No. 4, pp. 596-601
Platform     : PDMS microfluidic chip with surface-patterned planar Nafion ion-selective membrane (PDMS/glass)


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a high-throughput proteomic sample preconcentration method in PDMS
microfluidic chip format using a surface-patterned submicron Nafion ion-selective membrane.
The key innovation is the direct printing of a Nafion permselective membrane onto a glass
substrate by micro-flow patterning, followed by single-step integration into the device via
standard O2 plasma bonding. This eliminates the need for complicated nanochannel fabrication
(as in prior silicon micro/nanofluidic devices) and cumbersome thin-film integration processes.

The preconcentration mechanism is electrokinetic trapping, exploiting the ion-selectivity of
the Nafion membrane to generate a depletion region in the sample microchannel. Charged
molecules (proteins, peptides, or any charged species regardless of size) are concentrated
in front of the depletion boundary by the combined action of a normal electric field (En)
across the membrane and a tangential electroosmotic flow (EOF) along the sample channel.

Key performance metrics:
  - Concentration factor: ~10^4 in 5 min from 400 pM starting concentration
  - Concentration factor: ~5x10^4 in 10 min from 40 pM starting concentration
  - Concentrated plug volume: ~200 pL per single unit
  - 12x faster than prior PDMS preconcentrator (junction gap breakdown method: ~10^4 in 1 h)
  - 350x larger concentrated volume vs. prior silicon nanochannel device (few pL)
  - 10-unit array: ~2 nL concentrated volume (3500x vs. silicon device); compatible with
    mass spectrometer interfacing via electrospray or droplet dispensing
  - Coefficient of variance: 11.5% across 3 devices (reasonable given pattern alignment inaccuracy)

The Nafion membrane is applicable to any charged molecule; the preconcentration platform is
general and not limited to specific biomarkers or antibody-antigen interactions.


SEPARATION PHYSICS
------------------
The device operates in two sequential electrical phases:

Phase 1 - Depletion mode (V1=50V, V2=50V, buffer GND):
  A voltage difference is applied between the sample channel and the flanking buffer channels
  through the planar Nafion membrane. The normal field component (En) perpendicular to the
  membrane depletes ions in the sample channel and drives charged molecules away from the
  membrane toward the V1 reservoir. A stable depletion region forms and grows with time.

Phase 2 - Concentration mode (V1=50V, V2=25V, buffer GND):
  V2 is reduced to 25V; Vdiff = V1-V2 = 25V (optimum). The tangential field component (Et)
  along the sample channel generates an electroosmotic flow (EOF) that injects molecules from
  the bulk toward the depletion boundary. Molecules stack in front of the depletion zone,
  forming a stable concentrated plug at equilibrium distance Y2 from the Nafion membrane.
  The balance of En (depletion force) and Et (electroosmotic injection) determines plug position.

Optimum Vdiff = 25V: at higher Vdiff, secondary nonlinear electrokinetics near the membrane
destabilize the concentrated zone. Preconcentration factor and depletion region size both
increase monotonically with Vdiff up to 25V (Fig. 5 of paper).

Ion selectivity mechanism: Nafion's cluster network reduces the effective pore size, while
sulfonate groups (-SO3H) provide high negative charge density, achieving permselectivity
superior to prior PDMS nanojunction approaches. No voltage breakdown limit applies (unlike
silicon oxide-gated nanochannels), allowing higher applied voltages and faster concentration.


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Material         : PDMS bonded to Nafion-patterned glass substrate (25 mm x 75 mm slides)
Overall size     : 20 mm x 25 mm
Channel geometry : All channels 50 um deep x 50 um wide
Layout           : Dual gate design
                   - Center: sample channel (beta-PE protein in phosphate buffer; red in Fig. 4a)
                   - Flanking left and right: two buffer channels (phosphate buffer; green in Fig. 4a)
                   - Nafion stripe printed on glass connects all three channels at channel bottom
                     as planar permselective junction
Nafion membrane  :
  Material       : Nafion perfluorinated ion-exchange resin (Sigma-Aldrich), 5 wt% in
                   lower aliphatic alcohols/H2O, 15-20% water content
  Target thickness: 191-400 nm; measured 191 nm by SEM (Fig. 4b/c)
  Critical limit : >400 nm causes PDMS sealing failure; leakage at membrane edges compromises
                   ion selectivity and preconcentration efficiency/reproducibility
Electrode config : Pt wire electrodes inserted into channel reservoirs via punched access holes

Array format (10-unit):
  10 single preconcentrators arranged radially around a common collection reservoir (center).
  Sample reservoirs S1-S10 each at V1=50V; common reservoir at V2=25V; individual ground
  reservoirs per buffer channel. Nafion patterned in half-circle shape per unit.
  Concentrated volume: ~2 nL (10 x ~200 pL); sufficient for pipette transfer or direct
  electrospray to mass spectrometer.


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Nafion Membrane Patterning - Micro-flow Patterning (Preferred Method)
  Reagents    : Nafion perfluorinated resin (Sigma-Aldrich, 5 wt% in aliphatic alcohols/H2O)
  Volume      : 1 uL per pattern
  Method      : (1) Place 1 uL Nafion resin at reservoir of reversibly bonded temporary PDMS
                    channel (120 um deep x 200 um wide); capillary force fills channel immediately.
                (2) Apply negative pressure via syringe tubing at opposite channel end; flush
                    liquid resin out, leaving thin Nafion residual layer on hydrophilic glass.
                (3) Cure patterned stripe on hotplate at 95 C for 10 min.
  Target      : 191-400 nm; thickness controlled by applied negative pressure magnitude
  Reproducibility: Consistent at constant pressure; CV lower than micro-stamping alternative
  BioScript   : nafionResin self-mix (flow-coat abstraction) + heat at 95c for 10m

  Alternative - Micro-stamping Method:
  (1) Plasma-treat PDMS microstamp surface to improve Nafion wetting.
  (2) Ink PDMS microstamp with Nafion resin.
  (3) Transfer Nafion to clean pre-cleaned glass slide (IPA + DI water wash before printing).
  (4) Cure at 95 C for 10 min on hotplate.
  Thickness 0.5-1 um (less reproducible; pressure-distribution-dependent; risk of leakage).

Step 2 | PDMS Chip Plasma Bonding and Post-Bond Bake
  Method      : Standard O2 plasma bonding; PDMS chip placed on top of Nafion-patterned glass
  Alignment   : Not critical; performed without microscope
  Post-bond   : Hotplate 70 C for 12 h (720 min) to enhance bonding strength
  Result      : Elastic PDMS conforms adequately to rounded Nafion membrane profile at <=400 nm
                thickness (Fig. 4c SEM cross-section confirms membrane sealed between PDMS and glass)
  BioScript   : heat nafionCured at 70c for 720m (IR: 43200.0 SECOND)

Step 3 | Buffer Channel Loading
  Reagents    : 10^-2 M phosphate buffer pH 7
  Channels    : Both flanking buffer channels (dual gate); kept at GND during operation
  Duration    : 30 s loading (encoded as mix)
  Purpose     : Dual buffer channels generate depletion zones from both sides of the sample
                channel simultaneously, improving depletion region stability vs. single buffer
                channel design
  BioScript   : bufferLeft + bufferRight dispensed separately; mixed into bufferDual

Step 4 | Sample Loading
  Reagents    : beta-Phycoerythrin (beta-PE; MW 240 kDa, pI 4.3) in 10^-2 M phosphate buffer pH 7
  Concentrations tested: 40 pM, 400 pM, 4 nM, 40 nM
  Canonical   : 4 nM (Fig. 6 time-course experiment)
  Channel     : Central sample microchannel; both ends accessible for V1/V2 electrode connections
  Internal reference standards: 2 uM, 4 uM, 8 uM beta-PE for concentration factor calculation
  BioScript   : proteinSample dispensed; mixed with bufferDual into loadedSample

Step 5 | Depletion Mode (Phase 1)
  Voltage     : V1=50V (sample inlet), V2=50V (sample outlet), buffer channels GND
  Duration    : 1 min to establish and stabilize depletion region
  Physics     : En (normal field across Nafion) depletes ions in sample channel; negatively
                charged beta-PE (pI 4.3 < pH 7.0) pushed toward V1 reservoir; depletion zone
                visible as dark region by fluorescence microscopy
  BioScript   : mix loadedSample with bufferDual for 1m + heat depletedSample at 50c for 1m
                (50c temperature token encodes V1=50V; BioScript has no electrical primitive)

Step 6 | Concentration Mode (Phase 2)
  Voltage     : V1=50V, V2=25V (Vdiff = 25V optimum), buffer channels GND
  Duration    : 5 min (canonical; ~10^4 fold from 400 pM; extend to 10 min for 40 pM)
  Physics     : Et (tangential field along sample channel) drives EOF injecting molecules toward
                depletion boundary; molecules stack in front of depletion zone; En and Et reach
                equilibrium forming stable concentrated plug at distance Y2 from Nafion membrane
  Performance :
    40 nM     : Saturation in ~2 min
    4 nM      : Plug visible at 1 min; grows to 200 um plug at 10 min
    400 pM    : ~2x10^4 fold increase (8 uM reference) in 5 min
    40 pM     : ~5x10^4 fold increase in 10 min; fluorescence detection enabled
  Plug volume : ~200 pL per single preconcentrator unit
  CV          : 11.5% across 3 devices
  BioScript   : mix depletedSample with bufferDual for 5m + heat concentratedPlug at 25c for 5m
                (25c temperature token encodes V2=25V; unique variable avoids reassignment)

Step 7 | Fluorescence Detection and Quantification
  Platform    : Olympus IX71 inverted epifluorescence microscope
  Camera      : Thermoelectrically cooled CCD (Hamamatsu Co., Japan)
  Software    : IPLab (Scanalytics, Fairfax, VA)
  Acquisition : Every 30 s throughout concentration mode
  Shutter     : Mechanical shutter (Olympus) blocks UV between acquisitions (anti-photobleaching)
  Filter      : Neutral density filter prevents CCD saturation; increases dynamic range
  Reference   : 2, 4, 8 uM beta-PE internal standards for concentration factor calculation
  Pass        : Fluorescence intensity >= 8 uM reference (>=~10^4 fold from 400 pM at 5 min)
  BioScript   : detect preconcentrationDetection on concentratedPlug for 5m
                Branch BB2 (pass/dispose) vs BB3 (fail/drain -> membrane leak or voltage failure)


ARRAY OPERATION (10-Unit Format)
---------------------------------
The surface patterning technique allows deposition of Nafion anywhere on the glass substrate,
enabling massively parallel arrays. For the 10-unit radial array (Fig. 7):
  - 10 identical single preconcentrators oriented radially toward a common collection reservoir
  - Nafion patterned in half-circle shape on glass substrate per unit
  - Sample reservoirs S1-S10 each biased at V1=50V
  - Common reservoir (collection center) at V2=25V
  - Individual ground reservoirs per buffer channel for equal electric field conditions
  - All 10 units operated simultaneously; concentrated plugs released toward center
  - Total concentrated volume: ~2 nL (10 x ~200 pL)
  - 3500x larger than prior silicon nanochannel preconcentrator result
  - Sufficient for direct electrospray ionization to mass spectrometer or pipette transfer

BioScript note: BioScript has no loop construct; the 10-unit array requires explicit unrolling
of 10 identical BB1 blocks. The current .bs file encodes the canonical single-unit protocol.
A separate .bs file with explicit 10-unit unrolled structure is a candidate for future encoding.
