PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : A Digital Microfluidic Approach to Heterogeneous Immunoassays
Source Link  : https://microfluidics.utoronto.ca/papers/DMF%20immunoassays%201.pdf
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1007/s00216-010-4368-2
Journal      : Analytical and Bioanalytical Chemistry, 2011, Vol. 399, pp. 337-345
Platform     : Digital microfluidics (DMF) on EWOD electrode array


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a heterogeneous sandwich immunoassay for Human IgG implemented
on a digital microfluidic (DMF) device. The digital approach manipulates samples and
reagents as discrete droplets on a 2-D array of electrodes, as opposed to the streams of
fluid used in microchannels. Capture antibody (Fc-specific anti-human IgG) is physisorbed
directly onto the hydrophobic Cytop/ITO top plate of the DMF device, and all incubation
and wash steps are performed via DMF actuation at 75 V_rms, 18 kHz -- eliminating the need
for beads, magnets, BSA blocking, or a carrier oil interface.

The protocol is divided into two phases:
  1. Off-device: capture antibody spot immobilization on the Cytop/ITO top plate
  2. On-device: DMF-actuated antigen incubation, rinse, detection antibody incubation,
     final rinse, and fluorescence plate reader readout

Key advantages over conventional 96-well plate immunoassays:
  - Analysis time reduced from up to 2 days to 2.5 h total
  - Sample and reagent volumes reduced ~100-fold (1 uL vs 100 uL per assay)
  - Waste volumes reduced ~3 orders of magnitude (4 uL vs 3.5 mL per assay)
  - Diffusion-limited kinetics eliminated by active droplet transport
  - BSA blocking step eliminated by Pluronic F-127 additives
  - Dynamic range ~1 order of magnitude (R^2 = 0.9964); reproducibility %RSD < 25%
  - Multiplexed devices (6-10 platforms wired in series) enable parallel replicate assays


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Bottom plate : Patterned chromium electrodes on glass substrate (80 St George St. fabrication
               facility, University of Toronto). Two designs used:
               - Design A: 10 x 1x1 mm actuation electrodes + 2 x 2.5x2.5 mm reservoir electrodes
               - Design B: 20 x 1x1 mm actuation electrodes + 4 x 2.5x2.5 mm reservoir electrodes
               Inter-electrode gap: 40-75 um.
               Coated with 2 um parylene C (vapor deposition, Specialty Coating Systems) +
               130 nm Cytop (2.25% w/w in CT-SOLV180, spin-coated 1000 RPM 60 s,
               post-baked 180 C 15 min).

Top plate    : Unpatterned ITO-coated glass (Delta Technologies Ltd, Stillwater MN).
               Coated with 130 nm Cytop (as above).
               Pre-spotted with capture antibody before device assembly.
               Discarded after each assay; bottom plate reused.

Spacer       : Two pieces of double-sided tape, ~150 um thick.
               Defines the gap between top and bottom plates and sets droplet volume.

Droplet volume : 1 uL (sandwiched between plates ~150 um apart).

Hydrophobic coating note:
  Teflon-AF (50 nm, standard DMF coating) was found too fragile for surface-based assays.
  Antibody spot printing caused flaws and tears in Teflon-AF, preventing DMF actuation.
  Cytop (130 nm) was selected for its greater mechanical ruggedness -- spots can be
  dried and rinsed without causing the hydrophobic layer to delaminate.

Multiplexing : 6 or 10 DMF platforms wired in series on a single device. Homologous
               electrodes bussed together for simultaneous control of replicate assays.
               Devices placed on a multiwell plate for fluorescence measurement.

Actuation    : 75 V_rms, 18 kHz (function generator output amplified); applied manually
               to exposed contact pads on bottom plate surface.
               Monitored by CCD camera on stereomicroscope with fluorescence imaging
               (Olympus Canada, Markham ON).

Detection    : PheraStar multiwell plate reader (BMG Labtech, Durham NC).
               lambda_ex = 485 nm, lambda_em = 520 nm, focal height = 12.1 mm.
               Device remains on multiwell plate throughout assay; inserted into
               plate reader after all DMF steps are complete.


REAGENTS
--------
- Capture antibody : Fc-specific anti-human IgG (goat); Sigma Chemical, Oakville ON
                     0.22-2.2 mg/mL; 250-500 nL per spot
- Detection antibody: Fab-specific FITC-labeled anti-human IgG (goat); Sigma Chemical
                     FITC label: ex 485 nm / em 520 nm
- Antigen           : Human IgG, reagent grade from serum; Sigma Chemical
- Pluronic F-127    : 0.00625% w/v in all standard solutions (0.0113% in diluted serum)
                     Prevents non-specific protein adsorption; enables droplet movement;
                     replaces BSA blocking step used in conventional well-plate methods
- Rinse buffer      : 10 mM sodium phosphate pH 7.4 + 0.00625% Pluronic F-127 in 18 MOhm cm DI water
- BSA               : NOT used on DMF device; Pluronic F-127 serves equivalent function
- Bovine serum      : 100x diluted, spiked with human IgG 0-10 ug/mL; Sigma Chemical
                     Resulting bovine IgG ~100 ug/mL (100-fold excess over highest human IgG spike)
- Parylene C dimer  : Specialty Coating Systems, Indianapolis IN
- Cytop resin       : CT-809M in CT-SOLV180; Bellex International, Wilmington DE
- Tween 20          : Sigma Chemical, Oakville ON


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Capture Antibody Immobilization (Pre-Assembly, Off-Device)
  Reagents    : Fc-specific anti-human IgG (0.22-2.2 mg/mL), 0.1% Pluronic F-127 rinse
  Operation   : Manual pipetting of 250-500 nL onto unpatterned Cytop/ITO glass slide
  Post-spot   : Air-dry; rinse in 0.1% w/v Pluronic F-127 in DI water; air-dry before use
  Substrate   : Cytop/ITO top plate (unpatterned)
  Notes       : Concentration chosen to ensure surface saturation in spot area.
                Top plate chosen over bottom plate for three reasons:
                (1) Top plate simpler to fabricate and replace than patterned bottom plate.
                (2) Printing/drying/rinsing process can damage bottom plate insulator,
                    causing electrolysis susceptibility.
                (3) Spot area ~ actuation electrode area but small relative to ground electrode;
                    less likely to interfere with actuation when on top plate.
                Using top plate extends bottom plate lifetime for multiple assay reuse.

Step 2 | Device Assembly and Reservoir Loading
  Operation   : Assemble top and bottom plates with spacer; load reservoirs
  Droplet vol : 1 uL per aliquot (antigen sample + rinse buffer loaded before assembly)
  Spacer      : ~150 um double-sided tape

Step 3 | Sample Incubation -- Antigen Binding to Capture Spot
  Reagents    : HumanIgG_Sample (1 uL), AntiHumanIgG_Capture (surface-bound)
  Operation   : DMF actuation -- 10-pass cyclic translation across capture antibody spot
  Duration    : 10 min
  Passes      : 10 (back-and-forth across spot)
  Actuation   : 75 V_rms, 18 kHz
  Notes       : Active transport delivers antigen to capture surface without diffusion
                limitation. Diffusion distances ~100x smaller than well plate.
                Dynamic system also reduces non-specific adsorption relative to static
                well-plate incubation. 10 passes used to eliminate variance from fluid
                movement timing; Fig. 3 shows saturation after only 2 passes, but 10
                passes used for protocol robustness (Fig. 2a/b).

Step 4 | Sample Removal
  Operation   : Withdraw sample droplet to reservoir; manually remove waste droplet
                with laboratory tissue (Fig. 2b)
  Notes       : Waste contains unbound Human IgG and matrix proteins.

Step 5 | First Rinse -- Removal of Non-Specifically Bound IgG
  Reagents    : PluronicF127_RinseBuffer (1 uL), AntiHumanIgG_Capture (surface-bound)
  Operation   : DMF actuation -- 2-pass rinse across capture spot
  Duration    : 2 min
  Passes      : 2
  Actuation   : 75 V_rms, 18 kHz
  Notes       : 10 mM phosphate pH 7.4 + 0.00625% Pluronic F-127. Fig. 3 (inset) shows
                non-specific adsorption to native surface and BSA spots is very low;
                2 passes sufficient for NSB removal without displacing specifically bound
                antigen. Rinse waste droplet removed to reservoir.

Step 6 | Device Reload for Detection Antibody
  Operation   : Replace Cytop/ITO top plate; reload bottom plate reservoirs with 1-uL
                aliquots of FITC-Fab-specific anti-human IgG and rinse buffer (Fig. 2c)
  Notes       : Cover plate replaced to expose fresh reservoir area.

Step 7 | Detection Antibody Incubation
  Reagents    : FITC_AntiHumanIgG_Detection (1 uL), AntiHumanIgG_Capture (surface-bound)
  Operation   : DMF actuation -- 10-pass cyclic translation across assay spot
  Duration    : 10 min
  Passes      : 10
  Actuation   : 75 V_rms, 18 kHz
  Reynolds No : ~210 (laminar flow)
  Notes       : FITC-Fab-specific anti-human IgG delivered to spot and cycled 10 times
                (Fig. 2c/d). Completes the sandwich: capture antibody -- antigen -- detection
                antibody. Binding saturation reached after 2 passes (Fig. 3); 10 passes
                used for robustness. Pluronic F-127 in droplet does not strip previously
                bound antibody. Bulk droplet retains far more FITC-anti-IgG than bound by
                surface -- spot size and antigen density are limiting factors.
                Laminar flow (Re ~210); spot captures antibody from fluid layer in direct
                contact with surface per pass.

Step 8 | Detection Antibody Removal
  Operation   : Withdraw detection antibody droplet to reservoir; manually remove waste
                with laboratory tissue (Fig. 2d)

Step 9 | Final Rinse -- Removal of Unbound Detection Antibody
  Reagents    : PluronicF127_RinseBuffer (1 uL), AntiHumanIgG_Capture (surface-bound)
  Operation   : DMF actuation -- 2-pass final rinse across assay spot
  Duration    : 2 min
  Passes      : 2
  Actuation   : 75 V_rms, 18 kHz
  Notes       : Critical step. Residual unbound FITC-anti-IgG in droplet would produce
                high background fluorescence at plate reader, elevating baseline and
                reducing signal-to-noise. Rinse waste droplet removed to reservoir
                (Fig. 2e/f).

Step 10 | Fluorescence Detection
  Operation   : detect (plate reader fluorescence measurement)
  Instrument  : PheraStar multiwell plate reader (BMG Labtech, Durham NC)
  Settings    : lambda_ex = 485 nm, lambda_em = 520 nm, focal height = 12.1 mm
  Duration    : 30 s
  Notes       : Device (still on multiwell plate) inserted into plate reader for FITC
                fluorescence readout from sandwich complex immobilized on capture antibody
                spot on top plate. After detection, Cytop/ITO top plate discarded; patterned
                bottom plate retained for reuse in subsequent experiments.
                Comparison assay: same protocol run in 96-well plates with 100 uL aliquots
                using published commercial protocol (Amplex ELISA kit); same absolute mass
                amounts of antigen used on DMF and well plate for direct comparison.


ASSAY PERFORMANCE
-----------------
Dynamic range      : ~1 order of magnitude (both DMF and 96-well plate methods)
DMF R^2            : 0.9964 (stronger linear correlation than well plate)
Well plate R^2     : 0.9839
Reproducibility    : %RSD < 25% per data point (3-5 replicates); well plate slightly lower variance
Analysis time      : 2.5 h (DMF) vs up to 2 days (conventional 96-well plate)
Sample volume      : 1 uL (DMF) vs 100 uL (well plate) -- ~100-fold reduction
Waste volume       : ~4 uL/DMF spot vs ~3.5 mL/well -- ~3 orders of magnitude reduction
Serum validation   : Human IgG detected in 100x diluted bovine serum with 100-fold excess
                     bovine IgG; no significant cross-reactivity; linear standard curve obtained
LOD/LOQ            : Not yet determined; preliminary data show assay can run at concentrations
                     at least 1 order of magnitude lower without sacrificing reproducibility
                     or dynamic range (data not shown in paper)

                 30 s detection     -> 30s  (IR: 30.0 SECOND)

v2 fix (use-after-dispose):
  In v1, antigenBound was disposed after sample removal but then referenced again in
  rinseStep1 (STEP 5) and sandwichComplex (STEP 7), creating a use-after-dispose in
  BioScript's data-flow model. Fixed in v2 by introducing captureSpot as a persistent
  variable (never disposed) that models the physical antibody spot on the ITO top plate.
  All four mix operations (antigenBound, rinseStep1, sandwichComplex, rinseStep2) now
  reference captureSpot directly. Only liquid droplet variables are disposed.
  IR confirmed clean: captureSpot1[0] referenced across all four mix calls without
  any intervening dispose.
