PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Clathrate Nanostructures for Mass Spectrometry
Source Link  : https://masspec.scripps.edu/research/pdf/northen_nature06195.pdf
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06195
Journal      : Nature, 2007, Vol. 449, pp. 1033-1036
Platform     : Nanostructure-Initiator Mass Spectrometry (NIMS) -- porous silicon surface
               with trapped perfluorinated siloxane initiator molecules; compatible with
               337 nm nitrogen laser (laser-NIMS) and Bi3+ ion beam (ion-NIMS) irradiation


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol introduces NIMS (Nanostructure-Initiator Mass Spectrometry), a matrix-free
surface-based mass spectrometry technique for spatially defined, high-sensitivity analysis
of biomolecules, metabolites, and drugs directly from cells, tissues, and biofluids.

The NIMS surface is a porous silicon chip (~10 nm pores, fabricated by anodic etching)
loaded with perfluorinated siloxane initiator molecules that are trapped inside the pores
in a clathrate-like ordered arrangement. Laser or ion irradiation rapidly vaporises the
initiator, triggering desorption and ionisation of analytes adsorbed on the surface --
without any conventional MALDI matrix. This eliminates matrix-related background, crystal
size limitations on spatial resolution, and the need for sample preparation steps.

Key advantages over existing techniques:
  - No matrix required (vs MALDI); no background below m/z 700
  - Lateral resolution ~150 nm with ion-NIMS Bi3+ beam (vs >10 um matrix crystal for MALDI)
  - Reduced molecular fragmentation vs secondary-ion mass spectrometry (SIMS)
  - Direct analysis of sub-microlitre blood and urine without extraction or derivatisation
  - Multiply charged protein ions analogous to ESI (laser-NIMS)
  - Compatible with laser (N2, 337 nm) and multiple ion sources (Au+, Ga+, Bi+, Bi3+)

The protocol is divided into six sections encoding five application demonstrations from
the paper:
  Section I   : NIMS surface fabrication (anodic etch + initiator loading)
  Section II  : Laser-NIMS -- peptide digest and small molecule detection (Fig 1d)
  Section III : Single cancer cell metabolite profiling (Fig 2)
  Section IV  : Peptide microarray characterisation by ion-NIMS Bi3+ (Fig 3)
  Section V   : Mouse embryo tissue imaging by laser-NIMS (Fig 4)
  Section VI  : Direct biofluid analysis -- blood and urine (Fig 5)


NIMS SURFACE ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------
Substrate  : P-type boron-doped silicon wafer, <100> orientation
             Resistivity: 0.01-0.02 Ohm.cm; thickness: 525 +/- 25 um; diameter: 100 mm
             Supplier: Silicon Valley Microelectronics
Pores      : ~10 nm diameter (confirmed by SEM, Fig 1c); fabricated by anodic electrochemical
             etching in 25% hydrofluoric acid in ethanol at 48 mA.cm^-2 for 30 min, 25 deg C
             CAUTION: HF is acutely hazardous; certified fume hood, PPE, and neutralising
             agent (calcium gluconate gel) required at all times.
Initiator  : Laser-NIMS: bis(tridecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrooctyl)tetramethyldisiloxane
             (undiluted, MW ~500 Da, perfluorinated siloxane)
             Ion-NIMS:   14-kDa poly(3,3,3-trifluoropropylmethylsiloxanes)
             Applied at 25 deg C for 30-60 min; excess removed with nitrogen jet.
             Ion-NIMS initiator baked overnight at 100 deg C; rinsed with tert-butyl methyl ether.
Clathrate  : Initiator molecules are highly ordered inside pores (reversible migration
             on slight heating/cooling; Supplementary Movie 1). Perfluorinated initiators
             preferred for laser-NIMS due to low analyte solubility in resulting clathrates
             and efficient trapping.

Alternative surfaces tested: native silicon (SiH), silanized, Au/Pd sputtered -- all
show NIMS activity. Alternative initiators tested: lauric acid, polysiloxanes, siloxanes,
silanes (MW 200-14000 Da; Supplementary Table 1).

---------------

  Acceleration voltage: 20 kV

  Extraction delay    : 100 ns

PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | NIMS Surface Fabrication -- Anodic Etching   [Section I, BB1]
  Reagents    : P-type <100> boron silicon wafer; 25% HF in ethanol
  Operation   : Anodic etch at 48 mA.cm^-2 for 30 min (1800 s), 25 deg C
  IR output   : etchedSurface1[0] = mix(siliconWafer1[0], etchSolution1[0]) @ 1800.0 SECOND
                heat(etchedSurface1[0]) @ 25.0 CELSIUS @ 1800.0 SECOND
  Result      : ~10 nm pore nanostructured silicon surface
  Notes       : Etching chamber constructed in-house; current from Bio-Rad PowerPack 1000.
                Detailed step-by-step at http://masspec.scripps.edu/Research/nims/create.php

Step 2 | Initiator Loading and N2 Jet Removal   [Section I, BB1]
  Reagents    : Perfluorinated siloxane initiator (undiluted); nitrogen gas
  Operation   : Apply initiator at 25 deg C for 45 min (2700 s); remove excess with N2 jet (30 s)
  IR output   : loadedSurface1[0] = mix(etchedSurface1[0], initiator1[0]) @ 2700.0 SECOND
                heat(loadedSurface1[0]) @ 25.0 CELSIUS @ 2700.0 SECOND
                nimsSurface1[0] = mix(loadedSurface1[0], nitrogenJet1[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
  Notes       : Initiator fills pores by capillary force; highly ordered arrangement confirmed
                by reversible migration (heating/cooling; Supplementary Movie 1).
                Both silanized and unsilanized surfaces are supported.

Step 3 | NIMS Surface QC   [Section I, BB1 -> BB2/BB3]
  Operation   : Test laser shot; detect initiator-derived background ions (nimsLaserDetection)
  IR output   : surfaceQC1[0] = detect(nimsLaserDetection, nimsSurface1[0]) @ 10.0 SECOND
  Gate        : surfaceQC > 0  -> BB2 (pass): dispose(nimsSurface1[0]); proceed to analyte sections
                surfaceQC <= 0 -> BB3 (fail): drain(nimsSurface1[0]); repeat surface preparation

Step 4 | BSA Tryptic Digest and Small Molecule Detection by Laser-NIMS   [Section II, BB4 -> BB5/BB6]
  Reagents    : BSA tryptic digest (500 amol); verapamil (700 ymol); 1:1 AcN/water + 0.1% TFA
  BSA prep    : Reduced with DTT; alkylated with iodoacetamide; digested with porcine trypsin (Promega)
  Drop volume : 0.5 uL per spot (contact spotting); solvent evaporated before laser irradiation
  Operations  : mix bsaDigest + solvent (10 s) -> heat 25 deg C (300 s) -> mix onto NIMS (5 s)
                -> detect nimsLaserDetection (30 s)
                -> dispense verapamil -> mix onto NIMS (5 s) -> heat 25 deg C (300 s)
  IR output   : bsaSpot1[0] = mix(bsaDigest1[0], bsaSolvent1[0]) @ 10.0 SECOND
                heat(bsaSpot1[0]) @ 25.0 CELSIUS @ 300.0 SECOND
                bsaSpot_nims1[0] = mix(bsaSpot1[0], nimsSurface2[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                peptideSignal1[0] = detect(nimsLaserDetection, bsaSpot_nims1[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
                verapamilSpot1[0] = mix(verapamilDrop1[0], nimsSurface2[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                heat(verapamilSpot1[0]) @ 25.0 CELSIUS @ 300.0 SECOND
  Results     : BSA: 55% sequence coverage at 500 amol (Fig 1d upper right)
                Verapamil: detected at 700 ymol (~420 molecules, Fig 1d lower left)
                beta-Lactoglobulin: multiply charged at 50 nmol (ESI-like, Fig 1d upper left)
                LysoPPC: 1-palmitoyllysophosphatidylcholine at 50 amol (Fig 1d lower right)
                Sensitivity 1000x higher than nanoESI and MALDI (Supplementary Figs 2, 3, 5)
  Gate        : peptideSignal > 0  -> BB5 (pass): dispose analyte spots
                peptideSignal <= 0 -> BB6 (fail): drain analyte spots

Step 5 | Single Cancer Cell Metabolite Profiling by Laser-NIMS   [Section III, BB7 -> BB8/BB9]
  Cell line   : MDA-MB-231 (highly metastatic breast cancer, ATCC HTB-26)
  Medium      : Leibovitz's L-15 + 2 mM L-glutamine + 10% calf bovine serum; 37 deg C
  Harvest     : trypsin-EDTA
  Deposition  : Micromanipulator (MINJ-PD; Tritech Research) precisely counts and deposits
                single cell onto NIMS chip; cell dried at 25 deg C (5 min) before MS
                NOTE: mix() in BioScript abstracts the micromanipulator deposition step
  Initiator   : bis(tridecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrooctyl)tetramethyldisiloxane;
                calibrated with Na+, K+, phosphocholine, 1-palmitoyllysophosphatidylcholine
  Operations  : dispense single cell -> mix onto NIMS (2 s) -> heat dry 25 deg C (300 s)
                -> detect nimsLaserDetection (30 s)
                -> dispense FDA/PI stain -> mix + stain (600 s)
                -> detect nimsFluorescenceDetection (30 s)
  Post-MS     : Fix: 2% paraformaldehyde. Permeabilise: cold methanol.
                Block: 20% human AB serum. Stain: anti-Pan cytokeratin (mouse monoclonal)
                + Alexa555 goat anti-mouse + DAPI nuclear counterstain.
  IR output   : cellOnSurface1[0] = mix(singleCell1[0], nimsSurface2[0]) @ 2.0 SECOND
                heat(cellOnSurface1[0]) @ 25.0 CELSIUS @ 300.0 SECOND
                cellSignal1[0] = detect(nimsLaserDetection, cellOnSurface1[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
                stainedCell1[0] = mix(cellOnSurface1[0], fdaPiStain1[0]) @ 600.0 SECOND
                fluoroSignal1[0] = detect(nimsFluorescenceDetection, stainedCell1[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
  Results     : Endogenous phospholipids detected from single cell (m/z 400-900);
                spectral complexity superior to hundreds of cells by nanoESI, MALDI, or
                ion-NIMS (Fig 2c). Cell location marked by green fluorescent box (Fig 2a).
  Gate        : cellSignal > 0  -> BB8 (pass): dispose stained cell + fluoro signal
                cellSignal <= 0 -> BB9 (fail): drain stained cell + fluoro signal

Step 6 | Peptide Microarray Characterisation by Ion-NIMS (Bi3+)   [Section IV, BB10 -> BB11/BB12]
  Peptides    : Bradykinin 2-9 (904 Da, red), Neurotensin (1673 Da, blue),
                Bradykinin 1-7 (757 Da, yellow); all at 1 fmol per spot
  Printing    : Robotic contact printing (custom arrayer, ArrayIt head); 1.4 nL drops;
                1:1 AcN/water + 0.1% TFA; array dried at 25 deg C (5 min) before analysis
                NOTE: mix() abstracts robotic contact printing of each peptide spot
  Operations  : dispense each peptide -> mix with solvent (5 s each)
                -> mix onto NIMS surface sequentially (5 s each)
                -> heat dry 25 deg C (300 s) -> detect nimsIonDetection (30 s)
  IR output   : bk27_spot1[0] = mix(bk27_solution1[0], arraySolvent1[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                bk17_spot1[0] = mix(bk17_solution1[0], arraySolvent1[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                nt_spot1[0]   = mix(nt_solution1[0],   arraySolvent1[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                peptideArray1[0] = mix(bk27_spot1[0], nimsSurface2[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                peptideArray2[0] = mix(bk17_spot1[0], peptideArray1[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                peptideArray3[0] = mix(nt_spot1[0],   peptideArray2[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                heat(peptideArray3[0]) @ 25.0 CELSIUS @ 300.0 SECOND
                arraySignal1[0] = detect(nimsIonDetection, peptideArray3[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
  Results     : 1 fmol sensitivity per peptide -- 1000x over TOF-SIMS (Ref 16, 21);
                doubles mass range vs cluster sources (Ref 15); 150 nm spatial resolution;
                BK peptides localise to centre of printed spot at higher concentration (Fig 3c);
                BK 1-7 on silicon control shows significant fragmentation vs NIMS (Fig 3d)
  Gate        : arraySignal > 0  -> BB11 (pass): dispose(peptideArray3[0])
                arraySignal <= 0 -> BB12 (fail): drain(peptideArray3[0])

Step 7 | Mouse Embryo Tissue Imaging by Laser-NIMS   [Section V, BB13 -> BB14/BB15]
  Sample      : C57Bl/6J mouse embryo, embryonic day 16.5
  Section prep: Frozen in OCT compound (VWR International) on dry ice; equilibrated to -20 deg C;
                cryosectioned at 12 um (Leica CM3050 S); thaw-mounted onto NIMS chip
                Parallel sections collected on Superfrost Plus slides (H&E histology)
  Initiator   : bis(heptadecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrodecyl)tetramethyldisiloxane
  Two-step imaging protocol:
    (i)  Laser etching: ~0.4 J.cm^-2 per pulse (337 nm) ablates 12 um tissue to expose
         underlying NIMS surface at the tissue/surface interface
         NOTE: heat() at 25 deg C abstracts this pulsed photothermal ablation step (2 min)
    (ii) NIMS imaging: ~0.01 J.cm^-2 per pulse (337 nm); 100-shot average per raster point;
         20 um step size; ~15 um laser beam diameter; nimsLaserDetection abstracts acquisition
  Operations  : dispense tissue section -> mix onto NIMS (30 s)
                -> heat etch 25 deg C (120 s) -> detect nimsLaserDetection (30 s)
  IR output   : mountedTissue1[0] = mix(tissueSection1[0], nimsSurface2[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
                heat(mountedTissue1[0]) @ 25.0 CELSIUS @ 120.0 SECOND
                tissueSignal1[0] = detect(nimsLaserDetection, mountedTissue1[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
  Results     : Glycerophosphatidylcholine (m/z 782) localised around developing vertebra (Fig 4b);
                300 um-wide false-colour image; red/yellow/cyan = high lipid signal;
                dark blue = developing vertebra. Phosphatidylethanolamine also detected
                (post-source decay). Scale bar 1 mm.
                NIMS complementary to MALDI for tissue: NIMS excels for metabolites/lipids;
                MALDI for peptides/small proteins (Ref 23).
  Animals     : C57Bl/6J housed in Scripps vivarium; IACUC-approved (LLNL guidelines)
                Embryos collected at E16.5; dams anaesthetised by isofluorane, sacrificed
                by cervical dislocation; embryos frozen on dry ice immediately.
  Gate        : tissueSignal > 0  -> BB14 (pass): dispose(mountedTissue1[0])
                tissueSignal <= 0 -> BB15 (fail): drain(mountedTissue1[0])

Step 8 | Direct Biofluid Analysis by Laser-NIMS   [Section VI, BB16 -> BB17/BB18]
  Samples     : 1 uL fresh human blood; 1 uL fresh human urine
                No sample preparation, extraction, or derivatisation required
                Spotted directly onto NIMS surface; held 30 s; excess blown off with N2 stream
  Initiators  : (a) bis(heptadecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrodecyl)tetramethyldisiloxane
                    (perfluorinated; lipid-selective; used for blood)
                (b) 1,3-bis(hydroxybutyl)tetramethyldisiloxane
                    (polar hydroxylated; small metabolite-selective; urine comparison)
                ~86% of ions from (b) are unique to the polar initiator surface, showing
                initiator selection targets metabolites by chemical functionality
  Blood protocol:
    1. Spot 1 uL fresh blood onto NIMS; wait 30 s; blow off excess with N2
    2. Laser-etch at ~0.4 J.cm^-2 to ablate cellular blood matrix (RBCs, proteins)
    3. NIMS analysis of exposed interface layer (dominant ions: phospholipids / PC lipids)
    NOTE: heat() at 25 deg C (60 s) abstracts the laser-etch ablation step
  Urine protocol:
    1. Spot 1 uL fresh urine onto NIMS; wait 30 s; blow off excess with N2
    2. NIMS analysis directly (no etch step needed for urine)
    3. Dominant ions: small metabolites <500 Da; also GM2 (m/z 1545.9) and GM1 (m/z 1561.9)
  Operations  : dispense blood -> mix onto NIMS (30 s) -> mix N2 blow-off (5 s)
                -> heat etch 25 deg C (60 s) -> mix NIMS interrogation (5 s)
                -> dispense urine -> mix onto NIMS (30 s) -> mix N2 blow-off (5 s)
                -> mix NIMS interrogation (5 s)
                -> detect nimsLaserDetection blood (30 s); detect nimsLaserDetection urine (30 s)
  IR output   : bloodOnSurface1[0]  = mix(bloodSample1[0],   nimsSurface2[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
                bloodExcess1[0]     = mix(bloodOnSurface1[0], nitrogenBlowOff1[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                heat(bloodExcess1[0]) @ 25.0 CELSIUS @ 60.0 SECOND
                bloodNIMS1[0]       = mix(bloodExcess1[0],    nimsSurface2[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                urineOnSurface1[0]  = mix(urineSample1[0],    nimsSurface2[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
                urineExcess1[0]     = mix(urineOnSurface1[0], nitrogenBlowOff21[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                urineNIMS1[0]       = mix(urineExcess1[0],    nimsSurface2[0]) @ 5.0 SECOND
                biofluidSignal1[0]  = detect(nimsLaserDetection, bloodNIMS1[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
                urineSignal1[0]     = detect(nimsLaserDetection, urineNIMS1[0]) @ 30.0 SECOND
  Results     : Complex spectra without background ions (Fig 5); linear codeine response
                up to 15 ng.mL^-1 vs 75-250 ng.mL^-1 for conventional approaches (Ref 28).
                Data processing: ASCII export -> SpecAlign (baseline correction, TIC
                normalisation, spectrum realignment). Statistical analysis: SigmaPlot 10.
  Gate        : biofluidSignal > 0  -> BB17 (pass): dispose blood, urine, urine signal droplets
                biofluidSignal <= 0 -> BB18 (fail): drain  blood, urine, urine signal droplets

Terminal      : BB19 (bsbbif_19_j): dispose(biofluidSignal1[0]); NOP

ASCII constraint:
  ANTLR4 FileStream defaults to ascii encoding; initial file contained non-ASCII characters
  (degree symbol, micro sign, middle dot, superscripts, Ohm, +/-, Nordstrom o-umlaut).
  All replaced with ASCII equivalents: deg, u, ., ^-2, Ohm, +/-, Nordstrom.
  Fix applied with sed; verified by grep -P '[^\x00-\x7F]' (zero matches after fix).

mix() abstraction note:
  mix() is used throughout to abstract non-DMF physical steps:
    - Anodic etching of silicon wafer (S1)
    - Initiator capillary loading (S2)
    - Single cell deposition by micromanipulator MINJ-PD (S5)
    - Tissue section thaw-mount onto NIMS chip (S7)
    - Robotic contact array printing (S6, ArrayIt head, 1.4 nL drops)
    - Blood/urine direct spotting and N2 blow-off (S8)
  Each such step is annotated with a comment in the .bs source file.

nimsSurface SSA renaming:
  nimsSurface appears as nimsSurface1[0] in BB1/BB2/BB3 (initial dispense after fabrication)
  and as nimsSurface2[0] in all subsequent sections (BB4 onward). The compiler correctly
  applies SSA (Static Single Assignment) renaming to the second dispense of the same
  manifest, ensuring distinct variable identifiers for each use.
