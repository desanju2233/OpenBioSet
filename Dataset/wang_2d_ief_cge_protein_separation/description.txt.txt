PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : Two-Dimensional Protein Separation with Advanced Sample and Buffer Isolation Using Microfluidic Valves
Source Link  : https://pubs.acs.org/ancham/article-pdf/76/15/4426/37843760/ac0497499.pdf
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1021/ac0497499
Journal      : Analytical Chemistry, 2004, Vol. 76, No. 15, pp. 4426-4431
Platform     : Continuous-flow PDMS microfluidic chip with pneumatic monolith valves (pressure-driven + electrokinetic)


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a chip-based two-dimensional (2D) protein separation system that couples
isoelectric focusing (IEF) with capillary gel electrophoresis (CGE) or capillary zone
electrophoresis (CZE) using active pneumatic microvalve control in a double-layer PDMS device.
The key innovation is the use of monolith valves to physically isolate incompatible separation
buffers (ampholyte for IEF vs. high ionic-strength gel/CE buffer), preventing peak interdiffusion
and band dispersion during the coupling step between the two separation dimensions.

The protocol is divided into four functional phases:
  1. Device surface passivation (polyacrylamide + methylcellulose coating to suppress EOF and
     protein adsorption)
  2. Sequential buffer loading: CGE gel buffer into the 2nd-dimension channel (right valves closed),
     then IEF ampholyte-sample mixture into the 1st-dimension channel (left valves closed)
  3. IEF focusing in a 10 mm PDMS channel at 500 V/cm; protein peaks mobilised by EOF into
     the 3 mm peak transfer region; target peaks isolated by closing both valve sets at 80 psi
  4. CGE/CZE 2nd-dimension separation in a 25 mm channel at 250 V/cm; sample stacking at the
     ampholyte/gel buffer boundary narrows the launching band and ensures independence of the
     two separation dimensions

The full 2D separation is completed within 10 min on-chip. A total of four fluorescently labelled
proteins are resolved: GFP (pI 5.6, 26 kDa), ovalbumin (pI 5.1, 45 kDa), low-density lipoprotein
protein (pI 5.11, 179 kDa), and trypsin inhibitor (pI 4.6, 20 kDa).

This BioScript file encodes the IEF-CGE arm as the primary representative experiment (Figures 3
and 4 in the paper). The IEF-CZE arm is structurally identical; a separate .bs file should encode
it with CZE_Buffer (0.375 M Tris-Cl, pH 8.6, 0.1% methylcellulose) substituted for CGE_GelBuffer.


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Substrate     : Poly(dimethylsiloxane) (PDMS) on flat glass
Fabrication   : Three-process PDMS multilayer bonding:
                (1) Master fabrication by photolithography (AZ-4620 positive + SU-8 50 negative
                    photoresist; Clariant AZ-4620, MicroChem SU-8 50)
                (2) PDMS shaping by spin-coating and casting
                (3) Oxygen plasma bonding (Harrick Scientific, Ossining, NY)

Separation channel mold : 150 um wide, 12 um high; heated to 150 C for 30 min (round-shaped)
Valve control channel   : 300 um wide, 50 um high (square cross section)
Silane treatment        : 30 min hexamethyldisilazane (Sigma-Aldrich) vapor before PDMS casting
PDMS mixing ratio       : 10:1 (Sylgard 184, Dow Corning); degassed at ~5 psi vacuum
Separation layer        : 70 um thick (spin speed 2000 rpm)
Control layer           : ~5 mm thick PDMS
Curing                  : 65 C, at least 2 h; oxygen plasma treatment before bonding
Bonding sequence        : Control layer (5 mm) bonded to separation layer (70 um), then onto
                          PDMS-on-glass substrate
Bonding strength        : At least 2 h rest after alignment-bonding; >50 psi without leakage
Control channel access  : 1/16-in. o.d. metal syringe needle (Hamilton, Reno, NV) for pressure
                          line connection via 1/16-in. Teflon tubing

Channel dimensions:
  IEF channel length      : 10 mm
  CE channel length       : 25 mm
  Peak transfer region    : 3 mm
  Dead volume at junction : 0.1 nL (smaller than commercial microvalves, 10-100 nL)

Valve specification:
  Type                     : Pneumatic monolith valves (Unger et al., Science 2000, 288, 113-116)
  Sets                     : 2 (left: valves 1,2; right: valves 3,4)
  Material                 : Sylgard 184 PDMS (tensile strength 7.1 MPa, elongation 140%)
  Closure pressure         : 80 psi
  Isolation resistance     : 699 MOhm at 30 psi; >1000 MOhm at 100 psi
  No cross-contamination   : Verified over 30 min post-isolation

Valve operation sequence (Figure 1):
  (b) Right valves (3,4) closed -> CGE gel buffer loaded into CE channel (20-60 psi)
  (c) Left valves (1,2) closed, right valves (3,4) opened -> IEF sample mixture loaded
  (d) Both valve sets closed -> target protein peaks isolated in peak transfer region
  (e) Left valves (1,2) opened -> proteins sent into 2nd-dimension CE channel

Note: Control channels must be pre-filled with water before use to prevent air bubbles
during high-pressure valve operation (PDMS is gas-permeable).


SURFACE TREATMENT
-----------------
Static coating  : 3-(trimethoxysilyl)propyl methacrylate (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO)
                  as adhesion promoter; followed by acrylamide + TEMED + APS solution
                  introduced into channel; polymerised under N2 purge in vacuum chamber
                  (homemade, N2 purging valve) for 30 min at 25 C to eliminate O2 quenching
Dynamic coating : 0.5% methylcellulose solution as pretreatment; further minimises
                  non-specific adsorption
Outcome         : Protein adsorption reduced to undetectable level by fluorescence microscopy
                  for duration of a single 2D separation experiment
Same silanol (-Si-OH) group in PDMS and glass allows identical acrylamide coating protocol.


MATERIALS AND REAGENTS
-----------------------

IEF sample solution:
  Ampholyte      : 3-10 carrier ampholyte (Beckman Coulter, Fullerton, CA)
  Glycerol       : 10% (BioRad, Hercules, CA)
  Methylcellulose: 1% (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO); same concentration in all solutions
                   (anolyte, catholyte, gel buffer) for better mixing in peak transfer region
  Protein samples: Fluorescein- or Alexa Fluor 488-labeled (attached to lysine residues;
                   proteins may acquire multiple dye molecules and exhibit multiple pI values)

Protein samples (100 uM each in IEF-CGE experiment, Figure 4):
  GFP                  : pI 5.6,  MW 26 kDa
  Ovalbumin            : pI 5.1,  MW 45 kDa
  Low-density lipoprotein protein : pI 5.11, MW 179 kDa
  Trypsin inhibitor    : pI 4.6,  MW 20 kDa

IEF linearity markers (separate experiment):
  Source         : Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO
  pI values      : 3.0, 4.5, 5.1, 6.2, 7.2, 8.1, 9.0, 10.3
  Concentration  : 10 ug/mL each
  Detection      : ex 330-340 nm, em 415-500 nm (fluorescence microscopy)

Buffers:
  Anolyte        : 0.1 M acetic acid, pH 2.5 (+ 1% methylcellulose)
  Catholyte      : 0.5% ammonium hydroxide, pH 10.5 (+ 1% methylcellulose)
  CGE gel buffer : SDS 14-200 gel buffer (Beckman Coulter); 3.0% Tris-hydroxymethylaminomethane,
                   2.0% ethylene glycol; 90% SDS 14-200 + 1% methylcellulose in experiment
  CZE buffer     : 0.375 M Tris-Cl, pH 8.6, 0.1% methylcellulose (IEF-CZE arm only)

Detection system:
  Microscope     : Inverted epi-fluorescence (Olympus, Melville, NY), 100 W mercury lamp
  Filter set     : Standard FITC
  Camera         : Thermoelectrically cooled CCD (Cooke Co., Auburn Hills, MI)
  Acquisition    : IPLab 3.6 (Scanalytics, Fairfax, VA); 1 frame/s


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Surface Passivation - Static Polyacrylamide Coating
  Reagents    : AcrylamideCoating (acrylamide + TEMED + APS + 3-(trimethoxysilyl)propyl
                methacrylate adhesion promoter), GlycerolMethylcellulose (0.5% methylcellulose)
  Operation   : mix; then heat at 25 C for 30 min under N2 purge (5 psi vacuum)
  Duration    : 30 min (1800 s)
  Purpose     : Grow polyacrylamide polymer on PDMS surface to prevent protein adsorption
                and decrease uneven EOF. Follow with dynamic 0.5% methylcellulose coating.
  Note        : One-time chip preparation step before each experiment run.

Step 2 | CGE Gel Buffer Loading into Second-Dimension Channel
  Reagents    : CGE_GelBuffer (SDS 14-200 + 1% methylcellulose)
  Operation   : Pressure-driven injection (abstracted as mix in BioScript)
  Valve state : Right valves (3,4) closed at 80 psi to isolate IEF channel
  Pressure    : 20-30 psi (up to 60 psi applicable without breaking valve seal)
  Purpose     : Fill 25 mm CE channel with gel buffer without contaminating the
                ampholyte-sensitive IEF channel.

Step 3 | IEF Sample Preparation
  Reagents    : Ampholyte_3_10, GlycerolMethylcellulose, GFP_Sample, OvalSample,
                LDLPSample, TrypsinInhibitorSample
  Operation   : Sequential mix steps; ampholyte + methylcellulose/glycerol carrier
                prepared first; all four labeled protein samples then combined and
                merged into the carrier
  Purpose     : Prepare 3-10 ampholyte mixture with 10% glycerol, 1% methylcellulose,
                and all four fluorescently labeled protein analytes.

Step 4 | IEF Sample Loading and Reservoir Buffer Replacement
  Reagents    : AnolyteSolution (0.1 M acetic acid, pH 2.5),
                CatholyteSolution (0.5% ammonium hydroxide, pH 10.5)
  Operation   : Pressure-driven injection (abstracted as mix in BioScript)
  Valve state : Left valves (1,2) closed to isolate CGE gel; right valves (3,4) opened
  Purpose     : Load ampholyte-sample mixture into 10 mm IEF channel; replace reservoirs
                with anolyte (anode end) and catholyte (cathode end).

Step 5 | IEF Focusing (1st Dimension)
  Operation   : Electric field application (abstracted as heat in BioScript)
  Field       : 500 V/cm across 10 mm IEF channel (500 V total)
  Duration    : 130 s
  Temperature : 37 C (abstraction for field-driven step)
  Outcome     : pH 3-10 gradient established; proteins focus to stationary pI bands.
                EOF mobilises focused peaks slowly from anode to cathode.
                Steady state confirmed by monitoring current through channel.
  Speed       : Equilibration <30 s; 2 orders of magnitude faster than CIEF in 25-50 cm
                capillaries (~30 min). Comparable to short fused-silica CIEF (280-360 s,
                Wu et al., Anal. Sci. 2001) but with better S/N and separation properties.
  EOF control : Critical - if too high, peaks are flushed out within minutes.
                Controlled by methylcellulose concentration and polyacrylamide coating.

Step 6 | IEF Peak Detection (QC Gate 1)
  Operation   : iefFocusingDetection module; fluorescence microscopy
  Duration    : 30 s detection window
  Outcome     : Verify focused protein peaks have entered the 3 mm peak transfer region
  Branch      : Pass (iefSignal > 0)  -> dispose(iefChannel); proceed to peak isolation
                Fail (iefSignal <= 0) -> drain(iefChannel); experiment terminated

Step 7 | Peak Isolation by Valve Closure
  Operation   : Pneumatic valve actuation (abstracted as dispense re-entry in BioScript;
                no native valve primitive in lilott8/BioScript)
  Valve state : Both valve sets (1,2 and 3,4) closed at 80 psi
  Dead volume : 0.1 nL (peak transfer region between the two valve sets)
  Isolation   : >1000 MOhm electrical resistance; no protein intercontamination after 30 min
  Purpose     : Physically trap target protein peaks in the 3 mm transfer region, isolated
                from both the IEF channel and the CGE channel. Symmetric channel layout
                prevents valve actuation from mobilising trapped proteins.

Step 8 | CGE Buffer Equilibration
  Reagents    : CGE_GelBuffer
  Operation   : Diffusion equilibration (abstracted as mix in BioScript)
  Valve state : Left valves (1,2) opened
  Duration    : 30 s
  Purpose     : Allow buffer ions to diffuse into peak transfer region to prevent abrupt
                conductivity change at the buffer boundary before CE field is applied.
                Protein diffusion is negligible (large MW, slow diffusion constant relative
                to buffer ions). Small-MW ampholyte co-isolated with proteins is eluted
                rapidly by electrophoresis (lower MW than protein analytes).

Step 9 | CGE Separation (2nd Dimension)
  Operation   : Electric field application (abstracted as heat in BioScript)
  Field       : 250 V/cm across 25 mm CE channel (625 V total)
  Duration    : ~6 min (360 s)
  Temperature : 37 C (abstraction for field-driven step)
  Separation  : Charge-to-mass ratio (non-denaturing CGE, no SDS)
  Stacking    : Proteins enter high-conductivity gel buffer from low-conductivity ampholyte;
                sudden velocity decrease at boundary narrows launching band (stacking effect).
                Highly viscous gel also contributes sieving to stacking.
                Stacking ensures 1st and 2nd dimension separations are fully independent.
  Mobilities  : Ovalbumin (45 kDa): -1.3e-4 cm2/V.s
                GFP (26 kDa): -1.0e-4 cm2/V.s
                Ovalbumin elutes before GFP (higher charge-to-mass ratio, faster mobility)
  Total time  : 10 min for complete 2D separation

Step 10 | CGE Fluorescence Readout (QC Gate 2)
  Operation   : cgeDetection module; fluorescence intensity at end of 25 mm CGE channel
  Duration    : 30 s detection window
  Readout     : Electropherograms (fluorescence intensity vs. time); FITC filter, CCD camera
  Branch      : Pass (cgeSignal > 0)  -> dispose(equilibrated); proceed to cleanup
                Fail (cgeSignal <= 0) -> drain(equilibrated); experiment terminated


BIOSCRIPT COMPILER NOTES
-------------------------
BioScript file : wang_2d_ief_cge_protein_separation.bs
Compiler       : lilott8/BioScript (ANTLR4-based)
IR output      : 7 basic blocks (BB1-BB7), 2 if/else gates, 8 CFG edges
DAG            : wang_2d_ief_cge_protein_separation_main_dag.dot

Duration units : All durations converted to seconds (s) or minutes (m) for compiler
                 compatibility (BioScript supports s and m only; h token not supported)
                 30 min coating    -> 30m  (IR: 1800.0 SECOND)
                 130 s IEF         -> 130s (IR: 130.0 SECOND)
                 6 min CGE         -> 6m   (IR: 360.0 SECOND)

Modules        : iefFocusingDetection, cgeDetection

Platform note  : This is a continuous-flow pressure-driven + electrokinetic chip, not a
                 DMF/EWOD device. BioScript primitives are used as abstractions:
                   mix    -> pressure-driven injection and buffer loading steps
                   heat   -> electric field application (IEF focusing, CGE/CE separation)
                   detect -> fluorescence microscopy readout (CCD camera, IPLab 3.6)
                 No native electrokinetic or valve primitive exists in lilott8/BioScript.
                 Valve state changes are modelled as mix steps transitioning between
                 separation phases. Peak isolation (Step 7) uses a fresh dispense to
                 re-introduce the isolated fluid as a new BioScript variable.

Known compiler behaviours observed in IR:
  - drain lowers to dispose() in IR output (known compiler bug; .bs source authoritative)
  - True-branch blocks (bsbbif_2_t, bsbbif_5_t) emit no unconditional jump in IR text
    (known limitation); DOT edges resolve correctly

IEF-CZE arm note : The IEF-CZE arm (paper Section "IEF Coupled with Second-Dimension
                   Capillary Zone Electrophoresis") is structurally identical to this file.
                   A separate .bs file should encode it with CZE_Buffer
                   (0.375 M Tris-Cl, pH 8.6, 0.1% methylcellulose) substituted for
                   CGE_GelBuffer at Steps S2 and S8. All other steps, durations, and
                   CFG structure remain unchanged.
