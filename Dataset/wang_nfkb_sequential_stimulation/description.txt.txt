PROTOCOL DESCRIPTION
====================

Title        : NF-kappaB Memory Coordinates Transcriptional Responses to Dynamic Inflammatory Stimuli
Source Link  : https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/pdfExtended/S2211-1247(22)00968-8
Paper DOI    : https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2022.111159
Journal      : Cell Reports, 2022, Vol. 40, Article 111159
Platform     : Automated pressure-driven microfluidic live-cell imaging (channel-based PDMS/glass device,
               not DMF); 64-chamber device with 14 stimulus inputs; MATLAB-driven pneumatic valve control


OVERVIEW
--------
This protocol describes a high-throughput microfluidic live-cell imaging approach to systematically
profile single-cell NF-kappaB responses to 24 non-repeating sequential combinations of four
inflammatory ligands (TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, LPS, PAM2CSK4) at three dose levels (high, mid, low)
in RelA^-/- NIH/3T3 mouse embryonic fibroblasts expressing a RelA-DsRed fusion protein. The core
finding is that NF-kappaB dynamics encode short-term memory of prior stimulus history through
ligand-dependent engagement of distinct negative feedback modules.

The protocol has four functional phases:
  1. Microfluidic device preparation, cell loading, and equilibration
  2. Sequential four-ligand stimulation (S1 -> S2 -> S3 -> S4, 2 h per interval) with continuous
     live-cell time-lapse imaging and nuclear/cytoplasmic RelA-DsRed quantification
  3. Information-theoretic analysis of NF-kappaB dynamics using channel capacity (CC) to quantify
     how accurately subsequent responses encode prior stimulus history
  4. Mechanistic dissection via IKK inhibition (PS1145), mathematical ODE modelling, and bulk
     RNA sequencing to identify ligand-dependent negative feedback modules

The complete 8 h imaging experiment profiles >10,000 single cells across 72 conditions
(24 sequences x 3 dose levels) in 2 independent biological replicates.


DEVICE ARCHITECTURE
-------------------
Type      : Pressure-driven automated microfluidic cell culture device (channel-based; not DMF).
            No electrodes, no EWOD actuation. Reagent switching achieved by pneumatic valves.
Material  : PDMS (Momentive RTV-615, 10:1 monomer:catalyst) bonded to glass slide by oxygen plasma.
Layers    : Control layer: 66 g PDMS poured on silicon wafer master, cured 80 C overnight, ~2 cm thick.
            Flow layer: 15 g PDMS spun at 2200 rpm on silicon wafer master (50 um thick), cured 80 C 1 h.
            Both layers plasma-treated, aligned under custom stereomicroscope, baked 80 C overnight.
Chambers  : 64 independently controlled cell culture chambers, each 3.5 x 0.8 x 0.035 mm; >500 cells/chamber.
Inputs    : 14 unique stimulus inputs.
Coating   : 0.25 mg/mL human plasma fibronectin (Millipore FC010) in sterile PBS pH 7.4; overnight RT.
Control   : Pneumatic solenoid valves; electronic controller boxes; MATLAB scripts + custom GUI.
            Input pressure: 4 psi; reagent delivery via PEEK tubing (VICI, TPK.505).
Microscope: Nikon Ti2 enclosed in temperature-controlled incubator (Life Imaging Services); 37 C,
            100% humidity, 5% CO2 throughout all imaging steps.
Camera    : Hamamatsu ORCA-Flash4.0 V2 sCMOS; 20X magnification; images every 6 min.
Channels  : RelA-DsRed (555 nm excitation, 0.5-1 s exposure; NF-kappaB nuclear translocation)
            H2B-GFP (485 nm, 50-100 ms; nuclear segmentation and cell tracking)
            JNK-KTR-mCerulean3 (440 nm, 100 ms; JNK kinase activity)
Design ref: Kellogg et al. 2014, Nat. Protoc. 9, 1713-1726; Son et al. 2021, Sci. Signal. 14, eaaz4382


CELL LINE
---------
RelA^-/- NIH/3T3 immortalized mouse embryonic fibroblasts (3T3s) stably expressing:
  - RelA-DsRed fusion protein (NF-kappaB nuclear translocation reporter)
  - JNK-KTR-mCerulean3 (JNK kinase activity reporter)
  - H2B-GFP (nuclear marker for segmentation and tracking)
Source    : Markus Covert laboratory (Stanford University)
Culture   : DMEM High Glucose (Gibco) + 10% FBS (Omega Scientific) + 1% GlutaMAX (Gibco)
            + 100 U/mL penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco); 37 C, 5% CO2.
Imaging   : FluoroBrite DMEM (Gibco, A1896701) + same supplements; reduces autofluorescence.
Passage   : No more than 15 passages; passed before reaching 100% confluency.
MyD88 KO  : CRISPR-Cas9 knockout line generated using pX330 backbone (Addgene #42230) and
            guide RNA 5'-TCGCGCTTAACGTGGGAGTG-3'; confirmed by Sanger sequencing and western blot.
            Used to confirm MyD88-dependence of LPS, IL-1beta, and PAM signalling (Supp. Fig. 5).


LIGANDS AND DOSE LEVELS
------------------------
Four inflammatory ligands covering two receptor pathway groups:

  TNFR pathway (MyD88-independent):
    TNF-alpha : Recombinant Mouse TNF-alpha (R&D Systems, aa 80-235)
                Signals via TNFR -> TRADD/TRAF2 -> IKK -> NF-kappaB
                High: 90 ng/mL (>95% activated); Mid: 30 ng/mL (85-95%); Low: 3 ng/mL (50-70%)
                NOTE: maximal TNF-alpha activation ~90%; high dose activates slightly fewer cells than
                other ligands at high dose.

  MyD88-dependent pathway (shared IRAK1/4 -> IKK -> NF-kappaB):
    IL-1beta  : Recombinant Mouse IL-1 beta/IL-1F2 (R&D Systems, 401ML010CF); receptor IL-1R
                High: 3 ng/mL; Mid: 0.2 ng/mL; Low: 0.05 ng/mL
    LPS       : Ultrapure LPS E. coli 0111:B4 (InvivoGen, tlrl-3pelps); receptor TLR4
                High: 400 ng/mL; Mid: 100 ng/mL; Low: 12.5 ng/mL
                NOTE: LPS produces characteristically delayed NF-kappaB activation kinetics
                (linked to ligand-specific IKK cycling rates); TRIF/TRAM-independent in 3T3s.
    PAM       : PAM2CSK4 synthetic diacylated lipopeptide (InvivoGen, tlrl-pm2s-1); receptor TLR2/6
                High: 1 ng/mL; Mid: 0.2 ng/mL; Low: 0.01 ng/mL

All ligands diluted from -80 C stocks immediately before each stimulus, stored on ice during
the experiment. Delivered at 4 psi via PEEK tubing at each 2 h interval switch.


PROTOCOL STEPS
--------------

Step 1 | Device Fabrication and Surface Coating
  Reagents  : PDMS (RTV-615, 10:1), PBS (Gibco, pH 7.4), fibronectin 0.25 mg/mL
  Operation : Plasma-bond PDMS device to glass. Fill chambers with fibronectin/PBS solution.
  Duration  : Overnight at room temperature (fibronectin coating); flush with complete media before loading.
  Purpose   : Fibronectin coating promotes cell adhesion to PDMS chamber floor.

Step 2 | Cell Loading and Equilibration
  Cell line  : RelA^-/- NIH/3T3 (RelA-DsRed / JNK-KTR / H2B-GFP)
  Preparation: Harvest with trypsin; wash with complete medium; resuspend at 5e6 cells/mL
               in FluoroBrite DMEM + supplements.
  Loading    : Load at ~50% confluency to optimize single-cell tracking efficiency.
  Equilibration: 5 h (300 min) at 37 C, 5% CO2, 100% humidity before stimulus and imaging begin.
  Purpose    : Allow cells to adhere, spread, and return to baseline signalling state.

Step 3 | S1 Stimulus - Naive NF-kappaB Response
  Ligand (canonical mid-dose arm): IL-1beta 0.2 ng/mL
  Duration   : 120 min (2 h interval)
  Delivery   : MATLAB valve script switches input from media to ligand at t=0 min; 4 psi, PEEK tubing.
  Imaging    : Time-lapse epifluorescence, 555 nm excitation, 0.5-1 s exposure, every 6 min.
               For time-resolved LPS-to-IL-1beta switching experiments: imaging every 3 min.
  Readout    : Nuclear/cytoplasmic RelA-DsRed ratio quantified by custom MATLAB image analysis.
               H2B-GFP used for nuclear segmentation; single-cell trajectories tracked over full
               experiment. Traces smoothed with lowess (span 3) to identify local maxima.
               Multiple local maxima allowed (min 5-frame = 30 min spacing between peaks).
               Cutoff: 95th percentile of maxima from unstimulated cells; sub-threshold values set to 0.
  Information: Nuclear NF-kappaB at 6 timepoints (20, 30, 40, 50, 70, 90 min post-stimulus)
               used as 6-dimensional input for channel capacity (CC) calculation (Fig. 3A).
  Result     : S1 response is naive baseline; used to normalize all S2-S4 responses for that ligand.

Step 4 | S1 QC Gate
  Condition  : s1Signal > 0 -> pass (valid naive response); proceed to S2.
               s1Signal <= 0 -> fail (no response: dead, unhealthy, or incorrectly loaded cell);
               dispose ligand variable; chamber excluded from downstream analysis.

Step 5 | S2 Stimulus - First History-Conditioned Response
  Ligand (canonical mid-dose arm): LPS 100 ng/mL
  Duration   : 120 min; valve switches from IL-1beta to LPS at t=120 min.
  Key finding: Prior IL-1beta (S1, MyD88-dependent) induces IRAK1 auto-inhibitory phosphorylation,
               attenuating the subsequent LPS response in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 4B/E).
               Asymmetry: prior LPS inhibits subsequent IL-1beta/PAM more strongly than vice versa
               at mid and low doses (Fig. 4E, red vs blue/green). Prior TNF-alpha (TNFR-dependent)
               only weakly attenuates MyD88-dependent signalling (Fig. 4D, blue).
  CC result  : Channel capacity increases from S1 to S2 despite response attenuation, indicating
               NF-kappaB dynamics encode prior history information (Fig. 3B).

Step 6 | S3 Stimulus - Two-Ligand History Response
  Ligand (canonical mid-dose arm): PAM 0.2 ng/mL
  Duration   : 120 min; valve switches to PAM at t=240 min.
  Key finding: Cumulative MyD88-dependent history (IL-1beta + LPS, S1+S2) induces downstream
               NF-kappaB-dependent negative feedback proteins (A20/Tnfaip3, IkappaBepsilon/Nfkbie,
               Nfkbia). Channel capacity remains elevated at S3 vs S1 (Fig. 3B/C).

Step 7 | S4 Stimulus - Three-Ligand History Response
  Ligand (canonical mid-dose arm): TNF-alpha 30 ng/mL
  Duration   : 120 min; valve switches to TNF-alpha at t=360 min.
  Key finding: TNF-alpha signals via TNFR (distinct receptor-associated intermediaries from MyD88);
               converges on IKK downstream of the accumulated IRAK1 auto-inhibition and
               NF-kappaB-dependent feedback induced at S1-S3. TNF-alpha response is only weakly
               attenuated regardless of prior MyD88-dependent history (Figs. 2B, 4B/D).

Step 8 | Cell Retrieval for Transcriptomics (S4 pass path only)
  Timing     : End of 8 h imaging window (t=480 min)
  Method     : TrypLE Express (Gibco); 1 min; detaches cells from fibronectin-coated surface.
               PBS wash; cells accumulate at outlet channel; removed in 2 uL droplet by pipette.
               Deposited in 10 uL ice-cold lysis buffer (0.1% Triton-X100 + RNase inhibitor, Takara).
               Stored at -80 C until processing. ~1500 cells per replicate per condition.
  Library prep: SMART-Seq2 pipeline. OligodT30VN annealing at 72 C. Reverse transcription
               (SuperScript III, Invitrogen) + TSO oligo (AAGCAGTGGTATCAACGCAGAGTGAAT[rG][rG][rG])
               at 42 C for 90 min + 10 cycles 50/42 C. 6-cycle KAPA HiFi preamplification.
               Ampure XP bead purification (1:1). Nextera XT library prep (Illumina, FC-131-1024).
  Sequencing : Illumina HiSEQ4000; single-end 50 bp; University of Chicago Genomics Facility.
  Analysis   : STAR alignment to GRCm38. featureCounts transcript quantification.
               edgeR + limma (R) differential expression. BH FDR < 0.01, log2FC > 1.
               3 biological replicates. Data deposited: GEO GSE193053.
  Conditions : LPS 100 ng/mL vs PAM 0.2 ng/mL vs IL-1beta 0.2 ng/mL vs untreated; 2 h stimulus.
  qPCR       : CellDirect One-Step RT-qPCR. Custom primers for Tnfaip3. IDT PrimeTime probes
               for Csf2, Csf3, Cxcl2, Cxcl3, Il23a, Gapdh. TaqMan probes for Nfkbia, Nfkbie.
               Ct normalized to Gapdh (deltaCt); fold change over unstimulated (deltadeltaCt).
               BH-adjusted two-tailed t-test. Data shown as mean +/- SEM, n=3 replicates.

Step 9 | IKK Inhibition Sub-Protocol (Fig. 5A, Supp. Fig. 6C/D)
  Purpose    : Determine whether NF-kappaB-mediated gene expression downstream of IKK is required
               for LPS-specific asymmetric attenuation of subsequent MyD88-dependent signalling.
  Reagent    : PS1145 dihydrochloride (Tocris, 4569); reversible IKK-beta inhibitor; 40 uM.
  Sub-steps  :
    9a. Load fresh RelA-DsRed 3T3 cells; equilibrate 5 h at 37 C, 5% CO2 (as Step 2).
    9b. Pre-treat with PS1145 40 uM in complete media for 90 min at 37 C.
    9c. Stimulate with LPS (25-400 ng/mL) + PS1145 40 uM for 240 min at 37 C.
        (NF-kappaB translocation is significantly reduced during LPS due to IKK-beta inhibition;
        Supp. Fig. 6D confirms reduced activation vs untreated at all LPS doses tested.)
    9d. Wash out PS1145: 2 x 15 min washes with complete DMEM at 37 C.
    9e. Restimulate with IL-1beta 3 ng/mL (high dose) for 120 min at 37 C.
        Detect: nfkbTranslocationDetection; 90 s readout.
  Result     : PS1145-treated cells show significantly stronger NF-kappaB response to subsequent
               IL-1beta vs untreated cells (***p < 1e-4, Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon rank-sum;
               >120 cells per condition; 2 biological replicates; Fig. 5A).
  Conclusion : NF-kappaB nuclear translocation and NF-kappaB-mediated gene expression downstream
               of IKK are required for LPS-specific asymmetric attenuation, confirming that
               transcriptional induction of downstream negative feedback drives the asymmetry.


MATHEMATICAL MODEL
------------------
Type       : ODE network model; 14 differential equations; 20 parameters.
Base model : Krishna et al. 2006, PNAS 103:10840 (minimal 3-equation NF-kappaB oscillator).
Expansion  : Added receptors TNFR, IL-1R, TLR4; IRAK1/4 auto-inhibitory phosphorylation
             (Equations 6-9; nonlinear inactivation proportional to active IRAK^2);
             downstream NF-kappaB-dependent inhibitor (Equations 4-5; targets IKK activation;
             Hill coefficient 3 for cooperativity); IKK cycling (Equations 10-11).
LPS vs IL-1beta feedback: LPS-induced downstream inhibitor C parameter set 4x lower threshold
             than IL-1beta or TNF-alpha, modelling stronger transcriptional negative feedback.
Implementation: Python; scipy.integrate.odeint; Bokeh visualization.
Key result : Two-motif model (IRAK1 auto-inhibition + differential downstream feedback) is
             sufficient to reproduce all observed ligand- and dose-specific attenuation patterns
             without additional components (Fig. 5C-E, Supp. Fig. 7A).
Code       : https://github.com/tay-lab/Sequential_NF-kB_stim
Zenodo     : https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6626195


KEY FINDINGS
------------
1. Sequential inflammatory stimuli broadly attenuate subsequent NF-kappaB responses (Fig. 2).
   LPS and PAM produce no response after first stimulus; TNF-alpha and IL-1beta show weak
   responses at S2-S4 depending on dose.

2. Variable attenuation encodes prior stimulus history. Channel capacity (CC) increases from
   S1 to S2 despite attenuation, and remains elevated at S3-S4 (Fig. 3B). NF-kappaB dynamics
   (multi-timepoint) carry substantially more information than peak amplitude alone (Fig. 3B/D).

3. TNF-alpha (TNFR) and MyD88-dependent ligands (IL-1beta, LPS, PAM) form two distinct
   history-encoding groups. Prior TNF-alpha weakly attenuates MyD88-dependent signalling
   and vice versa; prior MyD88-dependent signalling strongly attenuates subsequent
   MyD88-dependent signalling in a dose-dependent and asymmetric manner (Fig. 4D/E).

4. Asymmetry mechanism: at high dose, IRAK1 auto-inhibition produces symmetric cross-attenuation.
   At mid and low dose, differential downstream NF-kappaB-dependent gene expression (LPS induces
   2-4x more negative regulator expression than IL-1beta: Nfkbia, Tnfaip3, Nfkbie; Fig. 6C/E)
   produces asymmetric attenuation. This is IKK-dependent (PS1145 experiment; Fig. 5A).

5. LPS-specific prolonged NF-kappaB activation duration (not total AUC) explains differential
   downstream gene expression vs IL-1beta (Fig. 6E, Supp. Fig. 7F/G).


BIOSCRIPT COMPILER NOTES
-------------------------
BioScript file : wang_nfkb_sequential_stimulation.bs
Compiler       : lilott8/BioScript (ANTLR4-based)
IR output      : 16 basic blocks (BB1-BB16), 5 if/else gates, 20 CFG edges
DAG            : wang_nfkb_sequential_stimulation_main_dag.dot
Duration units : All durations in minutes (m); BioScript supports s and m tokens only.
                 300 min  = 5 h equilibration        (IR: 18000.0 SECOND)
                 120 min  = 2 h stimulus interval    (IR: 7200.0 SECOND)
                 240 min  = 4 h IKK LPS stimulus     (IR: 14400.0 SECOND)
                  90 min  = PS1145 pretreatment       (IR: 5400.0 SECOND)
                  15 min  = each PS1145 wash          (IR: 900.0 SECOND)
Modules        : nfkbTranslocationDetection, cellViabilityDetection, rnaSeqDetection
ASCII note     : All Greek letters replaced with ASCII equivalents (alpha, beta, kappa, epsilon, u)
                 to satisfy ANTLR4 FileStream ASCII-only requirement.
Variable scope : Chamber variables (s1Active, s2Active, s3Active, s4Active) are never disposed
                 or drained inside if/else branches to ensure they remain in scope at join nodes.
                 Only ligand dispense variables (s1Ligand, s2Ligand, etc.) are disposed/drained
                 inside branches. Bare manifest names are never used as mix arguments.
Dose arm       : Mid-dose arm encoded as canonical protocol. Swap _MidDose manifests for
                 _HighDose or _LowDose to run the other two arms with identical CFG topology.
Biological scope: This .bs file encodes the canonical mid-dose sequential stimulation arm
                 (IL-1beta -> LPS -> PAM -> TNF-alpha) plus the IKK inhibition sub-protocol.
                 The 23 remaining stimulus sequences require separate .bs files or
                 parameterisation of manifest sources.
